# Does a Texas RRC Drilling Permit Prove Your Lease Covers a Well?

**No.** A Texas Railroad Commission (RRC) drilling permit records regulatory permission and related filing details for a proposed well. It does not establish that your private mineral lease is valid, covers the relevant tract or depth, includes the proposed wellbore, or entitles you to royalties. The RRC expressly says it has no jurisdiction over the validity of private mineral leases. Its public permit result uses “Lease” as a regulatory label for lease, pooled, or unitized acreage—not as a title opinion about your contract. [RRC Permian Basin FAQs](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/major-formation-faqs/permian-basin/) · [RRC Drilling Permit Query Results Help](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/DPQuery_results_howto.htm)

That distinction does not mean an operator can drill without private rights. The RRC FAQ says an operator must have a legal mineral lease for extraction, but that requirement is different from the agency determining whether **your specific** lease covers **this specific** well. A mineral owner should treat a permit as a record to compare with private documents, not as proof that a specific lease covers a specific well. [RRC Permian Basin FAQs](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/major-formation-faqs/permian-basin/)

## What a permit can tell you

The RRC's [drilling-permit result guide](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/DPQuery_results_howto.htm) identifies result fields such as API number, county of the surface hole, regulatory lease name, well number, permitted operator, status and status date, filing purpose, and planned wellbore profile. A result can link to the current W-1 application, current permit, attachments listed on the W-1 screen, and a plat. Record the exact identifier and date from the document you actually open; the query result and its attachments are not interchangeable.

The guide explains that the displayed “Lease” name describes acreage for RRC regulatory purposes and may change at completion. The displayed operator is the applicant/permit holder for Commission purposes. Neither label is a substitute for a recorded lease, a chain of title, or a professional interpretation of a private agreement. [RRC Drilling Permit Query Results Help](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/DPQuery_results_howto.htm)

If you need a reproducible search trail before comparing documents, use the separate [Texas RRC Drilling-Permit Query Retrieval Provenance Worksheet](/blog/texas-rrc-drilling-permit-query-retrieval-provenance-worksheet/). That worksheet records how a query was run; this article explains why a result cannot answer a private-lease question by itself.

## Compare the two record sets without merging them

Use a short exception list rather than deciding from a matching name. Keep these columns separate:

| RRC-side item | Private-rights item to locate | Question that remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W-1, permit number, API and status date | The owner's recorded deed/assignment and lease | Do these documents describe the same interest and time period? |
| Regulatory lease name, operator, and filed plat | Private lease parties, legal description, and any amendments | Are similar names just labels, or do the instruments connect the tract? |
| Planned wellbore profile and locations shown in the filing | Any relevant pooling/unit documents and tract/depth language | Which instruments, if any, address this tract and interval? |

Write `not matched`, `document missing`, or `professional review needed` for a gap. Do not write `covered` or `not covered` from a public search alone. The RRC's [well-records guide](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/obtaining-commission-records/oil-and-gas-well-records/) identifies W-1 applications and location plats as agency well records and separately says the agency lacks authority over lease, royalty, and property-rights matters. For the private-record side, start with the county of the property and the recorded instruments; the [Texas GLO mineral-rights FAQ](https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/Minerals%20FAQ_updated%202023.pdf) describes why deed and lease examination matters. A qualified professional may need additional records not apparent from these public indexes.

## A surface-location mismatch is not the answer

A horizontal well can be drilled from a surface site outside the lease designated on its permit. The RRC specifically describes this possibility in its [Permian Basin FAQ](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/major-formation-faqs/permian-basin/). Therefore, a surface pin outside your tract neither proves you are excluded nor proves your lease is included. The well path, filed plat, relevant private instruments, and any unit documents need their own review. Do not infer a royalty share, trespass, or a remedy from a map pin.

An application is also not the same event as an issued permit, a drilled well, a completed well, or reported production. The [RRC W-1 application FAQ](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/drilling-permit-application-faqs/) describes an application that can remain work in progress, while the [query-results help](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/DPQuery_results_howto.htm) explains how status and approval dates appear. Preserve the actual status shown at the time checked rather than assuming activity progressed.

## The useful next step

If the RRC record and your private documents appear to concern the same place, assemble the exact W-1/permit and plat reference, the recorded deed or assignment, your lease and amendments, and any identified pooling/unit papers. Note the item you could not match. A Texas land/title professional can assess that defined question; this public article cannot. The [title, lease and ownership learning center](/learning-center/title-lease-ownership/) explains how the different record classes fit together, while [What Is an Oil and Gas Lease and How Does It Affect Your Mineral Rights?](/blog/what-is-an-oil-and-gas-lease-and-how-does-it-affect-your-mineral-rights/) covers the private agreement itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the RRC “Lease” field identify my signed mineral lease?

Not necessarily. The RRC defines that result field as a regulatory name for lease, pooled, or unitized acreage. Compare it to the actual recorded instruments and private lease rather than treating a name match as a title conclusion. [RRC result guide](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/help/DPQuery_results_howto.htm)

### If the permit was approved, did the RRC verify my lease?

The RRC says it does not have jurisdiction over the validity of private mineral leases. Approval of a regulatory permit should not be represented as an owner-specific lease adjudication. [RRC Permian Basin FAQs](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/major-formation-faqs/permian-basin/)

### Does a surface location outside my tract exclude my minerals?

No conclusion follows from the surface location alone. The RRC notes that a horizontal well's surface location can be outside the lease named on the permit. The relevant well path and private documents require separate review. [RRC Permian Basin FAQs](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/major-formation-faqs/permian-basin/)
