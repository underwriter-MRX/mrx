# North Dakota Inherited Royalty Questions: Records and Ombudsman

An heir with a North Dakota royalty-payment question may need **different sources for different parts of the problem**. The county recorder is the starting place for recorded instruments affecting a private mineral interest; North Dakota's Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) provides well and location context through its GIS map and Well Search; and the Department of Agriculture's Royalty Oversight Program can help a royalty owner with operator contact or understanding payments when the issue fits its scope. None of those routes, by itself, proves that a particular heir owns a stated interest or is owed a stated amount. [ND DMR mineral-owner information](https://www.dmr.nd.gov/dmr/oilgas/mineralownerinfo) and [royalty-owner routing](https://www.dmr.nd.gov/dmr/oilgas/surfacemineralinfo) explain these roles.

This is educational source navigation, not a title opinion, probate instruction, payment calculation, legal advice, tax advice, or certified appraisal. A qualified North Dakota professional may be needed for an owner-specific estate, title, contract, or dispute question.

## Match the question to the source

| Question to investigate | Source to start with | What it cannot establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What recorded instruments might connect the prior owner and the claimed private interest? | The recorder for the county where the mineral interest is located, using the property description and recorded references. | That an index hit or statement of claim proves this heir's current ownership. |
| Which wells have a matching surface location, and which operator appears in public well data? | DMR's GIS map or Well Search, using the legal description or available well identifiers. | That the inherited tract is covered by a particular well, or that the listed operator owes this heir a payment. |
| How can a royalty owner contact the operator or ask about a payment discrepancy? | The Department of Agriculture's Royalty Oversight Program, if the question fits its stated scope. | Eligibility, a favorable ombudsman result, a title cure, or a payable amount. |
| Does a state mineral tract or lease appear in a state-managed record? | Department of Trust Lands Minerals Management. | A comprehensive private-mineral ownership search. |

The [DMR mineral-owner page](https://www.dmr.nd.gov/dmr/oilgas/mineralownerinfo), [DMR royalty-owner routing page](https://www.dmr.nd.gov/dmr/oilgas/surfacemineralinfo), [Agriculture program page](https://www.ndda.nd.gov/divisions/administrative-services/royalty-oversight-program), and [Trust Lands Minerals Management page](https://www.land.nd.gov/minerals-management) are the official starting points for these four routes. Check the current page before relying on a copied instruction or contact detail.

## If the question is about the inherited interest

Start with the property description and the relevant county recorder's documents. DMR says mineral-interest records and leases are handled through county recording, while its Oil and Gas Division does not review private leases or contracts. Its [mineral-owner page](https://www.dmr.nd.gov/dmr/oilgas/mineralownerinfo) links to county-recording information. A name match or a statement-of-claim form is not, on its own, a determination that a particular heir owns the interest. Preserve record references and ask a qualified professional what they establish in the specific estate chain.

If an old royalty statement names a property or operator, keep that statement as a clue to research. It does not replace the recorded chain, resolve a reservation, or establish authority to act for an estate.

## If the question is about a well or operator

DMR's [owner page](https://www.dmr.nd.gov/dmr/oilgas/mineralownerinfo) points to its GIS map and Well Search. Search with the legal description or available well identifiers and record the query and result reference. Ask which wells have a matching **surface location** and which operator DMR lists for a displayed well. A result can give context for the next inquiry, but it does not establish that the well covers the inherited tract or prove the heir's payee decimal, contractual deductions, private ownership, or entitlement to a check.

Keep the public well reference separate from the name of the payor actually shown on a royalty statement. If those names differ, record the discrepancy as a question; do not infer that either public screen identifies who owes a particular payment.

## If the question is about a royalty payment

DMR directs royalty owners seeking operator contact or help understanding royalty payments to the [North Dakota Department of Agriculture Royalty Oversight Program](https://www.dmr.nd.gov/dmr/oilgas/surfacemineralinfo). The [program page](https://www.ndda.nd.gov/divisions/administrative-services/royalty-oversight-program) describes an independent ombudsman who works with royalty owners and well operators on payment issues. It provides the current request route and contact details. Check that official page rather than relying on a copied phone number or third-party form.

The Agriculture Department says the program does not work on royalty issues already under regulatory review or state or federal litigation, and does not work on issues beyond the previous six years. It also notes limits in some working-interest situations. These are the agency's stated **program limits**, not a conclusion about any heir's legal rights or what a court might decide. The current [program page](https://www.ndda.nd.gov/divisions/administrative-services/royalty-oversight-program) controls the request process.

An heir can prepare a focused question without sending private estate documents to an unverified contact. Note the prior owner's name as it appears on the statement, the tract or legal description shown there, the payor/operator named on it, the payment periods in question, and dated correspondence already available. Keep sensitive identifiers and complete documents for a verified agency, operator, or professional channel only. If the payor has not recognized the heir or the ownership chain is disputed, the program's general contact/payment role should not be mistaken for a title-curative service. Ask the payor what documentation it requires and seek qualified title or estate advice as appropriate.

## Keep state-owned mineral records separate

The [Department of Trust Lands Minerals Management Division](https://www.land.nd.gov/minerals-management) manages state-owned mineral interests and offers a portal for state mineral tracts and leases. That portal is not a comprehensive private-ownership search for an inherited family interest. Do not infer that a private interest is absent merely because it does not appear there.

## Keep one bounded source log

Use a one-page log with the question asked, exact property or well identifier used, agency or record searched, date, record reference, what the source actually displayed, and the unresolved question. Label a name or well match as a **research lead**, not `owned`, `owed`, `eligible`, or `approved`. Keep real names, account identifiers, complete statements, estate documents, and correspondence out of a public worksheet.

The [North Dakota mineral-rights page](/mineral-rights/north-dakota/) provides broader state context; [royalty checks after inheritance](/blog/understanding-royalty-checks-after-inheriting-mineral-rights/) explains the separate estate and payor-account workstreams. Neither replaces North Dakota-specific professional review.

## Five common questions

### Does a county record or statement of claim prove I inherited the mineral interest?

No. A recorded reference can be an important piece of the paper trail, but an index hit or form alone does not resolve the particular estate chain, reservations, or this heir's authority. Preserve the exact reference and ask a qualified North Dakota professional what it establishes for the identified property.

### Is the operator in DMR Well Search necessarily the company that owes my royalty?

No. DMR's public result identifies a listed operator for a well with a matching surface location. It does not by itself establish unit or tract coverage, the royalty payor for a specific owner, the payee decimal, or a payment obligation. Compare the public reference with the operator or payor named on the actual statement, then keep differences unresolved for an appropriate official or professional question.

### Can the Royalty Oversight Program decide my title or calculate what I am owed?

The [agency describes](https://www.ndda.nd.gov/divisions/administrative-services/royalty-oversight-program) assistance with royalty-payment issues through an independent ombudsman. Do not treat that description as a title opinion, a guaranteed case acceptance, or a calculation of what an heir is owed. Use the agency's current request route for an in-scope operator-contact or payment-understanding question; seek qualified title, estate, or contract help when that is the actual dispute.

### Does an empty Trust Lands search mean my family's private mineral interest does not exist?

No. [Trust Lands describes](https://www.land.nd.gov/minerals-management) its search as state mineral tracts and leases. It is not a comprehensive inventory of privately owned minerals. Keep private county-record research separate from that state-managed portal.

### What should I prepare before contacting an agency or operator?

Write down the specific question, property description or well reference, prior owner's name as shown on the available statement, payor or operator name, payment periods, and dated correspondence. Keep a source log and use the verified official route. Share sensitive identifiers or full estate documents only through a verified agency, operator, or qualified professional channel when needed.
