# Ohio Mineral Records and ODNR Well Search: What Each Can Show

If you find an Ohio oil-and-gas well near land tied to your family, keep **two record questions separate**. The county recorder's recorded instruments can help you gather deeds, leases, and other documents for the identified property. Ohio Department of Natural Resources (ODNR) well information can help identify a public well record and the operator shown there. A well result does not establish that you own minerals in its tract or that its operator owes you royalties; a name match in a recorder index is not a complete title determination. [Ohio Revised Code §317.08](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-317.08) identifies recordable deeds and leases, while [ODNR's Well Information search](https://apps.ohiodnr.gov/oilgas/rbdmsreports/ER_WellInformation.aspx) describes its well-data scope.

This is an educational route to public sources, not a mineral-title opinion, lease interpretation, royalty calculation, or advice to file an instrument. An Ohio-qualified title professional or attorney can evaluate the documents and facts for a particular interest.

## Start with the county and the recorded instrument

Use the property's county and any legal description or recording reference already on a deed, lease, estate document, or correspondence. Ohio law directs county recorders to keep official records that include deeds and leases or lease memoranda presented for recording. [Section 317.08](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-317.08) lists those instrument types. [Section 317.13(F)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-317.13) requires county-recorder websites to make electronic indexes and versions of instruments recorded on or after January 1, 1980 available, subject to the section's exceptions. It does not tell you that a particular person's entire ownership history is online or that every county uses the same search controls.

For a concrete example, [Belmont County Recorder's search guide](https://belmontcountyrecorder.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/FraudSleuth-Handout-1125.pdf) says a visitor can use its public-records search as a guest, try the recorded person's name with or without a middle name, or use a volume/page, instrument number, or recording date. That is **Belmont County's** interface, not a statewide instruction. If the property is elsewhere, use that county recorder's official site or ask the office how to retrieve the relevant instrument. Keep the instrument number, parties, date, legal description, and a copy of the actual document if available. Do not rely on a search-result label alone to decide what rights were conveyed or reserved.

## Use ODNR for a different question

ODNR's [Well Information search](https://apps.ohiodnr.gov/oilgas/rbdmsreports/ER_WellInformation.aspx) offers county, township, and operator-name inputs. The page explicitly says **only active wells are included in this search**. ODNR's [oil-and-gas database](https://apps.ohiodnr.gov/oilgas/RBDMSReports/default.aspx) also lists other reports; the active-well lookup is not the entire state record system. Save the query, well identifier if displayed, operator shown, and date viewed.

A nearby well, matching county, or listed operator is context for a follow-up question. It does not prove the well covers your exact mineral tract, that you are in its unit, the lease that governs your interest, the correct payor or ownership decimal, or a payment due. Equally, no result in an active-well-only search does not prove there was never a well or that your minerals are unleased. Avoid substituting water-well records for oil-and-gas records.

## Keep a short source log before discussing an offer

Make two columns: **recorded-interest documents** and **public well information**. For each entry, note the source, search terms, date, identifier, document or result you actually viewed, and the question still open. Compare the property description in the recorded instruments with the location information available from ODNR without declaring that a nearby well and your interest are legally connected. If the chain, reservation, lease, estate transfer, or tract relationship is unclear, preserve the uncertainty for Ohio-qualified review rather than treating either public search as a shortcut to an ownership answer.
