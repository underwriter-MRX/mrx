**No.** A Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) production result can help you identify a reported well and reporting period, but it cannot verify what **you** were owed or paid. DEP says its [public well-production report](https://greenport.pa.gov/ReportExtracts/OG/OilGasWellProdReport) contains data supplied by well operators. DEP also says it [does not audit private royalty payments or regulate lease agreements](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/laws-regulations-and-guidelines). Your royalty statement, lease, division order, payor accounting and any allocation records answer different questions. A difference between the two sources is a research lead, not proof of an error, title, tract coverage, or value.

This is educational record-source guidance, not a payment audit, Pennsylvania legal or tax advice, a title opinion, an engineering conclusion, or a valuation. Do not use it to decide that a payor breached a lease. MRX may have a buyer interest in a later transaction and discloses that role before an agreement; it is not an independent royalty auditor.

## Start with the reporting period and well

The [DEP production extract](https://greenport.pa.gov/ReportExtracts/OG/OilGasWellProdReport) lets a visitor select reporting periods and export a CSV. Its description says the numbers are operator-reported to DEP. The [DEP mapping application](https://gis.dep.pa.gov/PaOilAndGasMapping/) can provide a possible well lead using a permit number, operator, county or municipality and can link to production reporting. Preserve the exact well or permit identifier, operator, selected period, export date and the unedited CSV or source reference. A similar name or a nearby map point is not proof that a well is attributable to your tract or lease. DEP warns that map coverage depends on locational information and disclaims errors and omissions.

Before comparing to a private statement, identify whether the DEP result concerns an unconventional or conventional well. DEP's [2023 oil-and-gas annual report, Reporting Requirements](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/annual-report/dep-2023-oil-and-gas-annual-report) describes monthly production reporting for unconventional wells and annual reporting for conventional wells. A conventional annual public result is therefore not a month-by-month check-stub ledger. Even for an unconventional monthly result, compare the regulatory reporting period with the statement's payment or sales period before drawing an inference; the two labels serve different purposes. Do not divide an annual total by twelve or attribute a public total to your owner decimal without supporting records.

## Compare identifiers before numbers

Place the DEP export next to your royalty statement without uploading the private statement to a public search. Note the statement's payor, property or well identifier, production or sales period, product, displayed volume, owner decimal, adjustments and deductions. If the identifiers or periods do not align, record the mismatch instead of forcing a calculation. If the public result appears absent or different, also preserve the search inputs and date: DEP's [report data dictionary](https://files.dep.state.pa.us/OilGas/BOGM/BOGMPortalFiles/OilGasReports/HelpDocs/SSRS_Report_Data_Dictionary/DEP_Oil_and_GAS_Reports_Data_Dictionary.pdf) describes the production report as operator-submitted and cautions that DEP does not guarantee report accuracy, completeness or timeliness. The dictionary is version 1.8, dated April 2022, so confirm any field label in the current export rather than assuming it is unchanged.

A well-production figure alone cannot establish your lease terms, owner decimal, realized price, line-item deductions, adjustments, net payment, or whether your payment is right or wrong. The existing [royalty-statement guide](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/) explains how to read those private line items; this Pennsylvania guide is about using the state report without turning it into a payor ledger. Confirm the current DEP export's actual field labels before describing any field as present or absent.

## Ask a bounded question, not for a verdict from DEP

If a difference remains after matching the well, product and period, ask the payor which well/property, production or sales period, owner share and adjustment records bridge its statement to the reported well data. Keep the original statement and response together with the dated DEP export. Avoid assuming that a missing report means no production, or that a reported well proves you own an interest in it.

DEP expressly says private lease terms and royalty-payment audits are [outside its role](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/laws-regulations-and-guidelines). For a disputed lease, decimal or payment right, seek a qualified Pennsylvania professional with the actual instruments and payor records. Do not send unredacted account details through an untrusted channel.

## Common questions

### Does a DEP production row prove that I own a royalty interest in the well?

No. DEP's [production report](https://greenport.pa.gov/ReportExtracts/OG/OilGasWellProdReport) is an operator-reported well-data source. It is not an owner title, lease-coverage or payee ledger. The relevant instruments and allocation records need separate review.

### Why might a conventional-well report not match one month's check?

DEP's [reporting-requirements summary](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/annual-report/dep-2023-oil-and-gas-annual-report) describes conventional production reports as annual, unlike monthly unconventional reporting. A private payment line may represent another period or accounting basis. The cadence alone does not diagnose your check.

### Can I calculate my royalty from DEP's public volume?

Not reliably from that number alone. The public report omits owner-specific interest, contract and payor-accounting inputs. Do not apply a guessed decimal or price and present the result as money owed.

### Should I ask DEP to audit my royalty payment?

DEP states that it [does not audit royalty payments or regulate private leases](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/laws-regulations-and-guidelines). Ask the payor for its calculation and records; bring a genuine private dispute to an appropriate qualified Pennsylvania professional.

### What should I save before asking the payor about a difference?

Save the dated DEP export and its reporting period, well/permit and operator identifiers, plus the relevant private statement and its own period and property identifiers. Keep private account details out of public tools. Ask one precise question about the unmatched identifier, period or line item.
