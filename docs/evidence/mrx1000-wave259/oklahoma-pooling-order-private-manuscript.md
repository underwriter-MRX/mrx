## Direct answer

Start with the OCC case or Conservation Docket number on your notice, if it appears. For case documents and orders from March 21, 2022 onward, use the [Oklahoma Corporation Commission's Electronic Case Filing document search](https://oklahoma.gov/occ/court-dockets/electronic-case-filing.html). For older case information, use [OCC Case Processing](https://oklahoma.gov/occ/court-dockets/case-processing.html) and its link to archived imaging. Preserve the actual order and source record, not just an application or mailed notice. If your notice or order asks you to make an election or respond by a date, take it promptly to a qualified Oklahoma oil-and-gas attorney; this guide does not calculate your deadline or tell you which option to choose.

## What to keep from the notice

Retain the complete notice, mailing envelope if available, any listed case number, named operator/applicant, date, legal land and formation description, and your own records showing why you think it relates to your interest. An identical surname, nearby tract or similar well name is only a search lead. Do not post an unredacted notice or account details in a public tool. [OCC's pooling overview](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/admin-court-services/court-services/pooling.html) says the application names unleased respondents on Exhibit A and that notice is mailed and published before the hearing. It also describes a later order prepared after the hearing and mailed to respondents. Those are different stages, so confirm which document you have.

## Choose the official record route by case period

1. **For the newer document route:** OCC's [Electronic Case Filing page](https://oklahoma.gov/occ/court-dockets/electronic-case-filing.html) links a public Case Document Search for documents and orders from March 21, 2022. Use the current search controls with the identifiers shown on your notice. Keep the displayed case reference, document label, file date, source URL and date you accessed it. Do not assume this guide knows a live search field that has not been checked in the current interface.
2. **For older case information:** OCC's [Case Processing instructions](https://oklahoma.gov/occ/court-dockets/case-processing.html) say that route contains case information before March 21, 2022. The instructions show a case-type selector, identifier search and a `Link to Imaging` after finding a case. Its legend calls `CD` a Conservation Docket and relief code `41` Pooling. Use those only as official search labels; a matching docket label is not an ownership finding.
3. **If the search is ambiguous or the record appears absent:** preserve the exact route, inputs and date, and check the other OCC-directed archival route or ask [OCC Consumer Services](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/oil-gas/consumer-services-department.html) how to locate the regulatory record. OCC describes Consumer Services as an information contact for mineral and surface owners, including pooling and databases. It is not a substitute for legal review of a private title, election or payment dispute.

Save the actual order if one is present. Check its case number, named parties, lands/formations and signature or issuance information against your notice; record any mismatch as an open question. Do not treat a search result card, application, hearing notice or respondent list as the final order. OCC's [pooling overview](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/admin-court-services/court-services/pooling.html) describes the hearing and later Commission order as separate steps.

## What the order can and cannot answer

The OCC order is a regulatory-case document. Its text may be essential to understand what was ordered for a specified unit or formation, but it does not by itself prove your present chain of title, your exact mineral fraction, that a private lease covers your interest, or the correct payor decimal. Those questions require the relevant deeds, probate or trust instruments, lease and amendments, payor records, and qualified Oklahoma review. The OCC overview itself describes operators searching county and other sources for persons with the right to drill and pooling orders commonly addressing unknown-address or title-problem owners; a named respondent therefore should not be presented as a title opinion.

Do not turn the OCC overview's general description of order elections into a personal deadline or a recommendation to participate, lease or take a cash/royalty alternative. The actual order, service and your facts matter. If a response window may be open, get qualified Oklahoma advice promptly and bring the full notice, located order and underlying ownership documents. If you are only researching, keep the dated source trail and ask one specific follow-up question about a missing case number, record, legal description or payor cross-reference.

This is educational record-location guidance, not a legal, title, tax, payment-audit, engineering or valuation opinion. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when applicable, that relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed. A research-only reader can follow the official records without contacting MRX.

## Common questions

### Is the pooling notice the same as the final order?

No. OCC describes notice and a hearing before a pooling order is prepared, reviewed and issued. Locate the actual case document; do not infer an issued order from an application or notice alone. [OCC Pooling Order Information](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/admin-court-services/court-services/pooling.html).

### Which OCC system should I search first?

OCC points to Electronic Case Filing's document search for documents/orders from March 21, 2022 and to legacy Case Processing for earlier case information. Follow the date and identifiers on the notice, then preserve what each route actually shows. [OCC ECF](https://oklahoma.gov/occ/court-dockets/electronic-case-filing.html); [OCC Case Processing](https://oklahoma.gov/occ/court-dockets/case-processing.html).

### What do `CD` and relief code `41` mean in legacy Case Processing?

OCC's published legend defines `CD` as Conservation Docket and `41` as Pooling. These are search labels, not proof that a result belongs to your mineral interest. [OCC Case Processing](https://oklahoma.gov/occ/court-dockets/case-processing.html).

### Does my name on a respondent list prove I own minerals?

No. The list is part of a regulatory application/notice process. Confirm title and any payment share from the applicable instruments and payor records with qualified help rather than treating a name match as a title conclusion. [OCC Pooling Order Information](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/admin-court-services/court-services/pooling.html).

### Can this article tell me how long I have to elect?

No. OCC's overview describes a general order-election period, but the actual order, service and case facts must be reviewed. If you may need to respond, seek qualified Oklahoma advice promptly. [OCC Pooling Order Information](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/admin-court-services/court-services/pooling.html).
