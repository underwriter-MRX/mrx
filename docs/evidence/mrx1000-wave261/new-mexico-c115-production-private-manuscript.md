**Answer first.** If your New Mexico royalty statement identifies a well by API number, the New Mexico Oil Conservation Division's (OCD's) Well Search can lead to the C-115 production reported for that well. OCD's [C-115 instructions](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/ocd/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/C115-INSTRUCTIONS.pdf) describe an API-number search, a Production section with yearly totals expandable to months, and an Excel export. Save the exact API, production month, result and access date before comparing it with a private statement. The public figure is an operator report, **not** an owner-level royalty payment or proof that the well is covered by your lease.

This is a public-record retrieval guide, not a royalty audit, a title or lease opinion, a New Mexico legal or tax conclusion, an engineering conclusion, or a valuation. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed. A reader may use the public source without contacting MRX.

## First secure the exact well identifier and month

Write down the statement's well or property name, API number if shown, product, and production or sales period if shown. A payor name, check date, or nearby well name is not a safe substitute for an API match. If the statement lacks an API number, ask the payor for the well/property cross-reference and period used for that line item. Do not select a similarly named well from a map and treat it as the paid well.

OCD describes the [C-115](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/ocd/ocd-e-permitting/) as an operator's monthly report. Its [instructions, “Verification of data submitted — Well Search Tool”](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/ocd/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/C115-INSTRUCTIONS.pdf) say that production and injection totals submitted on the C-115 by API number are available through Well Search. The instructions direct a user through OCD Permitting, Searches, Wells, an API-number lookup, and the well's Production quick link. The year row expands to monthly totals and can be exported to Excel. Confirm these labels in the current read-only interface before relying on them; the manual was written for operators, and a public owner should not enter an operator submission flow.

Keep a retrieval record with the official page URL, API number entered, well name shown, selected year and month, product and units as displayed, download filename if any, and access date. Preserve the untouched export. If the reported well identifier or production period does not match the private statement, stop the numeric comparison and ask for the cross-reference rather than guessing.

## Account for OCD's reported-data timing

OCD's [statistics page](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/ocd/ocd-data/statistics/) says its C-115 reported production data is updated nightly and has a **45-day lag**. Its [C-115 manual](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/ocd/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/C115-INSTRUCTIONS.pdf) separately says an accepted initial or amended report may appear the next business day after approval. A missing recent month is therefore an open reporting-timing question, not evidence that the well produced nothing or that your check was wrong. Record the date and repeat a bounded lookup after the relevant public period is available if needed. Do not use these notes to calculate an individual filing deadline or promise when a particular well's row will post.

The statistics page also offers operator, county and statewide production summaries. Those aggregate routes answer broader questions. For a statement line tied to a specific well, use the exact API-number record first; do not substitute a county total for one owner's paid volume.

## Compare the source roles, not just the numbers

| Item | OCD well-production result | Private royalty statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary identity | API-number well record and reporting month | Payor's property/well label and owner account line |
| Reported quantity | Operator-submitted production/injection data shown by OCD | Payor-reported volume, sales or allocation basis if shown |
| Owner-specific terms | Not established by the C-115 result | May include owner decimal, price, deductions, adjustments and net payment |
| Good next question | Is this the correct API, product and month, and is a later correction pending? | Which well, period, allocation and accounting records support this line? |

Use the table as a mismatch register, not a formula. Even matching public and private volumes would not establish your title, tract inclusion, lease royalty, division-order decimal, realized price, deductions or amount due. The [royalty-statement decoder](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/) explains the private line items; this guide explains where an OCD well report may provide a separately dated public lead.

If a discrepancy remains after matching the API, product and production month, send a narrow question to the payor: “Which well/API, property and production or sales period does this line represent, and what records bridge that line to my owner allocation?” Keep private account and tax identifiers out of public search fields and unsecured messages. A qualified New Mexico professional should review a genuine dispute over ownership, lease coverage, allocation or payment using the actual instruments and payor records. OCD's public well total alone does not decide it.

## Common questions to verify before public use

**Can I search if the statement has no API number?** Ask the payor for the exact well/property cross-reference. The official OCD source also has other search routes, but a similar name or nearby location is only a lead; do not use it as an owner-to-well match without supporting records.

**Why is my newest production month absent?** OCD's [statistics page](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/ocd/ocd-data/statistics/) reports a 45-day C-115 lag, and its [manual](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/ocd/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/C115-INSTRUCTIONS.pdf) describes overnight posting after accepted reports or amendments. Preserve the search date and ask about the actual reporting status rather than assuming zero production.

**Does an OCD well row prove I own royalty rights in that well?** No. The C-115 retrieval is a regulator-hosted operator report by API number. The owner-specific title, lease and payment questions require the actual private records and qualified review.

**Can I multiply a public monthly volume by my decimal to find what I am owed?** No. The public result does not establish the owner allocation, contract terms, payor sales/price basis, adjustments or deductions. Use it to frame a documented question, not to announce a payment shortage.

**Where should I go next?** The [oil-and-gas royalties pillar](/learning-center/oil-and-gas-royalties/) maps the public and private record types. If a mismatch remains, keep the dated OCD result and private statement together for the payor or qualified New Mexico professional. No contact with MRX is required to follow the public research path.
