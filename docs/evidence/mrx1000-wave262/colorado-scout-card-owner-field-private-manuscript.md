## Proposed article copy

**No.** A Colorado Energy and Carbon Management Commission (ECMC) COGIS Well Scout Card can display `Surface Mineral Owner Same: Yes` or `No`, but that flag does not name you, trace a deed chain, or establish your particular mineral title, lease coverage or royalty entitlement. Read the flag as a question about the surface/mineral-owner relationship recorded in the *wellbore-permit portion* of that agency card. Keep the specific API and card date, then examine the relevant private and recorded documents separately before making a conclusion about your own interest. [ECMC COGIS Well Scout Card example](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12319839)

This is an informational record-reading method, not an assessment of any person's ownership, land, well, lease, payment or legal rights. A Colorado land or title professional may need to evaluate disputed connections. Do not upload an unredacted deed, lease or payment statement to an untrusted site. ## Why matching `FEE` labels are not a person match

On one current public [ECMC card for API 05-123-19839](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12319839), the wellbore-permit area displays **`Surface Mineral Owner Same: No`** immediately above **`Mineral Owner: FEE Surface Owner: FEE`**. A second current [ECMC card for API 05-123-31228](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12331228) displays **`Same: Yes`** with the same pair of `FEE` labels. Taken together, the public examples show why two identical `FEE` entries cannot be treated as the answer to the separate Yes/No question, much less as a match to a named person's deed. These examples illustrate the display; they are not evidence about the reader's property.

An older [Colorado Form 2 instruction document](https://ecmc.state.co.us/forms/instructions/Form2_inst.pdf), under **Ownership**, describes the mineral and surface entries as *types of ownership* and separately asks whether the surface owner is also the mineral owner. That historical instruction is useful context for the two different questions the card appears to present. It is **not** proof that every current COGIS field has the same data source, that the agency independently verified the identity, or that a current Form 2 uses unchanged language. Verify present ECMC field guidance before treating that historical description as a current-form rule.

Do not infer from either `Yes` or `No` that you own or do not own the minerals beneath the well, that a named tract is inside the producing unit, or that a royalty is owed. The flag on these cards provides no named party, deed-reception number or private fractional interest for you to compare. That is an inference about what the displayed card **cannot establish**, not a claim that a particular filed answer is accurate or inaccurate.

## Preserve the card as one record, not as the title file

For the well you are actually researching, record the displayed API number, well name, county and location description, operator, wellbore-permit section, `Same` value, ownership-category labels, and the date you checked. Keep a URL or PDF/image of the exact card, not a search-result snippet. Do not combine a separate wellbore, formation or later status into the permit fields without checking their labels and dates. [ECMC COGIS example card](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12319839)

Then make a second, separately labeled list of your own claimed interest and its sources: the recorded deed, reservation or assignment you rely on; a relevant lease or amendment if you have one; and any probate or transfer documents that need to be traced. Use the county of the land to locate its clerk and recorder's current search and copy process. For **Weld County only**, its [recording FAQs](https://www.weld.gov/Government/Departments/Clerk-and-Recorder/Recording-Department/Recording-FAQs) say its document index can be viewed without charge while images/copies may require payment. Its [recording guidance](https://www.weld.gov/Government/Departments/Clerk-and-Recorder/Recording-Department/Recording-a-Document) cautions that recording a document itself does not confer legality or authenticity. Other Colorado counties may have different portals, coverage and fees; a negative online search is not a title conclusion.

Use an exception list instead of forcing a match: `same API but tract not established`, `recorded instrument found but ownership chain unresolved`, `permit flag conflicts with a private document`, or `professional review needed`. A county index, public regulator card and your private papers answer different questions. A qualified Colorado land or title professional can identify which instrument and tract questions remain open.

## Candidate FAQs (not yet schema-approved)

1. **If the card says `Yes`, am I the mineral owner?** No person-specific conclusion follows. The field neither names you nor traces your interest.
2. **If both lines say `FEE`, why can `Same` say `No`?** Current ECMC examples display exactly that combination. The category labels and separate Yes/No flag cannot be collapsed into one identity test.
3. **Does `No` mean my minerals are not in the well?** No. That inference is not supported by the public card; the relevant tract, instruments and wellbore connection require separate review.
4. **Can I rely on the Weld County online index for every Colorado county?** No. The cited access and fee details are Weld-specific; check the county where the land lies.
5. **What should I save before asking for professional review?** Save the exact COGIS card/API/date and separately list the instruments supporting your claimed interest, with unresolved matches clearly marked.
