## Proposed article copy

**`RI` is used for “royalty interest” in a Texas appraisal district's mineral-account code legend.** A 2024 data-exchange appendix on page 19 of [Tarrant Appraisal District's 2025–2029 mineral-services RFQ](https://www.tad.org/content/rfp/mineral-and-utility-rfq-2025-2029.pdf#page=19) names `Owner Interest Type` and `Owner Decimal Interest of Ownership` as separate fields and expressly lists `RI` as `Royalty Interest`. A current [Ward County Appraisal District mineral detail](https://wardcad.org/Home/Details?ownershipId=24888&parcelId=500066) also displays `Interest Type: RI` beside `Decimal Interest` in its lease-information section. The Tarrant legend is **not** a published Ward code dictionary. If the precise meaning of a Ward entry matters, confirm it with Ward CAD and the controlling records rather than treating a cross-district convention as a title opinion.

The code answers a *type-label* question. The number beside it is a *displayed decimal* in an appraisal system. Neither field by itself proves that you own the interest, that the displayed number is the payor's current payment decimal, that a lease covers your tract, or that any particular royalty is due. The Ward detail itself labels its information for research and warns that descriptions and acreage should be verified for legal use. This is a record-reading guide, not legal, tax, title, valuation, or payment advice. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction and discloses a buyer relationship before an agreement is signed.

## Read the three labels separately

| Visible label or code | What the cited source shows | What it does not settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `G1` | The Ward example shows `Category Code: G1`; the Tarrant exchange table separately uses `G1` as a state-use code for mineral leases. | It is not the `RI` interest-type code or a named person's proof of title. Do not assume identical data definitions across CAD systems. |
| `Interest Type: RI` | The Ward page displays the code; the separate Tarrant code legend expands `RI` as royalty interest in Tarrant's exchange format. | It does not reveal the instrument creating a particular interest, who owns it now, whether it is participating or nonparticipating, or how it was calculated. |
| `Decimal Interest` | The Ward page displays a numeric field separate from `Interest Type`. Tarrant's exchange format also has a distinct owner-decimal field, with its own contract description. | The two districts' decimal fields must not be assumed identical. A CAD number is not automatically the decimal a payor uses today or the fraction a buyer would acquire. |

This is why `RI`, `G1`, and the decimal should not be merged into one conclusion. The code may help you ask the right question; it cannot resolve a disagreement between a deed, lease, division order, tax account and royalty statement. The [Texas Railroad Commission](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/obtaining-commission-records/oil-and-gas-well-records/) states that private property-rights and lease/royalty matters are outside its authority. An RRC identifier appearing in a CAD row is a retrieval clue, not an agency title finding.

## Preserve the source before comparing records

If you are researching your own account, save the appraisal district, exact property-detail URL, year shown, the field labels, and the minimum parcel or lease identifier needed to find the same row later. Note whether the row is current or from an archived roll. Do not copy a stranger's name, address, or account data into a public question to demonstrate what `RI` means.

Keep your own claimed-interest documents in a separate list: the recorded deed or reservation, any relevant estate or assignment papers, lease and amendments, unit documents, the payor's division order, and recent statements. Compare property descriptions, parties, interest type, effective dates and numbers only within the scope supported by each source. The [MRX division-order guide](/blog/what-is-a-division-order-and-why-does-it-matter-for-mineral-rights-owners/) explains the payor-document side of that comparison; it does not turn a CAD field into the payor's current ledger.

If a material mismatch remains, ask the appraisal district what its displayed code and year represent, ask the payor for the basis of its own decimal where appropriate, and use a qualified Texas land or title professional for the recorded chain and legal effect. A research-only owner can follow those source routes without contacting MRX. If the question is part of a possible sale, bring a concise exception list to an MRX human discussion; MRX can organize records but does not certify title or a payable share.

## Candidate FAQs (not schema-approved)

1. **Does `RI` mean royalty interest?** Tarrant Appraisal District explicitly uses that expansion in its mineral exchange contract; the Ward example displays `RI` but does not define its code on the detail page. Confirm the issuing district's meaning when a decision depends on it.
2. **Is `RI` the same as the decimal beside it?** No. The examples display type and numeric interest in distinct fields; neither is a complete private-title or payment reconciliation.
3. **Does `G1` tell me I own a royalty?** No. It is a separate appraisal classification label, not an individual interest-type or title determination.
4. **Can I use the CAD decimal to check my royalty statement?** It is a comparison clue, not a substitute for the payor's current division-order calculation and the underlying property documents.
5. **What if the label or decimal conflicts with my records?** Preserve the exact account/year and conflicting source documents, ask each issuer about its own field, and obtain qualified Texas title or accounting review when the conclusion matters.
