**No state-trust or federal oil-and-gas lease rate published for Wyoming, by itself, sets the royalty fraction in your privately executed mineral lease.** First identify the lessor and the applicable lease and amendments. Wyoming’s [Office of State Lands and Investments (OSLI) leasing page](https://lands.wyo.gov/trust-land-management/mineral-leasing) addresses state-land mineral leases; [BLM Wyoming’s lease-sales page](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/oil-and-gas/leasing/regional-lease-sales/wyoming) addresses federal leasing. A dated [BLM comparative report](https://www.blm.gov/sites/blm.gov/files/docs/2022-01/2018%20Comparative%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Federal%20Oil%20and%20Gas%20Fiscal%20Systems%20Onshore%20Report_0.pdf) distinguishes private-land royalties as negotiated lease terms. Its old numerical examples are **not** a current private-rate benchmark.

This is educational source-selection guidance, not an interpretation of your lease, title, royalty check, tax position or value. It assumes a privately executed lease. Unleased or pooled interests, mixed public/private minerals, and unusual acquisitions can require another analysis. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction and would disclose a buyer relationship before an agreement. You can use these public research steps without contacting MRX.

## Match the rate to the actual lessor

- **An [OSLI state-trust leasing page or customer portal](https://lands.wyo.gov/customer-portal)** concerns Wyoming state-trust leases and permits. The portal expressly limits its services to that program. It does not establish a rate or lessor in a separate private agreement.
- **A [BLM Wyoming lease-sale notice](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/oil-and-gas/leasing/regional-lease-sales/wyoming)** concerns BLM-administered federal lease sales. It does not establish the royalty clause in a privately signed lease.
- **Your identified private lease and controlling amendments** are the contract documents to bring to a qualified owner-specific review. They still do not establish a final entitlement without checking parties, tract, depths, assignments, amendments and applicable facts.

These source categories are **not** a classification of your parcel. A Wyoming location, nearby well, similar lease number or public map point does not prove that the same lessor and instrument govern your minerals. The [BLM Wyoming Federal Mineral Estate layer](https://gis.blm.gov/wyarcgis/rest/services/Lands/BLM_WY_FederalMineralEstate/FeatureServer) expressly says it does not depict private mineral ownership patterns. It may be a lead for federal-interest research; it is not a private-title map.

## Keep the lease fraction separate from the payment decimal

Wyoming’s [W.S. 30-5-305(a)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) says a division order may not alter or amend an oil-and-gas lease or other contractual agreement, and the agreement takes precedence to the extent of an attempted alteration. The same section’s [payment-information list](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) calls for the owner’s interest in sales from the identified property or well to be expressed as a decimal. That payment decimal is a different field from the stated lease royalty fraction.

The statute does not decide which lease governs **your** land or calculate **your** decimal. Ownership, participation and other terms may affect a payor’s number. The [lease-rate/value guide](/blog/how-lease-royalty-rates-influence-mineral-value/) explains that arithmetic and its limits; this Wyoming guide addresses the earlier question of **which rate source belongs to which lessor**.

## Make a short source-and-document list

1. **Label every percentage by its source.** Record whether it came from your signed lease, an amendment, a division order, a check statement, an OSLI page or a BLM notice. Save the document date, lease or tract identifier and exact quoted field. Do not copy a public percentage into your private file as the answer.
2. **Identify the lessor and lease you actually have.** Note the parties, legal description, effective date, and any identified amendments or assignments. A shared county or well name is not enough to connect two instruments.
3. **Separate an agreement question from a payment question.** A lease fraction and a payment decimal are not interchangeable. If the statement’s decimal is the concern, keep the well, product and period with it and ask the payor for its own calculation records.
4. **Escalate a material conflict.** If the lease, amendment, division order and check cannot be reconciled, or you may be unleased, pooled or affected by mixed public/private minerals, ask a qualified Wyoming oil-and-gas attorney or land professional to identify the governing documents and legal effect. A payor or qualified royalty auditor may also help with an accounting question. Do not guess a replacement rate.

### Can I use a Wyoming state-trust rate for my private lease?

Not merely because the property is in Wyoming. [OSLI’s customer portal](https://lands.wyo.gov/customer-portal) limits its services to state-trust leasing and permitting. Identify the lessor and private agreement before comparing rates.

### Does a BLM lease-sale rate apply to a private mineral interest?

A [BLM Wyoming sale](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/oil-and-gas/leasing/regional-lease-sales/wyoming) concerns federal leasing. Its published terms do not, by themselves, establish the rate in a separate private lease.

### Does a division-order decimal replace the royalty fraction in my lease?

No. [W.S. 30-5-305(a)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) says a division order may not alter the lease or other contractual agreement. A decimal can reflect more than the lease fraction; the statute does not determine an individual’s correct number.

### Can a federal-mineral map prove I have a private lease?

No. The [BLM layer metadata](https://gis.blm.gov/wyarcgis/rest/services/Lands/BLM_WY_FederalMineralEstate/FeatureServer) says the map does not depict private mineral ownership patterns. Use it only as a public-interest research lead.

### What if my lease and check disagree?

Keep the relevant instruments and statement with their parties, tract, effective dates and payment period. Ask qualified Wyoming land or legal help for the governing-document question; ask the payor or a qualified auditor about accounting. A difference is a question to resolve, not proof that a public rate applies or money is owed.
