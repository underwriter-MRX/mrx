Working title: **Does Wyoming Set the Royalty Rate in My Private Oil and Gas Lease?**

Primary pillar: oil-and-gas royalties. Proposed reader: a Wyoming mineral owner comparing a royalty fraction in a privately signed lease with a state or federal rate found online. Proposed next action: identify the actual lessor and governing lease; keep a research-only route available. No program row, image, release, or published count is assigned by this draft.

## Proposed answer

**A Wyoming state-trust or federal oil-and-gas lease rate does not, by itself, set the royalty fraction in a privately negotiated mineral lease.** First identify whose minerals were leased and read the governing lease and any amendments. The Wyoming Office of State Lands and Investments describes its oil-and-gas leasing program as one for **state lands**. BLM Wyoming's lease-sales page describes a **federal** program. An older BLM comparative report distinguishes those regimes from private lands and says private-land royalties are negotiated and determined by the lease terms. That report is a dated explanation of the distinction, not a present-day percentage recommendation. [Wyoming OSLI mineral leasing](https://lands.wyo.gov/trust-land-management/mineral-leasing); [BLM Wyoming lease sales](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/oil-and-gas/leasing/regional-lease-sales/wyoming); [BLM comparative report, Wyoming section, PDF p. 215](https://www.blm.gov/sites/blm.gov/files/docs/2022-01/2018%20Comparative%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Federal%20Oil%20and%20Gas%20Fiscal%20Systems%20Onshore%20Report_0.pdf).

This is a **source-selection answer**, not a determination of your title, a lease's legal effect, a correct payment decimal, or a fair rate. If the interest is unleased, pooled, federally held, state held, or subject to an unusual acquisition or assignment, that is a different fact pattern and should not be forced into this privately executed lease example.

## Which rate source are you reading?

| Source encountered | What the agency page addresses | What it cannot establish for a private owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wyoming OSLI state-trust mineral leasing or its customer portal | Leases and permits for Wyoming **state trust lands**. The portal expressly limits its services to that state-trust program. | The royalty clause in a privately executed lease or the reader's private lessor status. |
| BLM Wyoming oil-and-gas lease-sales notice | Parcels offered under BLM-administered **federal** leasing. | The rate in a separate private lease. |
| BLM Wyoming federal-mineral GIS layer | A mapped lead concerning federal or Indian-trust mineral interests. BLM says the layer does not depict private mineral ownership patterns. | Present private mineral title, private lease coverage, or a payment fraction. |
| Identified private lease and controlling amendments | The contractual text to bring to a qualified review for this particular interest. | A final answer without checking the parties, lands, depths, assignments, amendments and applicable facts. |

The first three rows describe the agencies' own source scope, not a classification of any reader's parcel. [OSLI Customer Portal](https://lands.wyo.gov/customer-portal); [BLM Wyoming lease sales](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/oil-and-gas/leasing/regional-lease-sales/wyoming); [BLM Wyoming Federal Mineral Estate layer metadata](https://gis.blm.gov/wyarcgis/rest/services/Lands/BLM_WY_FederalMineralEstate/FeatureServer).

## A narrow document check before comparing percentages

1. **Write down the source of the percentage.** Is it quoted from your signed lease, a later amendment, a division order, a royalty statement, a state-trust lease page, or a federal lease-sale notice? Keep the document date and identified tract or lease number with the quotation. A public rate in a government lease notice should not be copied into a private document without a documented connection.
2. **Identify the lessor and lease in the documents you actually have.** Note the names, legal description, effective date and any amendments or assignments you can identify. Do not treat a shared Wyoming location or a well name as proof that the public lease and private lease are the same instrument.
3. **Separate the royalty fraction from the payment decimal.** A stated lease royalty is not automatically the decimal a payor applies to a particular well or production month. The [MRX lease-rate/value guide](/blog/how-lease-royalty-rates-influence-mineral-value/) explains why ownership, participation and other terms matter; this article's separate job is choosing the right **rate authority** before making any comparison.
4. **Escalate a conflict, do not guess a replacement rate.** If the lease text, amendment, division order and check disagree—or if the interest may involve unleased pooling or mixed public/private minerals—ask a qualified Wyoming oil-and-gas attorney or land professional to identify the governing documents and legal effect. For an accounting question, the payor or a qualified royalty auditor may be an appropriate additional contact. Do not replace the contract percentage with an online “Wyoming rate.”

An owner who only wants to research can keep this source-and-document list without creating an account or booking a call. The [oil-and-gas-royalties pillar](/learning-center/oil-and-gas-royalties/) explains the wider payment vocabulary. If the unresolved question is whether federal minerals were reserved under a Wyoming tract, the separate [federal-reservation guide](/blog/how-to-check-federal-mineral-reservations-in-wyoming/) addresses that record path; it does not calculate a private royalty rate.

## Source and compliance boundaries to preserve in any public version

- Do **not** present a particular percentage as standard, fair, current, minimum, required, or owed on a private Wyoming lease. The BLM comparative report's numeric examples are from a 2018 study and cannot establish today's private or public terms.
- Do **not** use the BLM 2024 Private Acquired Lease memorandum as if all private Wyoming leases were acquired by a federal agency. Its special PAQ scenario is not the reader's assumed fact pattern.
- Do **not** claim OSLI, BLM, WOGCC, or MRX has interpreted this owner's lease, title or royalty check. The educational comparison leaves those owner-specific conclusions open.
- Do **not** imply that the BLM Wyoming mineral-estate layer maps private ownership. Its metadata expressly disclaims that.
