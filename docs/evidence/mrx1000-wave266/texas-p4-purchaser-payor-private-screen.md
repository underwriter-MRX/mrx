## One reader job / one pillar

Question: **Does a Texas RRC P-4 Purchaser Have to Be My Royalty Payor?** A Texas owner compares a `Purchaser` in the public RRC P-4 Gatherer/Purchaser Query with a different company on a royalty check or division order. The answer belongs under the **oil-and-gas-royalties** pillar. It should explain what the two labels identify, why they can differ, and what dated records to compare. It must not adjudicate a particular payment obligation or imply that a regulatory P-4 result proves title, payee status, a royalty decimal, or the controlling lease terms.

## Primary-source evidence

- Current RRC [About Oil & Gas Data Queries](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/about-oil-gas-data-queries/) says the P-4 designates a `Gatherer` that transports the product off the lease and a `Purchaser` that first purchases the product. It describes the P-4 Gatherer/Purchaser Query as updated daily. This identifies a regulatory/marketing role, not an individual royalty-account payor.
- The RRC's [P-4 instructions](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/media/kx0hcdrw/p-4-ins.pdf), p. 1, say the operator files Form P-4 in part to identify authorized gatherers and purchasers and their RRC-assigned system codes for a producing well or lease. The [public P-4 query](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/gathererPurchaserQueryAction.do) offers district, oil lease number/gas well ID, operator, gatherer/purchaser and product criteria. A result must be read with its source, lease/well, product and access time; no owner-specific query was performed for this screen.
- Current [Texas Natural Resources Code §91.401(2)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.91.htm) defines the `payor` as the party undertaking to distribute oil-and-gas proceeds to the payee. It expressly contemplates a purchaser, operator, or lessee and states a first-purchaser default subject to the stated arrangement with the owner of the right to produce. The exact statutory conditions matter: **do not say a P-4 purchaser can never be a payor**, and do not claim the RRC query alone proves that a named entity is the payor in this owner's case.
- The RRC [Royalties FAQ](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/royalties-faq/) is an informal public guide, not legal advice; it says the RRC cannot intervene in private royalty matters. It identifies statement fields for property and payor contact, points to the statutory change-of-payor notice, and explains that a division order or check detail can serve as a notice if it has the required information. Avoid turning this article into a statutory notice or deadline checklist without renewed legal review.
