**Wyoming W.S. 30-5-305(b) lists eleven labeled information categories when payment for oil or gas production is made to an interest owner.** The information belongs on the check stub or an attachment **unless it is otherwise provided on a regular monthly basis**. Gather all of those materials before calling a field missing. The list helps you locate and question information; it does not decide title, a correct decimal, allowed deductions or an amount owed. [Wyoming Legislature, Title 30](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127).

This is educational source guidance, not a review of your payor's statement or legal compliance. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction and would disclose a buyer relationship before an agreement. You can use the cited source without contacting MRX.

## Start with all payment materials

Put the payment record, check stub, any attachment and any separate information the payor provides regularly each month into one dated file. The [statutory placement rule](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) does **not** require every category on one paper check. A check or statement date may differ from the month and year of the underlying sales. If several properties or months appear, keep each property's lines separate.

Do not put private owner numbers, tax identifiers, banking details or a real person's complete statement in a public search or chat. For a permitted private review, use a controlled copy with unrelated sensitive details removed.

## Locate Wyoming's eleven information categories

The current Legislature-hosted [W.S. 30-5-305(b)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) lists the categories below. The right column is a plain-language location aid, not a model statement or a ruling about your payor:

| Statutory category                                           | What to locate or ask the payor to identify                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Lease, property or well name **or** identification number    | Preserve the identifier that connects the reported sale to a property or well.                   |
| Month and year of sales                                      | Identify the sales period separately from check and statement dates.                             |
| Total barrels of oil or thousands of cubic feet of gas sold  | Note the product-specific quantity and unit.                                                     |
| Price per barrel of oil or thousand cubic feet of gas        | Note the unit price for the reported product and period.                                         |
| Total state severance, ad valorem and other production taxes | Keep taxes separate from other charges.                                                          |
| Itemized other deductions or adjustments                     | Locate each reported charge or correction rather than relying on an unexplained net.             |
| Net value of total sales after deductions                    | Distinguish property-level net sales from the owner's payment.                                   |
| Owner's interest in sales as a decimal                       | Record the reported share for the identified lease, property or well; it is not a title opinion. |
| Owner's share of total sales value before deductions         | Locate the reported owner-level gross share.                                                     |
| Owner's share of sales value less deductions                 | Locate the reported owner-level amount after deductions.                                         |
| Address for additional information and questions             | Identify the supplied route for questions about the owner's production interest.                 |

**Total sales, owner's share, taxes and other deductions are different statutory categories.** If a payor uses its own labels, do not silently substitute one number for another. Ask which displayed field corresponds to the statutory category. A category missing from the stub alone may appear in an attachment or regular monthly information.

## Ask about an unclear or unlocated category

Make a two-column inventory: statutory category and the exact document, page or field where you found it. Write `not located in materials reviewed` rather than `not provided` until you have checked the available stub, attachment and regular monthly information. Save the payment date, sales period, property ID and original unmodified records without posting private details publicly.

Ask the payor using a verified contact route where it supplies the category and what its labels or codes mean. [Section 30-5-305(b)(xi)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) calls for an address where additional information about the owner's production interest may be obtained and questions answered. It also contains a certified-mail response provision, but this checklist is not a formal-notice or remedy guide. Whether to use a legal process for a particular dispute calls for qualified Wyoming counsel.

If your concern is a **payment decimal or lease rate**, keep it separate from the information-location question. [W.S. 30-5-305(a)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) says a division order cannot alter a lease or other contractual agreement to the extent of an attempted alteration. The [Wyoming private-lease rate guide](/blog/does-wyoming-set-the-royalty-rate-in-my-private-oil-and-gas-lease/) explains why public state or federal rates do not set an unrelated private lease's fraction. Neither article calculates your correct decimal.

For a broader way to reconcile product, volume, price, taxes, deductions and adjustments over time, see [How to Decode Your Royalty Check Statement](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/). That general worksheet is not a substitute for Wyoming's specific disclosure list or an owner-specific audit.

### Must all eleven categories appear on one check stub?

No. [W.S. 30-5-305(b)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) allows the labeled information on the stub or an attachment unless it is otherwise provided regularly monthly. Review all supplied materials before describing a category as unlocated.

### Which property and period details should I locate first?

Locate the lease, property or well name or identification number and the **month and year of sales** for which payment was made. The payment date is not a substitute for the sales period.

### Does the reported owner decimal prove my correct share?

No. The statute calls for the owner's sales interest as a decimal. The displayed number records the payor's reporting, not a title opinion or an owner-specific entitlement finding. Keep the identified property and period with it.

### What if I cannot locate a required category?

Check the stub, any attachment and regular monthly information. Mark the category `not located in materials reviewed`, preserve the originals and ask the verified payor where it supplies or defines the field. One page alone does not prove a legal violation.

### Can this checklist tell me whether I was underpaid?

No. The disclosure list does not validate title, a lease provision, payment decimal, deductions or amount due. A material owner-specific dispute requires qualified Wyoming legal or accounting review.

**Bottom line:** Wyoming's eleven-category rule is a way to check where information appears, not a shortcut to a payment verdict. Preserve the full set of materials, keep the categories separate and ask the verified payor for a category you cannot locate or interpret.
