Provisional title: **What Information Does Wyoming Require for Oil and Gas Royalty Payments?**

Primary question: A Wyoming owner received an oil or gas payment. Which information should they look for in the payor's materials? Primary pillar: oil-and-gas royalties. This private manuscript is unadmitted, unreviewed public copy, unimaged, unpublished and uncounted. It must not be moved to `src/content/posts/` without the remaining gates in the private screen.

---

If you receive a payment for oil or gas production from a Wyoming property, start by collecting the payment record, any check-stub attachment, and any information the payor supplies separately each month. [Wyoming Statutes §30-5-305(b)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) lists eleven labeled categories of information to be included on the check stub or an attachment **unless the information is otherwise provided on a regular monthly basis**. It does not say that every category must appear on one paper check. Use the list to locate missing explanations, not to conclude that a specific amount is wrong or that you have established title.

## Match the rule to the materials you actually received

The statute concerns payment for production to an **interest owner**. It distinguishes the information that accompanies or is otherwise regularly provided with payment from the legal question of who is entitled to what share. Before reviewing a single line, put the check or deposit advice, statement pages, any electronic detail, and any separate monthly report in one dated file. A statement date and the **month and year of sales** are not necessarily the same thing. If several properties or months appear, keep each property's lines separate.

Do not enter private owner numbers, tax identifiers, banking details or a real person's complete statement in a public search or chat. If you need to share the records for review, use a controlled copy with sensitive details removed.

## Wyoming's eleven information categories

The current Legislature-hosted [Title 30 text, §30-5-305(b)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) lists these categories. The plain-language prompts below are a location aid, not a model statement or a ruling about your payor:

| Statutory category | What to locate or ask the payor to identify |
|---|---|
| Lease, property or well name **or** an identification number | Which property or well the reported sale belongs to. Preserve the exact identifier. |
| Month and year of sales | The sales period covered by the payment, separate from check and statement dates. |
| Total barrels of oil or thousands of cubic feet of gas sold | The product-specific quantity and the unit used. |
| Price per barrel of oil or thousand cubic feet of gas | The stated unit price for that product and period. |
| Total state severance, ad valorem and other production taxes | The taxes reported for the production. Do not merge them with other charges. |
| Itemized other deductions or adjustments | Each reported charge or correction rather than a single unexplained net. |
| Net value of total sales after deductions | The reported property-level net sales value, not automatically your payment. |
| Owner's interest in sales, as a decimal | The reported share tied to the identified lease, property or well. A displayed decimal is not a title opinion. |
| Owner's share of total sales value before deductions | The reported owner-level gross share. |
| Owner's share of sales value less deductions | The reported owner-level amount after deductions. |
| Address for additional information and questions | The payor contact route supplied for the owner's production-interest questions. |

The statute's labels matter: **total sales**, **owner's share**, **taxes**, and **other deductions** are different categories. An owner should not substitute a payor's own terms without asking which displayed field corresponds to a statutory category. Also, a missing field on the stub alone is not the entire inquiry; first look for an attachment or information provided regularly each month.

## If a category is hard to find

Keep a neutral two-column inventory: statutory category and the exact document/page/field where you found it. Write `not located in materials reviewed` rather than `not provided` if you have not checked a separate monthly report or all of the payor's channels. Save the payment date, sales period, property ID and the original unmodified records. Do not post this private inventory publicly.

Ask the payor, using its verified contact route, where the category is supplied and what its own codes or labels mean. [Section 30-5-305(b)(xi)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) calls for an address where additional information about the owner's production interest may be obtained and questions answered. It also contains a certified-mail response provision when information is requested by certified mail. Whether to use any formal notice or legal remedy for a particular dispute is a question for qualified Wyoming counsel; this educational checklist is not a demand letter and does not determine a violation.

If your issue is an unexplained **payment decimal or lease rate**, keep it separate from a missing-information question. Wyoming's [§30-5-305(a)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=127) says a division order cannot alter the lease or another contractual agreement to the extent of an attempted alteration. The [Wyoming private-lease rate guide](/blog/does-wyoming-set-the-royalty-rate-in-my-private-oil-and-gas-lease/) explains why a public state or federal rate does not set a private lease's fraction. Neither rule computes your correct decimal. A qualified land professional or attorney may need the actual lease, conveyances, unit documents and payor calculation support.

For a broader, nonstatutory way to reconcile products, volumes, prices, deductions and adjustments over time, see [How to Decode Your Royalty Check Statement](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/). That worksheet is not a substitute for Wyoming's specific disclosure list or an owner-specific audit.

## What this checklist cannot establish

The presence of eleven labels does not prove the amounts are correct. The absence of a label on one page does not prove the payor failed to provide it by another permitted method. A statement, even a complete one, does not prove ownership, lease status, a contractual royalty fraction, permissible deductions, a payment default or damages. Preserve the original records and ask the verified payor for missing explanations. Take a material legal or accounting dispute to a qualified Wyoming attorney, accountant or royalty specialist. MRX can help organize documents for a directional review but does not provide a title opinion, legal advice, certified audit or appraisal and may become a buyer in some transactions, which it discloses before an agreement.
