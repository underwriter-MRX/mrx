Provisional title: **When Can a Wyoming Payor Accumulate a Small Royalty Payment?**

Provisional primary pillar: `/learning-center/oil-and-gas-royalties/`.

**Wyoming W.S. 30-5-301(b) distinguishes a small accumulated production payment from the default payment schedule.** For a person legally entitled to proceeds, the statute provides for annual remittance of up to twelve months' accumulated proceeds when the total owed is $100 or less. It separately says that, on a payee's written request, a payment is to be remitted within 60 days after the request is received if accumulated proceeds are at least $25. Those are conditional rules, not evidence that a particular missing check was properly delayed—or that money is due to the reader. [Wyoming Legislature, current Title 30, W.S. 30-5-301(a)–(c)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124).

This is educational source guidance, not legal advice or a finding about any payor's compliance. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction and would disclose a buyer relationship before an agreement. You can read the statute and organize your records without contacting MRX.

## Read the two dollar figures as different conditions

The **$100-or-less** language concerns the total amount owed under the statute's annual-aggregation provision. It does not say a payor may hold a small balance indefinitely. The **$25-or-more** language is a separate condition tied to a **written request from the payee** and 60 days following its receipt. It does not say that every balance of $25 automatically generates an immediate check or that a casual inquiry qualifies as the statutory request. The text also addresses payment following cessation of production or a change in the party responsible for payment; those provisions should not be omitted when a case turns on those events. [W.S. 30-5-301(b)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124).

The subsection applies to proceeds from production and people legally entitled to them. [Subsection (a)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124) sets default first and subsequent payment periods but also recognizes other periods or arrangements in a valid contract with the entitled person. The responsible payor can depend on legal arrangements; [subsection (c)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124) addresses a purchaser paying owners under an operator–purchaser arrangement. None of those facts can be established by a generic article.

## What to check before drawing a conclusion

Keep the prior check or statement, property or well identifier, sales months, the name of the payor shown, any notice identifying a changed payor, and any communication about a held amount. Ask the **verified payor** whether it has recorded an amount for that interest and sales period, how it tracks accumulated proceeds, which payment arrangement it believes applies, and where it has sent recent statements or checks. Do not share full owner, tax or banking identifiers in a public search or chat. A payor's answer is evidence of its records, not independent verification of title, decimal or amount owed.

If you are considering a written request under subsection (b)(ii), the statutory wording and its application to your facts matter. This article is not a request template or instruction to send one; a qualified Wyoming lawyer can evaluate an owner-specific dispute, especially where entitlement, contract terms, amount or payor identity is contested.

Do not conflate this small-balance provision with a different explanation for nonpayment. [W.S. 30-5-302](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=125) addresses circumstances in which payment cannot be made and an escrow process; it does not establish that any reader's funds were escrowed. The Treasurer's [unclaimed-property search](https://statetreasurer.wyo.gov/unclaimed-property/) is another separate route, not a finding that the payor reported this interest. [Wyoming's payment-information guide](/blog/what-information-does-wyoming-require-for-oil-and-gas-royalty-payments/) explains a different question: what information accompanies a payment. The [private-lease rate guide](/blog/does-wyoming-set-the-royalty-rate-in-my-private-oil-and-gas-lease/) addresses what sets a lease's royalty fraction, not whether a small accumulated amount has reached a remittance condition.

**Bottom line:** The current Wyoming text distinguishes annual remittance for $100-or-less accumulated proceeds from a conditional written-request path at $25 or more. Neither figure, by itself, answers whether a particular owner is entitled, what has accumulated, or whether a payment is late. Preserve the specific property, sales period, payor and correspondence and resolve an owner-specific legal dispute with qualified Wyoming counsel.
