## Supporting article outlines — existing URLs, not new identities

These outlines identify useful future reviewed refinements to **already published** guides. They do not authorize a new canonical URL or claim the sections are not already present; inspect each current article before editing it.

1. **Offer review — [Mineral Rights Offer vs. Letter of Intent vs. Purchase Agreement](/blog/mineral-rights-offer-vs-letter-of-intent-vs-purchase-agreement/).** Open with the practical difference between document labels and actual terms. Show the owner how to inventory the full document packet, compare rights/price/adjustment/closing fields, flag missing exhibits, and preserve versions. End with questions for the buyer and qualified legal review, not a binding-effect conclusion. Distinct from the offer hub's four-part decision framework because it compares the roles of specific document types.
2. **Inherited rights — [Understanding Royalty Checks After Inheriting Mineral Rights](/blog/understanding-royalty-checks-after-inheriting-mineral-rights/).** Open with the payor-record handoff after a death; identify which owner number, lease/well, statements, estate records, and correspondence to collect; distinguish administrative payor updates from legal title; give a source-and-date register and a private-document next step. Keep Texas probate/title specifics in their dedicated guide and outside professionals' scope.
3. **Interest verification — [How to Find Out If You Own Mineral Rights in Texas](/blog/how-to-find-out-if-you-own-mineral-rights-in-texas/).** Route from county/legal description to recorded instrument chain, then separate RRC activity, tax, and payor evidence. For each record, state what it may show, what it cannot settle, the retrieval date, and the next unresolved link. Stop before owner-specific title certification or a conclusion from a single search hit. Link back to the cross-state pillar for non-Texas readers.
4. **Royalties — [How to Decode Your Royalty Check Statement](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/).** Use an explicitly fictional line-item illustration to walk through property/payor, product, production month, volume, price, decimal, deductions, adjustments, and net payment. Explain why statement, check, and public production periods may differ; route an unexplained line to the payor and qualified accounting/title/legal review. Keep Texas RRC data at its lease-level operator-reported scope and ONRR examples explicitly within federal/Indian payment systems.

Primary-source boundaries: [Texas State Library land/property research](https://www.tsl.texas.gov/ref/propertyresearch), [BLM federal land records](https://www.blm.gov/services/land-records), [Texas RRC production-data FAQ](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/production-data-query-system-faqs/), and the [ONRR federal/Indian statement example](https://onrr.gov/document/ExplanationOfQtrlyStatementSection_12-2024.pdf).
