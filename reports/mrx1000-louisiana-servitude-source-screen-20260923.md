The [Louisiana Mineral Code, R.S. 31:27](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=87935) lists nonuse for ten years among **several** ways a mineral servitude may be extinguished. [R.S. 31:30](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=87939) distinguishes actual drilling/mining operations from preparatory activity, [R.S. 31:33](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=87942) limits certain unit-operation effects to the relevant portion of the tract, and [R.S. 31:36](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/LawPrint.aspx?d=87945) addresses actual production. The state's [SONRIS Oil/Gas Wells layer](https://sonris-gis.dnr.la.gov/arcgis/rest/services/DNRSvc/OMR/MapServer/13) is a public well-location dataset. A well-map point or broad search query cannot establish whether a particular servitude exists, was used, was interrupted, was suspended, or has prescribed. [R.S. 31:115](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=87805) separately treats mineral leases; do not conflate a lease with a servitude or apply one rule to every mineral interest.
