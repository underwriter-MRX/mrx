## Reader question and proposed answer boundary

An owner has received a privately prepared Texas mineral ownership report and a buyer letter. What can the owner check in the report before using its stated tract or fraction in an offer discussion? Proposed answer object: a **report-to-record exception register** noting the preparer, report/as-of date, exact tract or legal description, interest type, stated fraction or acreage basis, cited county instrument references, declared scope/assumptions, and unresolved qualifications. The owner's actual report, recorded instruments, and letter remain unavailable to a public guide; the article cannot determine title, transferable acreage, royalty decimal, value, fairness, or legal effect. | [Texas Property Code, Chapter 13](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.13.htm) | Recording/notice framework for Texas real-property instruments. | A legal conclusion about an unseen deed, chain, reservation, or disputed interest. |
| [Historical GLO archive file containing a preliminary private ownership report](https://cdn.glo.texas.gov/ncu/SCANDOCS/archives_webfiles/arcmaps/webfiles/landgrants/PDFs/1/1/5/0/1150534.pdf), PDF page 26 of 40 | A *dated example*, visually inspected, of a report with preparer/date, tract, ownership table, title notes, expressly limited index review, and a disclaimer that it is not an attorney title examination/opinion. | A universal report format, current status of that tract, GLO endorsement of the private preparer, or owner-specific legal advice. The archive includes personal names/addresses; do not reproduce them or the page image in public content. |

The official archive is an example of what one report disclosed, not a template or authority for every report. A report may have no supporting instrument list, different interest definitions, or an express limitation. Missing or conflicting evidence should remain unresolved and be routed to a qualified Texas title professional rather than reconciled by guesswork.
