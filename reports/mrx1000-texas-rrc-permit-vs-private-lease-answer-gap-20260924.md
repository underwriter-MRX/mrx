## Reader job and locked answer boundary

Proposed exact title: **Does a Texas RRC Drilling Permit Prove Your Lease Covers a Well?** Primary pillar: `/learning-center/title-lease-ownership/`. The answer is **no**: a permit and its displayed “Lease” field are regulatory records, not an agency determination that a particular owner's private mineral lease is valid, covers a tract or formation, includes a wellbore, or creates a royalty entitlement. The useful answer object is a short **permit-to-private-record comparison**: identify the specific W-1/permit and plat; preserve its regulatory lease name, API/permit number, status, operator, and planned wellbore path; separately identify the owner's recorded instruments, private lease, and any relevant pooling/unit documents; list unmatched or missing items without resolving title. A qualified Texas title/land professional interprets any disputed connection.

This is not a drilling forecast, title opinion, pooling ruling, trespass allegation, royalty calculation, valuation, or advice to challenge a permit. A surface location outside a named lease for a horizontal well does not by itself settle lease coverage. A permit application, approved permit, completion, and production are different events and must not be collapsed.
