## Distinct reader job and answer contract

Reader situation: an owner has a Wyoming tract description and is considering whether a purported private mineral interest can be offered for sale, but a federal reservation or split estate may affect the tract. Primary question: **“How can I check whether minerals under my Wyoming tract were reserved to the federal government?”** This asks for a federal-record research path and a specific competing-estate question, not another broad “a state map does not prove my private title” warning.

Bounded answer: BLM describes private surface over federal minerals as one split-estate situation. For land patented under the Stock Raising Homestead Act, its owner guidance says mineral resources reserved in the patent belong to the United States; it directs the owner to the local BLM office, a Master Title Plat, and the original land patent to check federal reservation. BLM's Wyoming federal-mineral GIS layer depicts its federal/tribal mineral-interest compilation and explicitly **does not depict private mineral ownership patterns**. A layer symbol or absence is therefore a lead to reconcile with BLM's underlying records, not a title opinion, a complete private-chain search, or a sale-eligibility decision. Identify the tract accurately, retain the original patent/Master Title Plat references, and compare any private conveyance chain with qualified Wyoming title help before claiming what can be sold. Do not assert that every private Wyoming surface tract has federal minerals, that every reservation covers every mineral type/depth, or that a map result settles percentage or current private ownership.

Business objective: prevent an owner from treating surface ownership or a single map as sale-ready private mineral title and preserve a useful research-first next step.
