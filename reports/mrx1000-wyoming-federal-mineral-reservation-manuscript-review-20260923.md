# How to Check Federal Mineral Reservations in Wyoming

If you have a Wyoming tract description and want to know whether minerals beneath it were reserved to the federal government, start with the **federal land records**, not a surface-ownership assumption or one map color. The [Bureau of Land Management's split-estate guidance](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/mining-and-minerals/split-estate) directs an owner to the relevant BLM office, a Master Title Plat and the original land patent. Read the patent for any mineral reservation; BLM's Stock Raising Homestead Act example explains why the act under which land was patented can matter. This federal-record lookup does not review private conveyances or establish what you currently own or can sell. MRX recommends asking a qualified Wyoming title professional or attorney about unclear or conflicting instruments.

This is educational source navigation, not a title opinion, legal advice, a certified appraisal or a promise of a Wyoming transaction. The examples below do not determine whether a particular parcel has federal minerals, which substances or depths a reservation covers, the reader's ownership percentage, or anyone's authority to transfer an interest.

## Why the original patent matters

BLM describes **private surface over federally owned minerals** as one split-estate situation. Its [Stock Raising Homestead Act (SRHA) explanation](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/mining-and-minerals/split-estate) says mineral resources reserved in patents under that act belong to the United States. But BLM also explains that other Homestead Acts did not all reserve the same mineral interests. The relevant question is therefore not “Does my land look like an SRHA example?” It is “What does the original patent and its governing act say about this tract?”

For this federal question, use the patent and Master Title Plat identified by BLM. This article does not interpret a private deed, tax account, royalty statement or later conveyance. Keep the tract description from the document you are researching at hand when asking BLM which federal records to check. The [title, lease and ownership records hub](/learning-center/title-lease-ownership/) explains the different roles of public and private records.

## A source-first research sequence

1. **Write down the tract description exactly as it appears in an identified document.** Note the document type, date and source. If two papers describe the land differently, keep both versions separate; do not silently merge them into one assumed parcel.
2. **Ask the relevant BLM office what federal records cover that description.** BLM's [SRHA guidance](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/mining-and-minerals/split-estate) specifically directs owners to the local office when researching whether minerals are federally owned. An office can help identify the appropriate federal record set; this article cannot perform that match for a reader.
3. **Check the Master Title Plat for the specified township.** BLM says its [General Land Office records](https://www.blm.gov/services/land-records) include Master Title Plats showing current federal land status by township. Other oil-and-gas or leasable-mineral plats can add lease-status context. A plat is a federal-record lead, not a private title opinion.
4. **Read the original patent and the reservation it actually states.** BLM directs owners to the patent to identify the Homestead Act under which land was patented. Do not apply the SRHA example to a different patent or assume a reservation has the same scope for every mineral substance. If the patent, plat or underlying federal file is missing from the online collection, BLM says not every federal title record is online and points readers to a state or local office for additional records. [BLM Federal Land Records](https://www.blm.gov/services/land-records).
5. **Keep the federal and private questions separate.** Record what the federal documents appear to say. This article does not determine the effect of any private deed, reservation or later conveyance. MRX recommends qualified Wyoming title or legal review before relying on a private ownership or sale conclusion.

This sequence is a way to organize a research question, not a filing instruction, parcel-title determination or assurance that a particular BLM office can settle every private conveyance issue.

## Use the Wyoming federal-mineral layer as a lead, not an answer

The [BLM Wyoming Federal Mineral Estate layer description](https://gis.blm.gov/wyarcgis/rest/services/Lands/BLM_WY_FederalMineralEstate/FeatureServer/0) says its dataset depicts federal and Indian-trust mineral interests, including mineral types and stated reserved percentages. It also expressly says the dataset **does not depict private mineral ownership patterns** and was compiled from source materials of different vintages. Because of those stated limits, MRX treats the layer as a lead for further federal-record review, not as proof of private ownership or authority to sell.

This BLM layer describes federal and Indian-trust mineral-estate data only. It does not answer a private-title question. The [Wyoming mineral-rights page](/mineral-rights/wyoming/) offers state context; it does not certify an individual's title.

| Source | Useful question it can help frame | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original federal patent and any applicable Homestead Act | What reservation was stated when the land was patented? | The effect of every later conveyance or the reader's present private share. |
| Master Title Plat and related federal records | What federal land-status or lease context is recorded for the relevant township? | A full private mineral chain or a parcel-specific legal opinion. |
| Wyoming federal-mineral GIS layer | Is there a mapped federal/Indian-trust mineral-interest lead to investigate? | Private ownership patterns or authority to sell. |

The source roles in this table follow [BLM's SRHA guidance](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/mining-and-minerals/split-estate), [federal land-records description](https://www.blm.gov/services/land-records) and [Wyoming layer metadata](https://gis.blm.gov/wyarcgis/rest/services/Lands/BLM_WY_FederalMineralEstate/FeatureServer/0). The final column states research limits, not findings about a specific tract.
