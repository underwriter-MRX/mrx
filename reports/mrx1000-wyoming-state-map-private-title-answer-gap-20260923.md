## Reader job and evidence order

An owner researching Wyoming minerals before a possible sale sees a state “subsurface ownership” map and wants to know whether its result establishes that owner's **private** mineral interest. The narrow proposed answer is **no**: Wyoming's Office of State Lands and Investments (OSLI) says its Land and Lease Map Viewer covers assets and leases under the State Board of Land Commissioners, including state surface/subsurface ownership and mineral leases **on state land**. That scope is different from researching a private owner's recorded conveyances in the relevant county. A map hit, absence of a hit, well record, or royalty statement should not be presented as a private title opinion or sale-readiness decision.

Business objective: help a relevant owner identify the right evidence path and unresolved title question for a qualified human review. The useful research-first next step is to retain the property description and any deeds/estate papers, identify the Wyoming county, use that county clerk's recorded-land-document route, and ask an appropriate Wyoming title professional about conflicting or missing instruments.
