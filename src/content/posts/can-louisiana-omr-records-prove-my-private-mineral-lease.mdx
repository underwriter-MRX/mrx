**No.** Do not treat records described by Louisiana’s archived Office of Mineral Resources (OMR) pages, or a SONRIS result by itself, as proof of **your private mineral lease**. The state’s [archived OMR description](https://denr.louisiana.gov/page/divisions) says it manages the state’s mineral assets and that its Energy Lands Division maintains **state** mineral-lease ownership data and official **state** lease files. Its [division detail](https://www.denr.louisiana.gov/page/divisions-energy-lands) describes transfers of interests in state leases and SONRIS access to those state files. Those descriptions do not establish whether a particular private lease was recorded, is effective, covers your tract, or gives you a payment right. [Louisiana R.S. 44:71](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=99755) identifies a different record-office class: parish offices for conveyances and mortgages, with district-court clerks serving as parish recorders.

This is a distinction between **record sources**, not a title opinion. An OMR record might mention a private person; a parish-index hit is also only a lead. Keep the actual instrument and its source locator separate from any conclusion about ownership or lease coverage. This public article cannot review your deed, lease, chain of title, tract, payment entitlement, or a buyer’s offer. A qualified Louisiana-licensed attorney experienced in mineral title should review disputed rights. Do not send unredacted private documents through an untrusted channel. MRX may have an economic interest in a later transaction and discloses that relationship before an agreement.

## What the state files can and cannot answer

OMR’s [archived divisions page](https://denr.louisiana.gov/page/divisions) describes leasing of state-owned lands and water bottoms. The [Energy Lands detail](https://www.denr.louisiana.gov/page/divisions-energy-lands) says the Docket and Ownership Section reviews transfers of interests in **state leases** and the Records Management Section keeps state lease files; it also describes document and database access through SONRIS. These are useful research leads when the question actually involves a state lease or state tract. They are not described there as a universal private-owner title index.

As checked September 24, 2026, the cited archive said the Department of Energy and Natural Resources became the [Department of Conservation and Energy](https://www.dce.louisiana.gov/) in October 2025; the current agency home page listed an Office of State Resources. We cite archived OMR pages for their stated record scope, not as a claim that OMR remains the current office name or that a specific SONRIS function or document is available today.

## Where the separate parish question begins

[R.S. 44:71](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=99755) identifies parish offices for recording conveyances and mortgages and district-court clerks as parish recorders. That statute points to a relevant record-office category. It does **not** tell us whether your mineral instrument exists, was recorded correctly, remains effective, or covers a particular interest. A name or property-label match is only a possible record lead. Failure to locate a record is not proof that no right or instrument exists.

If you have a private lease, deed, reservation, or assignment, preserve its exact parties, dates, property description, parish, and any recording reference. Do not substitute a state-lease number, well map, or royalty statement for the instrument. If names or tract descriptions differ, leave the difference unresolved for qualified review rather than resolving it from a portal result.

## A bounded research sequence

1. Identify the question: **state mineral lease**, **private recorded instrument**, or an unresolved mixture. The presence of a state record does not settle the private question.
2. Preserve the document you actually have, including its page or document reference, date, parish, parties, and tract wording. Keep sensitive account and identity details private.
3. For an apparent state lease, use OMR’s described state-file scope as a lead. For an apparent private conveyance, ask the relevant parish recorder what records and search methods that office provides. This article does not assert a single statewide private-title portal or identical coverage at every office.
4. If the issue is ownership, lease coverage, payment entitlement, or conflicting instruments, give the actual documents and unresolved questions to a qualified Louisiana-licensed mineral-title attorney. This article cannot supply a conclusion for a signing or transfer decision.

## Common questions

### If I find my name in SONRIS, does that prove my private mineral lease?

No. OMR’s archived [Energy Lands description](https://www.denr.louisiana.gov/page/divisions-energy-lands) identifies state-lease ownership and documents among its records. A name hit needs the exact record and context; by itself it does not establish a private lease, mineral title, or tract coverage.

### If SONRIS does not show my lease, does that mean I have none?

No. The cited [OMR description](https://denr.louisiana.gov/page/divisions) concerns state mineral-lease administration. It does not establish that every private lease appears there. A missing result is not a determination about your instrument or rights.

### Does a parish recording prove I own the minerals?

No. [R.S. 44:71](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=99755) identifies recording offices; it does not decide the validity, priority, extent, or present effect of a particular recorded act. Keep the instrument and recording reference for qualified review.

### Should I use an OMR or parish record to decide whether a buyer’s offer is fair?

Neither source alone establishes ownership, acreage, lease coverage, or value. First resolve what document and interest the offer describes. The [title, lease, and ownership pillar](/learning-center/title-lease-ownership/) provides a broader document-first framework; it does not replace Louisiana-specific professional review.

### What details should I keep before contacting a parish recorder?

Keep the instrument you actually have and its parties, dates, parish, property description, and any recording reference. Ask that office what search methods and record coverage it provides. A match or missing result is not a title conclusion.
