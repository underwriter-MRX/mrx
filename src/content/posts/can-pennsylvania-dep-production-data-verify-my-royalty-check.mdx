**No.** A Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) production result can help you identify a reported well and reporting period, but it cannot verify what **you** were owed or paid. DEP says its [public well-production report](https://greenport.pa.gov/ReportExtracts/OG/OilGasWellProdReport) contains data supplied by well operators. Its [laws and regulations page](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/laws-regulations-and-guidelines) says the Commonwealth does not regulate lease agreements between mineral owners and producers and DEP does not audit payments. Your royalty statement, lease, division order, payor accounting and any allocation records answer different questions. A difference between the two sources is a research lead, not proof of an error, title, tract coverage, or value.

This is educational record-source guidance, not a payment audit, a Pennsylvania legal or tax conclusion, a title opinion, an engineering conclusion, or a valuation. Do not use it to decide that a payor breached a lease. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed. MRX is not an independent royalty auditor.

## Use DEP's well-report workflow, not a check-stub formula

The [DEP production extract](https://greenport.pa.gov/ReportExtracts/OG/OilGasWellProdReport) asks for a reporting period and offers operator and permit-number filters before exporting a CSV. Its description says the numbers are operator-reported to DEP. The [DEP mapping application](https://gis.dep.pa.gov/PaOilAndGasMapping/) lets users display well information by permit number, operator, county or municipality and link to DEP oil-and-gas reporting. A reviewer may use a map result as a possible permit or operator lead, not as proof of tract, lease or royalty attribution. That gives a Pennsylvania owner a bounded source route:

1. Identify the well or permit number on the private statement if it is shown. If it is missing, ask the payor for the well/property cross-reference; do not substitute a similar name or nearby map point.
2. In DEP's production report, record the reporting period and any operator or permit-number filter used. Export and retain the unedited CSV, source URL and access date. The interface states, “Limit to 13 selections unless an Operator or Permit is entered.” That is an export-interface rule, not a statement about how many months your check should cover.
3. Match the public result to the private statement's **identifier, product and period** before comparing volumes. If any of those do not align, record the difference as an open question rather than forcing a calculation.

The mapping app includes only wells with locational information within Pennsylvania and disclaims errors and omissions. A map point does not prove that a well is attributable to your tract or lease.

Before comparing to a private statement, identify whether the DEP result concerns an unconventional or conventional well. DEP's [2023 oil-and-gas annual report, Reporting Requirements](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/annual-report/dep-2023-oil-and-gas-annual-report) states that unconventional-well operators submit natural-gas production reports monthly, while conventional-well operators continue to report production data annually. A conventional annual public result is therefore not a month-by-month check-stub ledger. Even for an unconventional monthly result, compare the regulatory reporting period with the statement's payment or sales period before drawing an inference; the two labels serve different purposes. Do not divide an annual total by twelve or attribute a public total to your owner decimal without supporting records.

## Compare identifiers before numbers

Place the DEP export next to your royalty statement without uploading the private statement to a public search. Note the payor, property or well identifier, production or sales period, product, volume, owner decimal, adjustments and deductions **if shown** on the statement. If the public result appears absent or different, preserve the search inputs and date: DEP's [report data dictionary](https://files.dep.state.pa.us/OilGas/BOGM/BOGMPortalFiles/OilGasReports/HelpDocs/SSRS_Report_Data_Dictionary/DEP_Oil_and_GAS_Reports_Data_Dictionary.pdf) describes the production report as operator-submitted and cautions that DEP does not guarantee report accuracy, completeness or timeliness. The dictionary is Version 1.8, dated April 7, 2022, so confirm any field label in the current export rather than assuming it is unchanged.

A well-production figure alone cannot establish your lease terms, owner decimal, realized price, line-item deductions, adjustments, net payment, or whether your payment is right or wrong. The existing [royalty-statement guide](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/) explains how to read those private line items; this Pennsylvania guide is about using the state report without turning it into a payor ledger. If you consult DEP's dated data dictionary, confirm its field definitions against the current downloaded export.

## Ask a bounded question, not for a verdict from DEP

If a difference remains after matching the well, product and period, ask the payor which well/property, production or sales period, owner share and adjustment records bridge its statement to the reported well data. Keep the original statement and response together with the dated DEP export. Avoid assuming that a missing report means no production, or that a reported well proves you own an interest in it.

DEP's [laws and regulations page](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/laws-regulations-and-guidelines) says the Commonwealth does not regulate lease agreements between mineral owners and producers and DEP does not audit payments. For a disputed lease, decimal or payment right, seek a qualified Pennsylvania professional with the actual instruments and payor records. Do not send unredacted account details through an untrusted channel.

## Common questions

### Does a DEP production row prove that I own a royalty interest in the well?

No. DEP's [production report](https://greenport.pa.gov/ReportExtracts/OG/OilGasWellProdReport) is an operator-reported well-data source. It is not an owner title, lease-coverage or payee ledger. The relevant instruments and allocation records need separate review.

### Why might a conventional-well report not match one month's check?

DEP's [reporting-requirements summary](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/annual-report/dep-2023-oil-and-gas-annual-report) says conventional-well operators report production data annually, while unconventional-well operators submit natural-gas production reports monthly. A private payment line may represent another period or accounting basis. The cadence alone does not diagnose your check.

### Can I calculate my royalty from DEP's public volume?

Not reliably from that number alone. A well-production volume does not supply your owner-specific interest, contract and payor-accounting inputs. Do not apply a guessed decimal or price and present the result as money owed.

### Should I ask DEP to audit my royalty payment?

DEP [says it does not audit payments](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/oil-and-gas/laws-regulations-and-guidelines). Ask the payor for its calculation and records; bring a genuine private dispute to an appropriate qualified Pennsylvania professional.

### What should I save before asking the payor about a difference?

Save the dated DEP export and its reporting period, well/permit and operator identifiers, plus the relevant private statement and its own period and property identifiers. Keep private account details out of public tools. Ask one precise question about the unmatched identifier, period or line item.
