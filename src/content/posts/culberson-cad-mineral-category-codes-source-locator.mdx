## Use the five document-control states literally

### Source-recorded

`Source-recorded` means a publisher-page relationship, exact URL, document identity, page count, complete-file hash, broad section, page locator, or literal displayed label was captured from the retrieved source. It says nothing about equivalence, applicability, accuracy, completeness, classification, appraisal, tax treatment, protest position, legal effect, value, or an owner result.

### Not-displayed

`Not-displayed` means a defined item was not found in the specific retrieved source during the recorded review. It does not prove that the item never existed, should appear, is required, or has legal, appraisal, tax, or valuation significance.

### Source-conflict

`Source-conflict` means the current publisher page, linked PDF, retained official copy, or another official source displays a different URL, title, page count, hash, section name, page locator, category label, spelling, or code. Preserve each source separately. Do not merge wording, infer equivalence, or choose a controlling source.

### Controlled-record-stop

Use `controlled-record-stop` when the next step would require ZIP access, archive-member access, row data, private data, an owner, account, property, notice, appraisal, tax, lease, legal description, regulatory, production, protest, contact, communication, submission, payment, or transaction record. The label identifies a boundary. It does not authorize access.

### Authorized-human-stop

Use `authorized-human-stop` before code-equivalence judgment, applicability, account or property classification, evidence review, appraisal treatment, notice review, protest strategy, timing, tax position, legal interpretation, valuation, representation, contact, submission, payment, or sale advice.

The public workflow ends at either stop. It does not open credentials, collect private data, inspect a row, classify property, contact an office, draft a protest, submit a record, make a payment, or promise a result.

## Handle source drift without normalizing labels

Source drift can appear as a changed records-page link, filename, document title, page count, hash, section name, page locator, label, code, spelling, or punctuation. When a retained official copy differs from a current source:

1. preserve the live publisher-page URL and exact current linked PDF URL;
2. preserve the retained official URL or controlled-copy reference;
3. record retrieval date, time, and time zone for each source;
4. record each file's page count, bytes, and approved hash or pointer;
5. describe the exact displayed difference without correcting it;
6. mark `source-conflict`; and
7. stop before merging labels, inferring equivalence, choosing a controlling source, opening row data, or applying a code.

A newer timestamp does not authorize this page to decide priority. A matching title does not prove that labels or page locations are unchanged. A different hash proves only that the bytes differ; it does not explain why.
