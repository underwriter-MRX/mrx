> **MAP-review boundary.** This page preserves the current official Texas Comptroller Methods and Assistance Program source chain, exact DeWitt final-review PDF, complete-file identity, page locators, literal review labels, and retrieval provenance. It does not independently audit or certify the district, search a property, judge an appraisal, determine a tax or protest result, estimate mineral value, evaluate an offer, advise a sale, or promise an outcome. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The official **DeWitt CAD 2025 MAP review** is a 15-page Texas Comptroller report titled `2024-25 Final Methods and Assistance Program Review`. Its summary page records five mandatory results as `PASS`, four activity ratings as `Meets All`, and four area rows with displayed scores of `100`. The detailed Tier 3 pages show `Yes` or `N/A` answers and `No Recommendation` in every visible recommendation cell.

Those are the report's labels. They do not prove the accuracy, fairness, value, tax treatment, protest posture, or sale potential of any property or mineral interest.

## Start with the official MAP source chain

The [Texas Comptroller Methods and Assistance Program overview](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/index.php) says Tax Code Section 5.102 requires review of each appraisal district's governance, taxpayer assistance, operating procedures, and appraisal standards, procedures, and methodology at least once every two years. The Comptroller's Property Tax Assistance Division reviews approximately half of the appraisal districts each year, and the number of questions depends on a three-tier system.

That program page explains purpose and cadence. It does not supply the DeWitt result.

The separate [2025 MAP Review Results page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/2025/) says the listed appraisal-district reviews were completed in 2025 and provides the DeWitt link. Preserve that page because it establishes the current publisher-to-report relationship. A downloaded file alone does not prove where the publisher currently lists it.

## Read the page-2 summary literally

PDF page 2 displays five mandatory questions. The result column shows `PASS` for each:

- administrative functions under the cited Chapter 6 requirement;
- up-to-date appraisal maps;
- current implementation of the most recent reappraisal plan;
- up-to-date appraisal records and the stated procedures and practices; and
- reproducibility using written procedures and appraisal records.

The same page displays four activity ratings:

| Review area | Displayed rating |
| ----------- | ---------------- |
| Governance | Meets All |
| Taxpayer Assistance | Meets All |
| Operating Procedures | Meets All |
| Appraisal Standards, Procedures and Methodology | Meets All |

The report defines `Meets All` as a total point score of 100. It then displays these area rows:

| Review area | Questions excluding N/A | Yes points | Displayed score |
| ----------- | ----------------------- | --------- | -------------- |
| Governance | 17 | 17 | 100 |
| Taxpayer Assistance | 13 | 13 | 100 |

This source record transcribes the table. It does not recompute the score, create a combined total, compare another district or year, or convert a district-level label into an owner-specific conclusion.

## Keep the detailed review attached to its page range

PDF pages 3 through 15 identify the detailed instrument as a `2024-25 Final Methods and Assistance Program Tier 3 Review`. The numbered review questions run from 1 through 88 across governance, taxpayer assistance, operating procedures, and appraisal standards, procedures, and methodology.

Applicable answers are displayed as `Yes`; identified inapplicable answers are displayed as `N/A`. Every visible recommendation cell reads `No Recommendation`. Preserve those exact labels and the page range. Do not replace them with an unsourced claim that the district is perfect, flawless, universally compliant, or guaranteed to produce accurate appraisals.

The current [Guide to the Comptroller Methods and Assistance Program](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-1818.pdf) explains that reviewers evaluate district documentation and conduct onsite interviews, use mandatory pass/fail and yes/no questions, and publish final reviews after the review cycle. That process context does not add a property result or expand the meaning of the DeWitt report.

## Separate the MAP review from the annual-report G1 row

The [DeWitt CAD 2025 G1 annual-report source record](/blog/dewitt-cad-2025-g1-oil-gas-and-mineral-reserve-source-record/) owns a different document, publisher relationship, page, and field set. Its countywide G1 row does not supply evidence for this Comptroller MAP review. The MAP review does not validate, explain, or convert the annual-report row.

Do not combine the sources to calculate a score, judge a mineral category, infer appraisal accuracy, estimate a property value, evaluate a protest, or support an offer. A shared district name does not make two separate official publications analytically interchangeable.

## Use document-control states, not conclusions

Assign one of these states to a source item:

- `source-recorded`: the exact source URL, label, retrieval date, page locator, and visible observation were preserved;
- `not-displayed`: the current source did not visibly display the expected label or field;
- `source-conflict`: two preserved official sources display different identities, dates, labels, URLs, or results;
- `controlled-record-stop`: continuing would require a property record, private data, calculation, matching, comparison, or analysis outside this article; or
- `authorized-human-stop`: the question requires an authorized owner, appraiser, tax professional, attorney, or another qualified reviewer.

These states describe document control. They do not mean accurate, inaccurate, valid for a property, legally controlling, appraised, taxable, protestable, valuable, accepted, fair, or recommended.

## Stop before owner-specific use

The MAP report does not answer what one mineral interest is worth or whether a particular appraisal is correct. Stop before:

- searching an owner, account, address, lease, well, property, or legal description;
- treating a district-level review label as proof about a specific property or mineral category;
- adding the MAP results to an appraisal notice, annual-report row, tax statement, production record, royalty statement, valuation, or offer;
- recalculating, aggregating, normalizing, benchmarking, ranking, trending, or comparing the displayed findings;
- deciding whether to protest, pay tax, accept an offer, sell, retain, lease, or transfer mineral rights; or
- contacting an office, submitting a record, making a payment, negotiating, or promising an outcome.

A qualified review may use other records for a different purpose, but that work must preserve its own authority, privacy, assumptions, dates, and limitations.
