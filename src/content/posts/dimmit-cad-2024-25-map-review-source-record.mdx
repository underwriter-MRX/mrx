> **MAP-review boundary.** This page preserves the official Texas Comptroller source chain, exact Dimmit final-review PDF, complete-file identity, page locators, literal report labels, and retrieval provenance. It does not independently audit or certify the district, search a property, judge an appraisal, decide a tax or protest result, establish title or ownership, estimate mineral value, evaluate an offer, advise a sale, or promise an outcome. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The official **Dimmit CAD 2024-25 MAP review** is a 15-page Texas Comptroller report titled `2024-25 Final Methods and Assistance Program Review: Dimmit Central Appraisal District`. Its page-2 summary records all five mandatory requirements as `PASS`, all four activity areas as `Meets All`, and a displayed score of `100` for each scored section. Pages 3 through 15 contain questions 1 through 88 with `Yes` or `N/A` responses and no recommendation displayed.

Those are district-level report findings. They do not determine the value, appraisal accuracy, tax treatment, protest outcome, title, ownership, or sale merits of any mineral property.

## Start with the official publisher relationship

The [Texas Comptroller Methods and Assistance Program overview](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/index.php) says Tax Code Section 5.102 requires a review of each appraisal district's governance, taxpayer assistance, operating procedures, and appraisal standards, procedures, and methodology at least once every two years. That page explains the program; it does not supply the Dimmit result.

The separate [2024 MAP Review Results page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/2024/) says the reviews listed there were completed in 2024 and links the Dimmit report. It also records a January 2026 recommendation-completion date for districts. Preserve that as a dated statement on the results page, not as a present deadline and not as proof that Dimmit had a recommendation to complete.

## Read the five mandatory results literally

PDF page 2 displays `PASS` for each mandatory requirement:

- administrative functions under the cited Tax Code Chapter 6 requirement;
- maps being up to date;
- current implementation of the most recent reappraisal plan;
- current appraisal records and the stated procedures and practices; and
- reproducibility of values using written procedures and appraisal records.

The current [Texas Tax Code Chapter 5](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.5.htm) supplies the official statutory text, while the report states the authority it applies. This article preserves that source relationship without offering a legal interpretation, district compliance opinion, or property conclusion.

## Follow the detailed review through pages 3-15

PDF pages 3 through 15 present questions 1 through 88 across governance, taxpayer assistance, operating procedures, and appraisal standards, procedures, and methodology. Answers are shown as `Yes` or `N/A`, and no recommendation is displayed in the report's recommendation cells.

That wording is deliberately narrow. It does not mean that the district is perfect, permanently unchanged, free of every possible issue, or proven to produce the correct result for a particular property. The [Comptroller's MAP Review Documents page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/review-documents.php) supplies the review-cycle materials; it does not expand the meaning of the Dimmit findings.

## Use a citation-ready source chain

For a verifiable answer, keep four evidence roles separate:

1. The MAP overview explains the program and statutory cadence.
2. The 2024 results page establishes the Comptroller-to-Dimmit report link and the recorded completion-year context.
3. The exact Dimmit PDF supplies its title, district identity, page-2 summary, detailed questions, displayed results, page count, and file hash.
4. Tax Code Chapter 5 supplies the statutory text without applying it to a district, property, or owner.

When citing a PDF-specific fact, name the Texas Comptroller, the exact report period, the Dimmit Central Appraisal District, and the page locator. Do not cite this explanatory article as though MRX issued or audited the government report.

## Use document-control states, not property conclusions

Assign one of these states to a source item:

- `source-recorded`: the exact source URL, label, retrieval date, page locator, and visible observation were preserved;
- `not-displayed`: the current source did not visibly display the expected label or field;
- `source-conflict`: preserved official sources display different identities, dates, labels, URLs, or results;
- `controlled-record-stop`: continuing requires a property record, private data, calculation, matching, comparison, or analysis outside this article; or
- `authorized-human-stop`: the question requires an authorized owner, appraiser, tax professional, attorney, or another qualified reviewer.

These are record-handling states. They do not mean accurate, inaccurate, valuable, taxable, protestable, accepted, fair, compliant, recommended, or ready for a transaction.

## Stop before owner-specific use

The MAP review does not answer what one mineral interest is worth or whether a particular appraisal is correct. Stop before:

- searching an owner, account, address, lease, well, property, or legal description;
- treating a district-level result as proof about a property, mineral category, title, ownership, reserves, production, revenue, or royalty entitlement;
- combining the report with an appraisal notice, annual report, tax statement, production record, royalty statement, valuation, or offer;
- recalculating, aggregating, benchmarking, ranking, trending, or comparing the displayed findings;
- deciding whether to protest, pay tax, accept an offer, sell, retain, lease, or transfer mineral rights; or
- contacting an office, submitting a record, making a payment, negotiating, or promising an outcome.

A qualified review may use other records for another purpose, but that work must preserve its own authority, privacy, assumptions, dates, evidence, and limitations.
