The official **Dimmit CAD 2025-2026 reappraisal plan** is a 148-page district PDF. Page 1 identifies the document as the `DIMMIT CENTRAL APPRAISAL DISTRICT REAPPRAISAL PLAN 2025/2026`, labels it an adopted plan under Resolution 2024-05, and displays August 21, 2024. Page 124 begins `Document 3D`, the attached plan addressing periodic reappraisal of oil-and-gas property.

Those facts identify and locate the source. They do not determine what a mineral interest is worth, whether an appraisal is correct, what tax is due, who owns an interest, whether an offer is fair, or whether an owner should sell.

## Read the plan purpose before using a page locator

PDF page 4 describes the plan as an organizational tool for the next two years and says the district may modify it to meet the Texas Property Tax Code. That is a source description of the plan's purpose. It is not a statement that every listed activity occurred, every appraisal is correct, or every property received a particular treatment.

PDF page 5 connects the plan requirement and periodic reappraisal activities to [Texas Tax Code Section 6.05](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.6.htm#6.05) and [Section 25.18](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.25.htm#25.18). ## Keep the historical parcel estimate in context

PDF page 6 identifies 2025 and 2026 as reappraisal years and records an estimated `60,750` parcels as of July 2024, including mineral and industrial accounts. That is a historical, district-wide planning estimate.

Do not restate it as a current count, mineral-account count, owner count, tract count, well count, lease count, transaction count, or measure of value. The sentence says the estimate includes mineral and industrial accounts; it does not say how many accounts fall into either category.

Pages 7 through 8 state that the district reappraises 33 percent each year using field inspection, its computer-assisted mass appraisal system, or both. Preserve that as the plan's stated schedule and methods. Do not use the percentage to predict whether a particular property was inspected or reappraised, whether a notice is correct, or what a tax or protest result should be.

## Find the attachment relationship on page 31

PDF page 31 records that Eagle Appraisal is assigned real and commercial property and Capitol Appraisal is assigned mineral and utilities work. The same page says the contracted firms' plans are attached.

Those are source-recorded contractor assignments within the retrieved plan. They are not an MRX endorsement, a statement about a current contract, proof of performance, confirmation of authority, or evidence that a particular property's appraisal is correct.

## Locate Document 3D on page 124

PDF page 124 begins `Document 3D`, titled `2025-2026 CAD Plan for Periodic Reappraisal of Oil and Gas Property`. It states that oil-and-gas property appraised for the district under the Capitol contract is reappraised annually.

The attachment describes identification work using Texas Railroad Commission monthly lease data, plats, W-2 and G-1 records, plus annual updates to property characteristics. These are the plan's stated inputs and procedures. They do not establish that a specific lease, well, tract, owner, or mineral interest was identified, matched, appraised, noticed, taxed, or valued correctly.

Pages 136 through 138 contain valuation-method material within that attachment. Stop at the source locator.
