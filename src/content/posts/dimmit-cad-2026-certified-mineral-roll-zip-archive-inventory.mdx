## What the integrity test proves and what it does not

An `unzip -t` integrity test reported each of the 13 members `OK` and ended with no compressed-data error. That is evidence that the retrieved ZIP passed that archive-level test.

It is not evidence that:

- every expected record or field is present;
- any member or record is accurate, current, authentic, certified, or legally effective;
- a name, address, account, property, value, or ownership indicator is correct;
- the public archive matches another retained copy or a separately submitted government file; or
- a property appraisal, tax result, protest position, mineral value, offer, or transaction decision is supported.

Keep the complete-file hash next to the test result. A future retrieval with a different hash is a different binary state even if the visible link label and filename have not changed.

## Use controlled source states

Assign one state to each retained observation:

- `source-recorded`: the official publisher relationship, direct archive, retrieval metadata, complete-file identity, central-directory inventory, and test result were preserved;
- `source-changed`: a later retrieval has a different URL, byte length, response field, hash, member list, member size, timestamp, or test result;
- `source-conflict`: the current official page displays a different label, target, year, or source relationship;
- `controlled-record-stop`: continuing would require member extraction, record-level review, private information, property matching, or another controlled source; or
- `authorized-human-stop`: the question requires an authorized official, attorney, appraiser, tax professional, title professional, privacy reviewer, or another qualified reviewer.

These states do not mean authentic, inauthentic, complete, incomplete, correct, incorrect, owned, appraised, taxable, protestable, valuable, fair, or recommended.
