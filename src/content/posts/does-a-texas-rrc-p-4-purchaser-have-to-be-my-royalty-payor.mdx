**Not necessarily.** The Texas Railroad Commission's [P-4 query description](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/about-oil-gas-data-queries/) calls `Purchaser` the company **designated** to first buy a product from the stated well or lease. Under [Texas Natural Resources Code §91.401(2)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.91.htm), the actual first purchaser is the default **royalty payor**, but the statute describes a specific arrangement with the owner of the right to produce that makes that owner the payor instead. A public P-4 designation does not prove an actual sale or decide who undertook to distribute proceeds for your property and period.

This is an educational comparison of source roles, not a determination that a named company owes you money. The RRC says it cannot decide private lease or royalty-payment matters. ## Read the public P-4 designation narrowly

The RRC distinguishes a `Gatherer` designated to move product off the lease from a `Purchaser` designated to buy it first. Its [P-4 instructions](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/media/kx0hcdrw/p-4-ins.pdf) explain that the operator files the form in part to identify authorized gatherers and purchasers for a producing well or lease. Those are regulatory and product-sale labels, not an owner-account payment ledger.

The [P-4 Gatherer/Purchaser Query](https://webapps2.rrc.texas.gov/EWA/gathererPurchaserQueryAction.do) currently displays oil/gas lease type, district, oil lease number or gas-well ID, operator, Gatherer/Purchaser type, P-5 number and Gatherer Product controls. Record the source, accessed date, exact field label, identifier and product. Do not assume that an oil line also describes a gas line or that today's query is an unchanged record of an earlier payment period. The companion [P-4 query provenance worksheet](/blog/texas-rrc-p-4-gatherer-purchaser-query-retrieval-provenance-worksheet/) handles the retrieval trail without interpreting a displayed company's private duties.

## Read `payor` as a separate legal and owner-record question

[Texas §91.401(2)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.91.htm) defines the payor as the party undertaking to distribute production-sale proceeds to the payee. It expressly describes a purchaser, operator or lessee, then says the **actual first purchaser** is the default unless the first purchaser and the owner of the right to produce arrange for the first purchaser to pay the sale proceeds to that owner, who becomes the payor. The rule is not “the P-4 purchaser always pays me,” nor is it “the purchaser never pays.” The P-4 field does not reveal the actual transaction or a particular account arrangement.

The RRC's [Royalties FAQ](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/royalties-faq/) is an informal public guide and says private royalty matters are outside the agency's authority. A royalty statement or check detail, division order, and any dated payor-change communication may identify the company addressing an owner's payment account. Compare their property and effective-period references rather than replacing them with the public P-4 line. The [royalty-statement guide](/blog/how-to-decode-your-royalty-check-statement/) explains those owner-record fields; this article answers only the purchaser-versus-payor role question.

## Compare two record lanes without deciding the outcome

- **Public RRC lane:** Keep the P-4 query or record source and access date; district, oil lease number or gas-well ID; product; and the `Gatherer`, `Purchaser` and operator labels separately. This is a regulatory designation, not proof of an actual sale or payment duty.
- **Private payment lane:** Keep the dated statement, check detail, division order or payor notice; property name or number; sales period; product; and payor contact details. These are owner-account records, not by themselves a legal-duty finding.

If the names differ, ask: “For this property, product and sales period, which dated arrangement or payor communication explains who distributes proceeds?” A difference is not proof that a public entry or private statement is wrong. A matching name does not prove that the well is yours, your decimal is correct, or a payment amount is due.

Do not publish private account numbers, tax identifiers, statements or division orders while researching the difference. Share only records you are authorized to share through a verified channel. A disputed duty, title question, lease term or amount requires qualified Texas legal, land or accounting review, not an inference from either displayed name.

### Does the P-4 Purchaser field tell me who must pay my royalty?

No. The [RRC's P-4 description](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/about-oil-gas-data-queries/) identifies a designated first buyer, while [§91.401(2)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.91.htm) defines the actual first-purchaser payor default and stated arrangement exception. The P-4 result alone cannot decide your account.

### Can the first purchaser also be the royalty payor?

Yes. Under [§91.401(2)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.91.htm), the actual first purchaser is the default payor. The statute also allows the specifically described arrangement that makes the owner of the right to produce the payor.

### Why might the purchaser and my check payor names differ?

They may be describing different roles, products or periods; a statutory arrangement may also be relevant. The names alone cannot identify the applicable explanation or establish that anyone failed to pay. Reconcile the exact property and dated records before drawing a conclusion.

### What should I compare before asking about a payor difference?

Keep the public P-4 source, date, product and well/lease identifier. Separately keep your statement or check detail, division order and any payor communication with the property and sales period. The [royalties pillar](/learning-center/oil-and-gas-royalties/) explains the wider payment-record path without requiring a form or booking.

### Can the RRC decide my private royalty-payor dispute?

No. The [RRC's Royalties FAQ](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/royalties-faq/) says lease and royalty-payment matters are outside its authority. If a specific company duty or amount is disputed, seek qualified Texas legal or accounting review of the controlling records.

**Bottom line:** The P-4 `Purchaser` is the **designated** first buyer for a product. Texas law treats the actual first purchaser as the default payor, subject to its stated arrangement exception. Keep the public designation separate from the private proceeds-distribution evidence until a qualified review connects the property, product, period and governing records.
