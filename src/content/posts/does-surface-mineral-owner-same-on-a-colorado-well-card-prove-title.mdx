**No.** A Colorado Energy and Carbon Management Commission (ECMC) COGIS Well Scout Card can show `Surface Mineral Owner Same: Yes` or `No`, but that display does not establish **your** mineral title, private lease coverage or royalty entitlement. The field appears in a wellbore-permit section. The public card does not explain how its Yes/No value was determined or supply a person-specific deed chain. Save the exact card and API, then compare the relevant recorded and private instruments separately before reaching a conclusion about your own interest. [ECMC Well Scout Card example](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12319839)

This is an informational public-record reading method, not an assessment of anyone's land, well, title, lease, payment or legal rights. A qualified Colorado land or title professional may need to evaluate disputed connections. Do not upload an unredacted deed, lease or payment statement to an untrusted site. MRX may have a buyer interest in a later transaction and would disclose it before an agreement; no contact is required to use the research steps below.

## Why `FEE/FEE` does not answer the separate Yes/No field

One current [ECMC card for API 05-123-19839](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12319839) displays **`Surface Mineral Owner Same: No`** beside **`Mineral Owner: FEE`** and **`Surface Owner: FEE`**. Another [card for API 05-123-31228](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12331228) displays **`Same: Yes`** with the same `FEE/FEE` labels. These two public examples demonstrate a display distinction: identical ownership-category labels do **not** mechanically determine the separate Yes/No field. Neither card is evidence about your property.

Older [ECMC Form 2 instructions](https://ecmc.state.co.us/forms/instructions/Form2_inst.pdf) described mineral and surface entries as ownership *types* and separately asked whether the surface owner was also the mineral owner. This is **historical context**, not proof of the current COGIS field's source, the agency's verification method or today's form language. The displayed cards themselves do not name the reader or explain their Yes/No data lineage. Do not assume that `Yes` proves your name matches a deed, or that `No` excludes your minerals from a well.

The [Texas drilling-permit article](/blog/does-a-texas-rrc-drilling-permit-prove-your-lease-covers-a-well/) addresses a different field and question: an RRC regulatory `Lease` label versus private lease coverage. This Colorado question is specifically about the COGIS owner-same flag and the adjacent category labels. Neither article can decide a particular owner's rights from a regulator screen.

## Preserve this card as one source, not the title file

For the well you are actually researching, record its displayed API number, well name, county, location description, operator, the wellbore-permit portion, the `Same` value, both ownership-category labels and the **date you viewed** the card. Keep its exact URL or a dated copy rather than a search snippet. Note any **status date displayed by ECMC separately**; that is not necessarily the date you viewed the page. The [example card](https://ecmc.state.co.us/cogisdb/Facility/FacilityDetail?api=12319839) shows these areas in one public record, but its well-specific facts are not a template for another well.

Make a separate list of the instruments you rely on: recorded deed, reservation or assignment; relevant lease or amendment; and any probate or transfer paper that needs to be traced. Locate the current search and copy process for the **county of the land**. For **Weld County only**, the [recording FAQs](https://www.weld.gov/Government/Departments/Clerk-and-Recorder/Recording-Department/Recording-FAQs) describe an online index available without charge and image/copy options that may require payment. Weld's [recording guidance](https://www.weld.gov/Government/Departments/Clerk-and-Recorder/Recording-Department/Recording-a-Document) cautions that recording itself does not confer legality or authenticity. Other Colorado counties can have different portals, coverage and fees; an empty online search is not a title conclusion.

Use an exception list rather than forcing a match: `same API, tract connection not established`; `recorded instrument found, chain unresolved`; or `permit flag and private paper need professional comparison`. A public regulator card, county index and private documents answer different questions. A qualified Colorado land or title professional can identify which tract and instrument questions remain open.

Keep the ECMC card and your instrument list side by side. ### If the card says `Same: Yes`, am I the mineral owner?

No person-specific conclusion follows from that field alone. The card does not name you, trace your deed chain or state your private fractional interest. The qualified question is what the relevant documents establish for your tract and time period.

### Why can `Same` say `No` when both category lines say `FEE`?

Current ECMC examples show exactly that combination and also show `Yes` with `FEE/FEE`. Treat the categories and Yes/No field as distinct displayed values. The public cards do not establish how those values were determined or who owns your interest.

### Does `Same: No` mean my minerals are outside the well?

No. The flag alone does not identify your tract, private instrument chain or royalty interest. The relevant wellbore and property connection requires other records and, if disputed, professional interpretation.

### Can I use Weld County's online index for every Colorado county?

No. Weld's cited index and copy details are Weld-specific. Check the current clerk-and-recorder process for the county where the land lies and preserve the complete documents you find.

### What should I save before asking for professional review?

Save the exact COGIS card or URL, API, displayed field values and date viewed, then separately list the instruments supporting your claimed interest. Mark any unmatched tract, party or time-period connection instead of presenting it as established.
