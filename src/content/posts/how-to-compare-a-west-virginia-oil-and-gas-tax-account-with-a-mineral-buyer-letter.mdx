A West Virginia oil-and-gas royalty tax account and a private mineral buyer letter **may describe the same interest when several independent identifiers align**, but neither document, alone or together, proves ownership, title, acreage, a royalty decimal, or value. Compare the county, names, tract description, interest type, well references, and recording leads field by field. Mark missing or conflicting entries rather than treating a similar name or number as a confirmed match. Use the county assessor for a tax-account discrepancy, the county clerk to locate actual recorded instruments, and qualified West Virginia title review for unresolved ownership questions.

This is a document-comparison worksheet, not legal or tax advice, a title opinion, a buyer due-diligence endorsement, a price estimate, or a recommendation to sell. Keep a real tax account and buyer letter private. Do not post account numbers, addresses, or correspondence publicly to use the worksheet.

## Keep the two documents separate

The [West Virginia State Tax Division's oil-and-gas royalty appraisal explanation](https://tax.wv.gov/business/propertytax/OilAndGas/Pages/OilAndGasAppraisals.aspx) concerns property-tax valuation of royalty interests using an income approach and producer-supplied information. Its current public example discusses **Tax Year 2025** and earlier producer data. That is tax-administration context, not proof that a private buyer has accurately identified your interest or offered its present sale value. The Tax Division's [property-tax overview](https://tax.wv.gov/Business/PropertyTax/Pages/PropertyTax.aspx) also explains that property ownership, use, and value are determined as of July 1 for the following tax year. A buyer letter has its own sender, date, assumptions, and purpose. Do not compare a tax year with a letter date as if they were the same type of identifier.

The [title, lease and ownership records pillar](/learning-center/title-lease-ownership/) explains why tax accounts, well listings, offers, and individual deeds can be useful leads without resolving the entire ownership question. Here, the job is narrower: make an honest crosswalk of _what each of two documents actually says_ and route discrepancies to the right source.

## Build a private field-by-field crosswalk

Copy each field exactly as printed. Leave a blank when the document does not supply it; do not fill a tax-account gap with a guess from the buyer letter or the other way around. If an identifier is abbreviated, preserve the abbreviation and source date. Use this table as a comparison aid, not as a form to send to MRX.

| Field to compare                                           | Tax account or notice                      | Private buyer letter                                   | Responsible reading                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Issuer and document date                                   | State or county tax context and tax year   | Buyer's name and letter date                           | Different document roles and dates are not a mismatch by themselves.                        |
| County, district, map, parcel, or property reference       | Copy only what is printed                  | Copy only what is printed                              | A shared county helps route research; it does not establish the same tract.                 |
| Named owner or addressee and mailing address               | Preserve name variants                     | Preserve name variants                                 | A name or address is a clue, not proof that the recipient owns the interest described.      |
| Legal-description wording                                  | Quote the wording if present               | Quote the wording if present                           | Compare literally; an omission or difference needs actual-record review.                    |
| Interest description                                       | Royalty or other classification as printed | Mineral, royalty, leasehold, or unspecified as printed | Different interest types cannot be silently treated as equivalent.                          |
| Operator, payor, well name/number, or API number           | Copy any available reference               | Copy any available reference                           | Useful well-context leads; they do not prove private title or payment entitlement.          |
| Deed, lease, instrument, book/page, or recording reference | Copy any available locator                 | Copy any available locator                             | Locate and read the actual writing; a citation or index hit alone is not its legal effect.  |
| Acres, fraction, or decimal                                | Preserve the printed figure, if any        | Preserve the printed figure, if any                    | Record agreement or conflict only; do not calculate or validate the interest here.          |
| Tax account/ticket and buyer file number                   | Tax-side reference                         | Buyer-side reference                                   | Each may be meaningful to its issuer but is not automatically a shared property identifier. |

The tax-account role follows the [Tax Division's appraisal page](https://tax.wv.gov/business/propertytax/OilAndGas/Pages/OilAndGasAppraisals.aspx). For recorded-writing leads, [West Virginia Code §39-2-2](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/39-2-2/) describes county-clerk general indexes for grantors and grantees of deeds, contracts, leases, and other writings. The index helps locate a document to inspect; it does not pronounce the document valid, complete a chain of title, or establish present ownership. The [West Virginia Secretary of State county-clerk directory](https://sos.wv.gov/west-virginia-county-clerk-directory) is a contact route for the relevant county, not an ownership database.

## Label the result without declaring a match

Use a status for each field, not a single yes/no answer for the entire interest:

- **Exact alignment:** the available identifiers are substantially identical as printed. This still does not confirm the same interest or title.
- **Consistent but incomplete:** the available entries do not conflict, but important identifiers are absent.
- **Partial alignment:** some entries agree while a material field is missing or differs.
- **Conflict:** two comparable entries materially disagree; preserve both versions.
- **One-sided:** a field appears in only one document.
- **Not comparable:** the fields have different purposes, such as tax year versus buyer-letter date.
- **Locator lead only:** a reference may help find a recorded writing or well record but proves no private right.
- **Unresolved:** the documents do not support a responsible classification.

Do not turn even “exact alignment” into “same interest confirmed.” A buyer letter may omit interests, use shorthand, or describe a different right; this worksheet does not assess the buyer's legitimacy, diligence, or accuracy. If the same numeric acreage or decimal appears in both places, log the agreement but do not validate the number or calculate proceeds from it.

## Route a discrepancy to the right source

If the tax account's name, address, or legal description looks wrong, the [Tax Division directs those corrections to the appropriate county assessor](https://tax.wv.gov/business/propertytax/OilAndGas/Pages/OilAndGasAppraisals.aspx). Use its [assessor directory](https://tax.wv.gov/Business/PropertyTax/Pages/PropertyTaxCountyAssessors.aspx) for the county on the account. Describe the specific printed discrepancy and ask which tax-record source the office used; do not assume a correction request resolves title.

If the buyer letter cites a deed, lease, reservation, or book/page number, identify the [county clerk](https://sos.wv.gov/west-virginia-county-clerk-directory) for the county where the property is described and ask how to find or obtain the underlying writing. Use the parties' name variants and any recording reference as locator leads under [§39-2-2](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/39-2-2/). Inspect the actual writing rather than treating an index entry as a title opinion. County-specific access and copy availability must be checked with that office.

If both documents mention a well or API number, the [West Virginia DEP well search](https://dep.wv.gov/SearchDEP/Pages/Oil-and-Gas-Well-Search.aspx) can provide well context. DEP says the underlying oil-and-gas data are reported by operators and that it does not guarantee [their accuracy, precision, or completeness](https://dep.wv.gov/oil-and-gas/databaseinfo/Pages/default.aspx). Its search is not a private ownership, acreage, royalty-entitlement, or payment record. Keep a well-result lead separate from an instrument or tax-account conclusion.

For an unresolved tract, interest type, fraction, or recorded-writing question, ask a qualified West Virginia title professional or attorney to examine the actual documents. For a tax assessment or appeal question, contact the appropriate tax authority promptly and consult its current instructions; this article gives no generalized appeal deadline. The [Tax Division's property-tax overview](https://tax.wv.gov/Business/PropertyTax/Pages/PropertyTax.aspx) and oil-and-gas appraisal page describe distinct, time-sensitive tax processes that should not be inferred from a buyer letter.

## A useful next step

Keep the crosswalk and copies of the two original documents together, with each source and date labeled. Ask the assessor or clerk only the question within that office's record role, and obtain qualified title review before relying on an ownership conclusion.
