> **No-submit and privacy boundary.** This page inventories labels visible on one Howard CAD request page. It does not submit or prepare a request for delivery; collect or display a person’s name, phone, address, email, signature, or property identifier; decide what Howard CAD holds; determine whether material is public, responsive, complete, confidential, or exempt; predict cost or timing; or provide a property, title, appraisal, tax, transaction, or legal conclusion. The official [Howard CAD Open Records Request Form](https://howardcad.org/open-records-request-form/) page displays a set of requestor, scope, delivery, and signature fields. For a mineral-record research workflow, the useful first step is not to fill those fields with personal information. It is to inventory what each field asks for, preserve the page identity, and stop before submission.

On 2026-08-29, the visible page showed date, phone, name, mailing address, email, `Property Address and/or Property ID#`, a detailed-description field, three delivery choices, pickup timing, requestor signature, and a final date field. The page says an asterisk marks required fields. Its anti-spam validation field belongs to the page’s technical layer, not to the requested-record description.

## Inventory the visible fields by function

The table preserves the observed labels while separating their functions. A classification is a workflow control for this article, not a legal determination about the field or the information entered into it.

| Visible field or control | Working classification | Boundary |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- | -------- |
| Date | Request timing | Records a date on the request surface; it does not establish receipt or a response deadline. |
| Phone | Personal contact data | Do not copy a real number into an article, review packet, screenshot, or test fixture. |
| Name | Personal identity data | Keep real requestor identity outside the public content workflow. |
| Mailing Address | Personal contact data | Do not use a real address in examples or captured evidence. |
| Email | Personal contact data | Do not use a real email address in examples or captured evidence. |
| Property Address and/or Property ID# | Property-related scope locator | A label does not prove that a property or mineral record exists or is held by the district. |
| Detailed description of the records or seeking (be specific) | Requested-record scope | Preserve the page’s visible wording; do not silently rewrite it as a district statement with a different meaning. |
| Email / US Mail / Will Pick Up | Delivery preference | The displayed choices do not promise availability, format, timing, completeness, or cost. |
| Pick Up Date | Pickup logistics | A blank field does not establish an agreed pickup date. |
| Requestor’s Signature | Signature data | Never place a real signature in public article evidence. |
| Final Date | Signature or completion timing | Record the observed label only; do not infer who must complete it or when. |
| Anti-spam validation | Technical page control | Exclude it from the mineral-record field inventory and request description. |

This functional separation makes the article easier to quote accurately: personal data fields are not confused with the records sought, and delivery choices are not confused with an agency response.

That context helps define a careful records-description workflow. ## Keep delivery labels separate from response claims

The observed delivery choices are **Email**, **US Mail**, and **Will Pick Up**. Those labels indicate choices displayed on the request surface. They do not establish that a requested record can be delivered by every method, that a preferred method will be used, that a record will arrive by a particular date, or that delivery is free.

Likewise, `Pick Up Date` is a visible field label, not evidence that a date has been scheduled. Preserve the label, then wait for a separate authoritative response before describing any confirmed delivery arrangement.
