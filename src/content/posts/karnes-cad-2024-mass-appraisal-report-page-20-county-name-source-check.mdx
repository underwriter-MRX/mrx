## Separate provenance from validity

HTTP 200, official-site hosting, the complete-file hash, and a matching page render establish that the reviewed bytes came from the recorded publisher route and contained the observed text. They do not establish:

- that the “Bee County” phrase was intended;
- that “Karnes County” is the proper replacement;
- that a particular cost schedule or location modifier applies in either county;
- that the report controls a named property, account, mineral interest, appraisal, or tax;
- or that the wording remains unchanged in a later source state.

## Seek clarification before relying on local applicability

The [Texas Comptroller property-tax contact page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/contact.php) says local appraisal and tax questions should be directed to the county appraisal district or tax office and explains that the Comptroller does not hold local property records. That is a routing boundary, not an answer to the wording conflict.

For a document-specific question, preserve the exact file and page, then ask the current publisher whether clarification, a corrected report, or a later source state exists. Treat any response as new evidence with its own date and provenance.

Stop when the task requires deciding what the sentence should say, applying a cost schedule, identifying a property or account, calculating an appraisal or tax, determining ownership or title, estimating mineral-rights value, judging an offer, advising a transaction, or reaching a legal conclusion. Each requires a separate evidence package and, where appropriate, qualified professional review.
