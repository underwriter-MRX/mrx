> **District-plan and personal-deadline boundary.** This page identifies one Karnes CAD plan, two physical pages, and the activity bands printed there. It does not identify a person, property, account, lease, well, or Area Three assignment; establish an individual notice, protest, hearing, certification, inspection, payment, legal, or tax deadline; prove an appraisal change or tax amount; establish ownership, title, acreage, production, royalty, current mineral-rights value, sale value, or offer quality; or recommend a transaction. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The official [Karnes CAD 2025-2026 Reappraisal Plan](https://karnescad.org/Forms/GetDocument?fileName=1715867857_2025-2026+Reappraisal+Plan+with+Resolution.pdf&subPath=Data+Records) gives a bounded answer: **physical page 22 lists the district's 2025 activity bands, and physical page 23 says the same timetable and duties apply in 2026.** Page 23 also separates a year-round protest statement from the dated bands.

Use those pages as a schedule locator, not as a personal calendar. The plan describes district work. It does not say that every owner, account, or mineral property will receive each action in the listed period.

## Crosswalk the page 22 activity bands

Physical page 22 labels six time bands. The following crosswalk paraphrases the activities while keeping each observation attached to its printed band:

| Printed time band | Page 22 activity summary |
| ----------------- | ------------------------ |
| August to December | Plan and begin preparatory-year field inspections; mail the two-year agricultural survey in August; begin residential and commercial building reclassification using the new classification schedule. |
| Mid December 2025 | Begin planning sales-ratio studies for all district areas and gather current sales data from confirmation letters, deed records, and other sources. |
| January to March | Mail listed applications and forms, track returns, complete field inspections, begin sales-ratio reports, compare CAD values with sales information, and identify needed schedule adjustments. |
| March through April | Continue ratio reports; refine sales analysis and mass-appraisal schedules; test schedules; complete data entry and fieldwork; perform required appraisal or maintenance activity; prepare notices; work personal-property renditions; and prepare certified estimates. |
| May through June | Mail Notices of Value, hold informal hearings for owner inquiries and protest questions, and conduct ARB formal hearings. |
| July | Continue ARB work, process changes and orders, notify affected overlapping districts, approve appraisal records, and certify values to taxing units. |

The table is a page locator, not a replacement for the source. Terms such as `Notices of Value`, `ARB`, and `certifies values` remain attached to the district plan. This article does not calculate a deadline or determine which procedure applies to an individual situation.

## Keep the page 23 statements separate

Physical page 23 adds three different observations:

1. An `As needed throughout the year` line addresses resolution of outstanding protests through formal ARB hearings or informal handling when possible.
2. The `2026 Reappraisal Schedule` statement says the same timetable and duties apply in each year.
3. An additional sentence says field-appraiser staff will physically inspect property described as Area Three, while the chief appraiser and KCAD staff continue the same general duties and steps outlined for 2025.

Do not merge the year-round protest line into the six page 22 bands. Do not treat the 2026 statement as proof that a particular action occurred. Do not use the phrase `Area Three` alone to assign a property, owner, mineral account, tract, lease, or well to that area.

## Distinguish a district plan from an individual deadline

The [Texas Comptroller Appraisal District Reappraisal Plan explainer](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/98-1202.pdf) supplies statewide context: appraisal districts implement periodic-reappraisal plans, and boards adopt written plans on a biennial cycle. The current [Texas Tax Code Section 25.18](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.25.htm#25.18) supplies the statutory periodic-reappraisal framework.

Those statewide sources explain why a plan exists. They do not turn the Karnes schedule into a personal notice or deadline. They also do not establish that a named mineral account was inspected, reappraised, protested, heard, approved, certified, or changed.

For a property-specific question, preserve the relevant notice or official account record and verify the current governing procedure. A plan page is useful context, but it is not proof of service, receipt, eligibility, timeliness, or outcome.
