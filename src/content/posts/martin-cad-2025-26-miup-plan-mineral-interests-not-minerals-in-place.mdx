On physical page 20 / printed page 18, the plan distinguishes the property interest being appraised and placed on the tax roll from the oil or gas mineral itself. Preserve that statement as the plan's terminology. Do not turn it into an independent legal, ownership, appraisal, tax, or transaction conclusion.

## Do not turn appraisal terminology into ownership proof

The plan's use of `mineral interest` does not show:

- the name of a current owner;
- a legal description or tract identity;
- a decimal ownership interest;
- whether an interest is leased or unleased;
- executive, bonus, delay-rental, royalty, or surface rights;
- the effect of a deed, probate order, lease, assignment, reservation, or pooling instrument; or
- whether an appraisal record matches the legal title chain.

Those questions require different evidence and, when legal interpretation is needed, a qualified professional. The plan is an appraisal-method source. It is not a title plant, deed index, ownership report, division order, lease abstract, or legal opinion.

The [Texas Comptroller local property-appraisal directory](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/) can route a current appraisal or tax question to the applicable local office. That directory is routing evidence only. It does not determine ownership, interpret the plan, or resolve a transaction question.

## Do not turn the boundary into a sale-price estimate

The interest-versus-mineral wording supplies none of the inputs needed for a reader-specific mineral-rights transaction analysis. It does not state:

- net mineral acres or decimal ownership;
- lease royalty, bonus, term, or deductions;
- producing wells, statements, volumes, prices, or burdens;
- undeveloped locations, spacing, timing, or operator plans;
- title defects, curative work, or suspended proceeds;
- a buyer's underwriting assumptions; or
- an offer, appraisal, tax bill, or closing amount.

A county appraisal plan and a private mineral-rights transaction answer different questions. Keep those evidence lanes separate even if both use terms such as `interest`, `property`, `market value`, or `appraisal` elsewhere.
