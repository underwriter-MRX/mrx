> **Official-plan locator, not an account result or deadline notice.** This article records statements in Midland Central Appraisal District’s 2025-2026 Reappraisal Plan. It does not prove that a scheduled activity occurred, that a date remains operative, that a notice was mailed, that a hearing happened, that an account was certified, or that an owner’s appraisal, title, tax, protest, value, offer, or transaction result is correct. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **the plan separates the valuation date from the work calendar**. Physical page 69 prints **Mineral Property Valuation: January-May**, followed by planned notice, review, hearing, and certification windows. Physical page 102 says the Texas effective date is **January 1** and that mineral interests are reappraised annually. Physical page 103 calls lien-date value a **snapshot in time**.

That sequence is useful for locating the document’s statements. It is not evidence that Midland CAD or its contractor completed every activity on the printed schedule, that an account received a correct notice, or that any date is a current owner deadline.

Those controls bind the retrieved bytes and page positions. They do not prove that the file was never replaced, that a schedule item occurred, that a notice was delivered, that an account was certified, or that a current district instruction matches the planning document.

## The printed calendar on physical page 69

Physical page 69 is headed **Valuation Contractor’s General Annual Work Schedule**. It prints this sequence:

- field inspections and discovery from January through April;
- rendition review and processing from April through May;
- industrial personal valuation from January through May;
- **mineral property valuation from January through May**;
- utility listing and valuation from January through May;
- mail notices in the first week of May;
- informal valuation reviews from May through July;
- a formal ARB hearing in the first week of July; and
- certification of accounts before July 25.

These are the document’s labels and time windows. The page does not turn July 25 into an owner protest deadline, does not identify a particular account, and does not establish that a notice, review, hearing, or certification event occurred.

The page also places the calendar above a residential geographical listing. That neighboring content does not make the quadrant list a mineral-account locator. This article uses only the contractor calendar at the top of the page.

## Why January 1 and January-May are not competing dates

Physical page 102 says that for Texas ad valorem tax work, the effective date is January 1. It also says appraisal and report dates are typically several months later and describes limited circumstances in which later information may confirm a trend or condition that existed and was generally known as of the effective date. The page separately says mineral interests are reappraised annually.

Physical page 103 then describes value at the lien date as a **snapshot in time**. Read together, those pages distinguish the date the value is tied to from the later work used to develop, review, notice, defend, and certify appraisal records.

[Texas Tax Code Chapter 23](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.23.htm) supplies statutory context for appraisal at market value as of January 1. [Texas Tax Code Chapter 25](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.25.htm) supplies periodic-reappraisal and appraisal-roll context. This article does not interpret those chapters, decide how later information should be treated for an account, or give owner-specific legal or tax guidance.

## Source-data categories on physical pages 103 and 105

Physical page 103 says a common set of data characteristics for each mineral property includes historical production, price, and expense data. It names state agencies such as the Railroad Commission of Texas and Texas Comptroller, plus private firms that gather, format, and commercially repackage data.

Physical page 105 says the main source of property data is Railroad Commission data as reported by operators. It describes monthly receipt of updated and new well and production files. The page also names fieldwork, operator financial data, information from chief appraisers and tax assessors, trade publications, local newspapers, public reports of new wells, ownership information from pipeline purchasers or operators, and taxpayer-supplied deeds or correspondence.

That source list is not a completeness certification. It does not show which inputs were used for one account, whether an operator report was later corrected, whether ownership data is current, or whether a deed proves the interest someone thinks it proves. The [Midland CAD Open Records route guide](/blog/midland-cad-open-records-mineral-files-rolls-notices-source-routes/) owns the separate job of locating request channels; this page owns only the source categories printed in the reappraisal plan.

## A safe verification sequence

Use this sequence when the printed calendar matters to a current research question:

1. **Bind the source.** Save the Publications route, PDF URL, retrieval time, file size, SHA-256, and page count.
2. **Use the physical and printed locators.** The contractor appendix restarts its own printed page numbering, so cite both positions.
3. **Label dates as plan text.** Write “the plan prints” rather than “the district completed.”
4. **Keep January 1 separate from the work window.** One is the stated effective date; the other is a planned operating sequence.
5. **Do not relabel July 25.** The page calls it a certification timing line, not an owner protest deadline.
6. **Ask for current instructions.** Use Midland CAD’s current official channel for a present notice, deadline, hearing, or account-status question.
7. **Request account-level evidence separately.** A general plan cannot prove what data, corrections, or review occurred for one mineral interest.
8. **Keep tax appraisal separate from private transaction value.** The plan does not determine a private offer or sale result.
9. **Preserve source limitations.** An official source can still contain planning language, approximations, later-revised information, or facts that require account-level confirmation.
