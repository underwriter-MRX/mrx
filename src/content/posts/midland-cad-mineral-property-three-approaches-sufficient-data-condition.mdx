> **Source statement, not an account result.** This guide inventories a current public Midland Central Appraisal District statement. It does not determine which appraisal approach was used for an account, whether sufficient data existed, whether a contractor performed correctly, or whether an appraisal is accurate. It does not establish ownership, title, taxes, protest results, market or sale value, an offer, production, royalties, or a legal conclusion. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is **no**. The current [Midland CAD Mineral Property page](https://midcad.org/mineral-property/) does not say that every approach applies to every account. It names the **income approach**, **cost approach**, and **sales comparison approach**, then says the contractor considers them when **sufficient data is available**.

That last condition matters. The source supplies a district-wide description of a contractor’s consideration process. It does not reveal which approach was used on a particular account, whether one or more approaches were excluded, what data were available, or how a final figure was produced.

## Direct answer: three labels plus one condition

Read the official statement as four separate observations:

| Source element | What the page supports | What it does not support |
| -------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Income approach | The page names this approach | The approach was used for a particular account |
| Cost approach | The page names this approach | A cost calculation, input, or result for a particular account |
| Sales comparison approach | The page names this approach | A comparable sale, adjustment, or account result |
| Sufficient-data condition | Consideration is conditional on data availability | A finding that sufficient data existed for any named account |

The article’s information gain is the separation between **named approaches** and **account-specific application**. A list of methods is not evidence that all listed methods were run, reconciled, weighted, or accepted for every mineral property.

## Preserve the page’s exact scope

The current page says Midland Central Appraisal District contracts with Pritchard and Abbott Inc. for valuation of mineral, identified parenthetically as oil and gas, real and personal property. That is a **CONTRACTOR-STATEMENT** from the issuer’s page, not independent proof of the agreement’s current terms, scope, performance, or result.

The page’s second sentence uses singular grammar after listing three approaches. This guide preserves the issuer’s three labels and conditional meaning without silently rewriting the source into a broader claim. A clean paraphrase is: the page says the contractor considers the three named approaches when sufficient data is available. That paraphrase does not add a promise that all three apply to every account.

## Treat “sufficient data” as unresolved at the account level

The phrase **when sufficient data is available** is a condition. The page does not define a universal sufficiency threshold, publish an account-level pass or fail result, or identify the data used for a particular property.

A reader therefore should not infer any of the following from the page alone:

- that all three approaches were available or used;
- that one approach controlled the final property value;
- that the inputs were complete, current, comparable, or correctly applied;
- that a contractor reconciled multiple results in a particular way;
- that a listed figure is accurate or suitable for another purpose; or
- that a property-tax appraisal statement supplies a private mineral-sale formula.

If an account-specific method matters, the next evidence must be account-specific and issued through an appropriate official route. This article does not search for an owner or property, reproduce a record, or make that determination.

Those routes create three distinct source lanes:

1. **Current web-page statement.** What the Mineral Property page says generally about the contractor, three approach labels, and sufficient data.
2. **Publication or plan.** What a separately issued report or plan says for its own period, scope, and document identity.
3. **Account or roll record.** What an official record displays for its own account, year, fields, and status.

Do not merge the lanes by assumption. A current page statement does not import every detail from a report. A report does not prove an account result unless it actually identifies and supports that result. A roll or notice does not become title evidence or a private transaction valuation merely because it is official.

## Property-tax appraisal is not private sale valuation

The phrase **final property value** appears in an appraisal-district context. It should not be silently converted into a price at which a mineral owner could sell an interest.

A private transaction can involve different interests, effective dates, title conditions, lease terms, production history, deductions, buyer assumptions, risk adjustments, and contract terms. None of those property-specific or transaction-specific facts is supplied by the Midland CAD page.

Keep these questions separate:

- **Appraisal source question:** What does the district or its contractor say about a property-tax appraisal process or record?
- **Title question:** What instruments and legal chain establish an interest?
- **Tax or protest question:** What notice, deadline, rule, evidence, and procedure apply?
- **Transaction question:** What exact interest, evidence date, assumptions, buyer terms, and agreement are being evaluated?

They do not prove validity or performance.

## Build an account-specific follow-up without assuming the answer

If a reader needs to investigate an individual account, use neutral questions:

1. Which official account, roll, notice, or mineral-file record is the source?
2. What year, status, issuer, retrieval date, page or row locator, byte count, and file hash identify it?
3. Does the record itself identify an approach, input set, contractor action, or calculation?
4. Does the source define what sufficient data meant for that account and period?
5. Are any fields missing, conflicting, preliminary, superseded, or outside the source’s stated scope?
6. Which question requires the appraisal district, a qualified appraiser, a tax professional, or legal counsel?

An unresolved field should remain **unresolved**. Do not replace it with an estimate, a general method description, or a conclusion borrowed from another county or year.
