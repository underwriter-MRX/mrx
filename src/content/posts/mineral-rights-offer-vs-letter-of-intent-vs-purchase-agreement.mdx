> **Educational comparison, not contract interpretation.** This article describes common workflow roles for transaction documents. A title such as “offer,” “letter of intent,” or “purchase agreement” does not decide whether a document or clause is binding, valid, complete, enforceable, or appropriate for a particular owner. MRX does not provide individualized legal, tax, title, accounting, engineering, investment, or financial advice, and an MRX assessment is directional only, not a certified appraisal. Have a qualified attorney in the governing state review the actual documents before you sign or rely on them. MRX may be the disclosed direct buyer in a later transaction; that role is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **an offer usually presents proposed terms, a letter of intent usually records a preliminary deal framework, and a purchase agreement usually contains the definitive transaction obligations.** Those are common workflow roles, not automatic legal-status rules. The document's exact language, incorporated attachments, signatures, conditions, and governing law matter more than its heading.

## The three documents answer different questions

An owner can receive a short email, a one-page offer, an LOI, and a longer purchase agreement that all mention the same transaction. The papers may repeat a price while changing the property description, interest scope, deadlines, diligence rights, title assumptions, adjustment clauses, or termination rules.

Use this comparison to locate those changes before anyone evaluates the transaction:

| Document label                 | Common workflow role                                                                       | Questions to ask before moving forward                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Offer or proposal              | Presents proposed economics and a possible transaction outline.                            | What exactly is being proposed? How is acceptance described? Which terms are missing, conditional, or subject to another document?      |
| Letter of intent or term sheet | Records a more developed preliminary framework and the path to a definitive agreement.     | Which sections say they are binding or nonbinding? Are confidentiality, exclusivity, expenses, access, or deadlines treated separately? |
| Purchase agreement             | States the definitive transaction terms, conditions, allocations, and closing obligations. | What interest is covered? How do title, adjustments, diligence, closing, termination, remedies, and exhibits work together?             |

Do not promote a document from one row to another based only on length, formatting, a signature block, or what the sender calls it. Preserve the exact artifact and let a qualified attorney determine its legal effect.

## An offer is the proposal layer

A mineral rights offer often leads with a headline amount or price method, a tract or owner reference, and a response path. It may be a conversation starter, a proposal capable of acceptance, or something expressly conditioned on diligence and a later agreement. This article does not decide which description applies to a document in hand.

At the offer stage, capture the proposed facts without filling gaps from memory:

- sender-displayed party name and contact reference;
- owner or recipient reference;
- property, county, tract, legal description, lease, well, or interest language actually shown;
- amount, unit basis, payment method, and any stated assumptions;
- effective date and response deadline language;
- acceptance method and signature fields;
- diligence, title, approval, financing, or other conditions;
- referenced attachments or later agreements; and
- version date, received time, and retained source file.

The [offer version-control and deadline register](/blog/mineral-rights-offer-version-control-and-deadline-register/) owns the separate job of preserving every artifact and stated deadline. This article owns the stage-to-stage comparison, not a fairness score or accept-or-reject recommendation.

## A letter of intent is the framework layer

An LOI often sets out the contemplated structure while the parties prepare or negotiate definitive documents. It may describe the proposed interest, consideration, diligence period, exclusivity, confidentiality, expenses, approvals, or a target closing date.

The most important question is not “Is this called an LOI?” It is “What does each section say about obligation and next steps?”

The Supreme Court of Texas decision in [Energy Transfer Partners v. Enterprise Products Partners](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1445666/170862.pdf) reproduces transaction documents that described a term sheet as nonbinding while treating specified matters separately and requiring executed definitive agreements before transaction obligations arose. The decision is useful here as a concrete warning: one document can contain different commitment language for different sections. It is not a template for a mineral sale and does not decide another document's effect.

Build an LOI section map with four neutral states:

1. `expressly described as binding`;
2. `expressly described as nonbinding`;
3. `conditioned on a later definitive agreement`; or
4. `unclear - legal review required`.

Do not infer the state from the section topic. Confidentiality, access, exclusivity, expenses, governing law, or termination can be handled differently from the proposed transaction terms. Record what the document says and where it says it.

## A purchase agreement is the definitive-contract layer

A purchase agreement usually carries the detailed transaction rules: the interest to be sold, consideration, effective date, title review, due diligence, adjustments, closing conditions, representations, termination, remedies, and the documents to be delivered at closing.

That does not mean every purchase agreement has the same provisions or that every signed paper is complete and enforceable. The Supreme Court of Texas decision in [Chalker Energy Partners III v. Le Norman Operating](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1445957/180352.pdf) examined whether communications formed a contract when the parties' process required execution and delivery of a definitive agreement. The court's analysis reinforces a narrow operational lesson: preserve the stated formation and execution conditions instead of assuming that negotiations, emails, or a headline agreement completed the deal. The opinion does not interpret an owner's mineral-sale documents.

For Texas examples, [Business and Commerce Code Section 26.01](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.26.htm#26.01) requires specified agreements, including a contract for the sale of real estate, to be in writing and signed by the person to be charged or an authorized signer. [Property Code Section 5.021](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.5.htm#5.021) separately addresses written, subscribed, and delivered instruments for conveyance of a freehold estate. Those statutes show why contract formation and the conveyance instrument should remain separate review questions. They do not supply a nationwide answer or establish that a particular mineral document is sufficient.

For a deeper clause inventory, use [What Is a Mineral Rights Purchase Agreement and What Should It Include?](/blog/what-is-a-mineral-rights-purchase-agreement-and-what-should-it-include/). Keep this comparison focused on how the document stages connect.

## Keep one cross-document comparison sheet

Create one row for each field and one column for each exact document version. Never paste a conclusion into a fact cell.

| Comparison field      | What to preserve                                                                                                                   | What not to assume                                  |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Parties               | Exact displayed legal names, roles, and signature capacity.                                                                        | Identity, authority, or legitimacy.                 |
| Property and interest | County, tract, legal description, lease, well, depth, formation, royalty, mineral, executive-right, or other exact scope language. | Ownership, net acreage, or completeness.            |
| Economics             | Amount, per-unit basis, payment timing, deductions, adjustments, and assumptions.                                                  | Fairness, value, or guaranteed proceeds.            |
| Effective date        | Exact stated date and any definition or cross-reference.                                                                           | Who owns a payment or obligation.                   |
| Diligence             | Access, document requests, inspection period, approval conditions, and responsible party.                                          | That silence means no diligence right.              |
| Title                 | Title standard, defect notice, cure, exclusion, adjustment, and termination language.                                              | Good title or a correct ownership fraction.         |
| Conditions            | Approvals, financing, document delivery, and other stated conditions.                                                              | That signing alone completes every condition.       |
| Termination           | Expiration, withdrawal, termination, survival, and notice provisions.                                                              | Enforceability or the current deadline.             |
| Closing               | Closing date or method, payment route, deed or conveyance, tax forms, and other deliverables.                                      | That the agreement itself performed the conveyance. |
| Incorporated material | Exhibits, schedules, definitions, portals, later amendments, and referenced documents.                                             | That an omitted attachment is harmless.             |

Use `not stated`, `not attached`, `different`, or `unclear - review required` when that is what the evidence supports. Do not silently copy a clearer term from the purchase agreement backward into the offer or LOI.

## Watch for terms that change between stages

A repeated headline amount can make three documents look consistent while other terms move. Compare each version for changes in:

- the exact interest or fraction described;
- whether royalty, mineral, nonparticipating royalty, executive, surface, or leasehold language appears;
- included or excluded tracts, depths, formations, wells, or counties;
- the unit used for price or acreage;
- effective-date and payment-allocation language;
- title-defect, adjustment, cure, and exclusion provisions;
- diligence access and document-production duties;
- response, expiration, execution, and closing dates;
- assignment rights and the identity of the closing party;
- representations, warranties, indemnities, releases, or survival clauses; and
- which exhibits are complete, blank, or missing.

A changed field is not automatically better or worse. It is a prompt for a tract-specific factual, title, valuation, tax, or legal question.

## Do not confuse the agreement with the closing conveyance

The purchase agreement may require the parties to deliver a mineral deed, royalty deed, assignment, affidavit, tax form, settlement statement, or another closing document. The agreement and conveyance should be tracked as separate artifacts even when they arrive in one packet.

Ask:

- Which document states the promise to close?
- Which document is intended to convey the interest?
- Does the agreement attach the exact proposed conveyance form?
- Are property and interest descriptions consistent across both documents?
- Which closing conditions remain open?
- Who is responsible for recording, and what evidence of recording is expected?

Do not answer those questions from a blank template or a different transaction. Use the complete packet for the owner and property at issue.

## A safe handoff package

Before seeking legal or professional review, organize:

1. every offer, LOI, term sheet, purchase agreement, amendment, exhibit, and closing form received;
2. the original filename, received time, delivery channel, and version relationship for each artifact;
3. a field-by-field comparison sheet that quotes or locates terms without interpreting them;
4. property and ownership records used by the sender, kept separate from unverified assumptions;
5. a list of missing attachments, inconsistent descriptions, and open questions; and
6. the stated deadline text with its source location and displayed timezone.

Private names, signatures, account numbers, legal descriptions, prices, and correspondence belong in an access-controlled review system, not a public article or broadly shared screenshot.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a signed mineral rights offer the same as a purchase agreement?

Not necessarily. The label and signature do not answer the question alone. Review the complete language, attachments, acceptance method, conditions, termination terms, signatures, and governing law with a qualified attorney.

### Is a letter of intent always nonbinding?

No. An LOI can describe most proposed transaction terms as nonbinding while treating selected provisions differently. Another LOI may use a different structure. Map the exact language section by section.

### Can the purchase amount change after title review?

It depends on the agreement. Preserve any title-defect, exclusion, adjustment, cure, or termination clause and have the actual language reviewed. Do not assume the headline amount is either fixed or adjustable.

### Which facts should stay consistent across the documents?

Compare parties, property and interest scope, economics, effective date, deadlines, diligence, title assumptions, adjustments, conditions, termination, exhibits, signature status, and version date. Record differences without deciding their effect.

### Does the purchase agreement transfer the mineral interest by itself?

Do not assume that it does. A purchase agreement may establish obligations for a later closing while a deed or other instrument performs the conveyance. The documents and governing state law require qualified review.
