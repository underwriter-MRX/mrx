## MineralRightsXchange Valuation Process vs. Traditional Mineral Rights Buyers

> **Education-only boundary.** Mineral Rights Xchange provides educational information and resources only and does not buy mineral rights. MRX material is not a certified appraisal, legal opinion, tax opinion, title opinion, accounting advice, investment advice, or a promise of value, an offer, payment, sale, or closing.

## Compare educational review with a third-party buyer's offer

MRX educational material can help an owner organize records, assumptions, and written terms before comparing a third-party offer. The buyer's offer remains a separate commercial document. MRX does not make a purchase offer or participate as the buyer.

### Key takeaways

- Keep the property and interest description consistent across every document.
- Separate source records, assumptions, an educational range, and a buyer's written offer.
- Compare the full written terms, not only a headline amount.
- Direct property-specific legal, title, tax, accounting, appraisal, engineering, and financial questions to appropriately qualified professionals.
- Education-only status does not make MRX an independent appraiser, fiduciary, attorney, tax adviser, broker, or other licensed professional.

## What an MRX educational review can organize

An educational review may organize owner-provided records, public-source context, production history, royalty information, assumptions, and questions about a written third-party offer. It can show which inputs were used and which facts remain unverified. It does not determine ownership, certify value, guarantee accuracy, or decide what an owner should do.

## What a third-party buyer controls

A third-party buyer controls its own diligence, pricing model, written offer, conditions, deadlines, funding, purchase agreement, and closing process. Those items should be verified from the buyer's current written documents and qualified transaction-specific review. MRX educational material does not verify a buyer's authority, funding, fairness, or performance.

## Review the two lanes separately

| Review point | MRX education-only lane | Third-party buyer lane |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Purpose | Organize information, assumptions, and questions | Evaluate a potential purchase under the buyer's process |
| Output | Educational explanation or directional context | The buyer's written offer and transaction documents |
| Ownership and title | Not determined | Subject to the buyer's diligence and qualified title or legal review |
| Value | Not a certified appraisal or guaranteed result | Reflected only in the buyer's current written terms |
| Legal and tax effect | Not determined | Requires review of the actual documents and owner-specific facts |
| Decision | Remains with the owner | The owner may accept, reject, negotiate, or seek other information |

## Questions to ask about a third-party offer

1. Does the offer identify the exact interest, property, depth, acreage, and royalty scope?
2. Which amount is fixed, estimated, adjustable, or conditional?
3. What deadlines, exclusivity terms, extensions, deductions, or title-curative provisions apply?
4. Who is the legal buyer, and how should authority and funding be verified?
5. What documents control signing, payment, deed delivery, recording, and closing?
6. Which issues need an attorney, CPA, title professional, appraiser, engineer, or other qualified professional?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does MRX buy mineral rights?

No. Mineral Rights Xchange provides educational information and resources only and does not buy mineral rights.

### Is an MRX educational review an independent appraisal?

No. Education-only status does not establish independent-appraiser, fiduciary, legal, tax, accounting, brokerage, or other licensed-professional status. A formal or regulated purpose may require an appropriately qualified professional.

### Can MRX explain a third-party offer without requiring a sale?

MRX educational material can help organize the offer's visible terms and questions. It does not obligate the owner to sell and does not verify the buyer, interpret the agreement, certify value, or recommend a decision.

### What should an owner compare beyond the headline amount?

Compare the exact property scope, adjustments, conditions, deadlines, exclusivity, title requirements, payment sequence, deed language, recording process, costs, and expected net stated in the current documents.

### Who should answer property-specific legal or tax questions?

Use appropriately qualified professionals who can review the owner's facts and controlling documents. General educational content is not a substitute for that work.

## The bottom line

Keep MRX education separate from any third-party purchase process. Use the educational material to organize evidence and questions, then evaluate the buyer's written terms and obtain qualified property-specific help where needed.
