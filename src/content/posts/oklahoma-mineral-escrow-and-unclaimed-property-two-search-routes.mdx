> **Search leads, not title or payment findings.** This guide organizes two official Oklahoma searches. It does not establish that a payment exists, who owns a mineral interest, an heir's authority, a payable amount, where a particular payment is held, or a right to claim it. MRX's educational material is not legal, title, tax, accounting, appraisal, investment, or financial advice. MRX may have an economic interest as a buyer in a later transaction; that role is disclosed before an agreement is signed. Do not put identity documents, estate papers, account numbers, claim numbers, or bank details into a public worksheet or an unverified site.

If you suspect an old Oklahoma pooling bonus or royalty was not received, check **the Oklahoma Corporation Commission (OCC) MOEA owner search for pooling-related records** and **the Oklahoma State Treasurer's unclaimed-property search for possible reported property**. Record what each official route actually displays, the name variant and date used, and the next official checkpoint. The two searches are not mutually exclusive stages or two guaranteed accounts: a matter may appear in more than one workflow, and neither public result proves entitlement.

## Choose a route based on the clue you have

| Clue or question                                                                      | Official route to inspect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | What a result would mean                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A pooling order, operator name, or an OCC order number appears in an old record       | Use the **Escrow Account Owner Search** linked from the [OCC Mineral Owners Escrow Account page](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/oil-gas/mineral-owners-escrow-account.html) and record only the fields the official interface actually displays. | A displayed record is a potential pooling-related lead. Copy only fields the page actually shows. It is not a title or ownership decision.                          |
| You have an owner, estate, trust, or business name but no confirmed pooling reference | Start at the [State Treasurer's Unclaimed Property page](https://oklahoma.gov/treasurer/unclaimed-property.html) and use the official search linked there.                                                                                               | A possible name match is a lead for the Treasurer's separate claim process, not proof that the record concerns a mineral payment or that the searcher can claim it. |
| A prior result points from one route to the other                                     | Search both routes and preserve separate observations under the same controlled matter ID.                                                                                                                                                               | Do not add two displayed entries into a supposed total or assume money moved in a fixed order. Ask the relevant official agency how the references relate.          |

The [OCC pooling explanation](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/divisions/admin-court-services/court-services/pooling.html) says pooling orders commonly include escrow provisions for owners with unknown addresses or title problems. OCC's [current-rules index](https://www.oklahoma.gov/occ/rules/current-rules.html) links Chapter 10, effective August 1, 2026; its pooled-monies provisions describe escrow in relation to a Commission pooling order and unknown or unlocated persons. OCC labels that hosted rule copy **unofficial** and says the Oklahoma Secretary of State's official version controls. These sources do **not** imply every unpaid royalty or inherited mineral interest is in MOEA.

The [Treasurer's program page](https://oklahoma.gov/treasurer/unclaimed-property.html) provides a broader unclaimed-property name search and an official route to start a claim. It does not identify the reason for any particular missed mineral payment. The OCC page also lists a separate **$20 database CD**; this guide uses the online search, not that paid product.

These are not automatically two different payments. In the current [Oklahoma Title 52 statutes, §§554 and 556](https://www.oklegislature.gov/OK_Statutes/CompleteTitles/os52.pdf), the Commission transmits funds it receives under the pooled-owner law to the Treasurer, and the Treasurer later transfers qualifying older money to the Unclaimed Property Fund. That statutory relationship explains why a researcher should keep cross-route references linked. It does **not** tell us whether either public screen shows a particular person's payment today, which agency has custody of it, or whether that person is entitled to claim it.

## Prepare the names without publishing private records

From records you are authorized to use, list exact variants for the prior owner, estate, trust, or business. Keep the actual names in a controlled file. A public template should show only neutral attempt IDs and whether the route was checked. If you have a pooling order or operator record, preserve its original spelling and number privately; do not invent a property link from a similar name.

On each official site, record the starting agency URL, the final search URL, date/time with timezone, exact variant **reference**, and which filters you actually used. Do not search through a paid locator or a link in an unsolicited message. Do not enter tax identifiers, banking details, account credentials, or identity documents into a search box that asks only for a name or public order reference.

## Keep one two-route decision register

The answer object for this guide is one matter register with **a separate attempt record for each route and exact name variant**, not a single combined match count. Duplicate the blank template when you test another variant:

| Field                                             | OCC MOEA attempt                                           | Treasurer attempt                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Neutral matter and attempt ID                     | Blank                                                      | Blank                                                 |
| Official start and final search URL               | Blank                                                      | Blank                                                 |
| Access date, time, timezone                       | Blank                                                      | Blank                                                 |
| Controlled reference to exact name variant tested | Blank                                                      | Blank                                                 |
| Order or operator filter, only if used            | Blank or `not used`                                        | `not applicable` unless the portal actually offers it |
| Displayed apparent name and reference             | Controlled reference or `not displayed`                    | Controlled reference or `not displayed`               |
| Displayed property type, holder, or location      | Exact shown value or `not displayed`                       | Exact shown value or `not displayed`                  |
| Bounded result                                    | `potential record`, `no result displayed`, or `unverified` | Same three labels                                     |
| Evidence pointer                                  | Controlled screenshot/print reference, if permitted        | Controlled screenshot/print reference, if permitted   |
| Claim/status checkpoint                           | `not initiated` or controlled official status              | `not initiated` or controlled official status         |
| Next official question and responsible person     | Blank                                                      | Blank                                                 |

Use `potential record` only when the official route displays a record worth checking. Use `no result displayed` for one successfully completed search using the specified variant; it does **not** mean no payment exists. Use `unverified` if the site fails, the destination is uncertain, or the result cannot be preserved accurately. Do not copy a full real person's search history into the public template.

## Route a possible record without deciding the claim

For an OCC MOEA lead, preserve the displayed order/operator reference if present, then use the official OCC program resources or contact channel to ask what the record represents. Under [Oklahoma Title 52 §554(C)](https://www.oklegislature.gov/OK_Statutes/CompleteTitles/os52.pdf), claims against the Mineral Owner's Fund are submitted to the State Treasurer; confirm current instructions on the [official Treasurer page](https://oklahoma.gov/treasurer/unclaimed-property.html) before starting. For a Treasurer search lead, follow that page's official claim link and record only the administrative checkpoint in the shared register. The claimant, not this article or a public match, must use the agency's current instructions for any identity or ownership evidence.

Do not label either route `owned`, `owed`, `approved`, `payable`, or `title proved`. Do not infer a second pot of money from two similar-looking records. If the sources conflict, keep the link unresolved and ask the relevant agency or a qualified Oklahoma professional. An ordinary operator suspense or payment-delay question remains a different workflow; the [royalty overview](/learning-center/oil-and-gas-royalties/) and [inherited-rights guide](/inherited-mineral-rights/) explain the adjacent records without converting this search into an accounting or title opinion.

## Time-sensitive law note for a 2026 release

As of September 23, 2026, Oklahoma [HB 1371's enrolled final text](https://www.oklegislature.gov/cf_pdf/2025-26%20ENR/hB/HB1371%20ENR.PDF) is approved but states an **effective date of November 1, 2026**. It describes a future Treasurer-administered account for certain unpaid proceeds that is separate from the pooled-owner escrow addressed under Title 52 §552. That new account is not described here as operational today, visible in MOEA, or searchable by the present public interfaces. If you read this after the effective date, check the current official agency instructions; this dated guide cannot determine a particular payment's location or anyone's rights.

## Five questions to resolve before acting

### Should I check both MOEA and the Treasurer?

If a pooling clue exists, check MOEA and also consider the Treasurer's official route. Keep the observations separate but linked by a controlled matter ID. Neither is an exhaustive inventory of all unpaid proceeds, and they are not guaranteed sequential stages.

### Does an OCC order or operator result prove I inherited the interest?

No. It is a possible research lead. A public search cannot establish the chain of title, heirship, identity, or authority to claim. Keep estate and title questions for a qualified reviewer and the agency's authenticated process.

### What if one route has a result and the other does not?

Record each attempt's exact variant, filters, date, result, and evidence. Do not conclude that one agency is wrong or that two results represent different payments. Ask an official contact how the references relate.

### What if neither search displays a result?

Record `no result displayed` for each completed attempt, not `no funds`. A different spelling, reporting period, record type, holder, or route may matter; any next search should have a reason and a separately recorded attempt.

### Should I upload estate or bank documents to a public worksheet?

No. Keep the public register blank and the actual evidence in a controlled location. Provide sensitive documents only through an authenticated official claim process or to a verified qualified professional when needed.
