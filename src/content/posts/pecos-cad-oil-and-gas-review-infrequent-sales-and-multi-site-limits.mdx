> **Official-plan review locator, not a value result.** This article records what physical pages 44-45 of Pecos County Appraisal District's 2025-2026 Reappraisal Plan say about reviewing oil-and-gas appraisals. It does not determine a current account value, tax, protest position, ownership, title, interest decimal, private mineral value, offer, or transaction result. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **the Pecos plan describes several review signals, but it also documents why sales evidence can be hard to isolate**. It says annual review compares income indicators, year-to-year changes, and compliance with Texas Tax Code Section 23.175. It calls appraisal-to-sales ratios the preferred performance measure, then says sales are very infrequent, conditions often are not public, and transactions normally cover multiple sites while mixing different property types.

That is a review-method statement, not a Pecos market update and not a private-market comparable sale.

## Direct answer: what the plan uses and what limits it

| Review input              | What physical pages 44-45 say                                                                   | What an owner should not infer                                                            |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Income indicators         | Annual review compares property values with industry-accepted income indicators.                | That one indicator establishes a current account or private value.                        |
| Year-to-year change       | Annual review considers changes from one year to the next.                                      | That a change is automatically correct, incorrect, or transferable to another property.   |
| Section 23.175            | Review includes compliance with the Texas Tax Code provision identified by the plan.            | That this article has audited a model, calculation, or current account.                   |
| Experienced review        | Reassignment among appraisers or review by an experienced appraiser contributes to the process. | That a particular property received a second review or a particular conclusion.           |
| Appraisal-to-sales ratios | The plan calls these the preferred method for measuring performance.                            | That a clean, public, single-property comparable exists.                                  |
| Other appraisal evidence  | Valid single-property appraisals submitted for staff review may be compared.                    | That a submitted appraisal is automatically accepted, controlling, or publicly available. |

## The plan's annual review is a combination, not a single test

Physical pages 44-45 describe annual review through three comparisons: income indicators, year-to-year changes, and compliance with Section 23.175. Physical page 45 adds that periodic reassignment among appraisers or review by an experienced appraiser contributes to the process.

The [official Texas Tax Code page for Section 23.175](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?artSec=23.56&chapter=TX.23&code=TX&tab=1) addresses oil-or-gas interests when an appraisal method takes future income from production into account. It specifies a preceding-calendar-year average-price input and statutory adjustment mechanics for that context. This article uses the statute only to identify the provision named by the Pecos plan. It does not rebuild the model, select a discount rate, project production, calculate income, or audit compliance.

The [Texas Comptroller's Valuing Property page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/valuing-property.php) explains that appraisal districts determine taxable-property values and that mass appraisal includes applying and reviewing model results. That general statewide description helps distinguish a review process from a single private transaction. It does not validate the Pecos contractor's methods or establish a property-specific outcome.

## Why “preferred” sales ratios can still have limited evidence

Physical page 45 says appraisal-to-sales ratios are the preferred performance measure. The sentence does not end there. The plan immediately supplies three limits:

1. sales are **very infrequent**;
2. the conditions of sales that do occur are often **not made public**; and
3. market transactions normally cover **multiple sites** and include real and personal property, tangible and intangible.

Those qualifiers matter. A ratio needs a defensible numerator, denominator, property match, and transaction frame. When the consideration covers multiple sites or several asset categories, the total transaction amount does not, by itself, reveal the amount attributable to one oil-and-gas interest. When conditions are nonpublic, assumptions about timing, included assets, liabilities, adjustments, or allocation remain unresolved.

The plan characterizes that analysis as difficult and subjective. This article preserves that limitation instead of manufacturing a comparable sale from incomplete facts.

## A bundled transaction is not a one-property price

Use a four-part separation before treating any transaction as evidence for one account:

| Separation question | Record as known only when supported                                    | Stop condition                                                               |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Site scope          | The exact sites included in the transaction.                           | Stop if the consideration cannot be allocated to the subject site.           |
| Property type       | The real and personal property included.                               | Stop if equipment or other personal property is bundled but unallocated.     |
| Asset character     | The tangible and intangible components.                                | Stop if contracts, rights, or other intangibles are present but unallocated. |
| Sale conditions     | Date, parties, exposure, terms, adjustments, and disclosed conditions. | Stop if material conditions are unavailable or cannot be verified.           |

This worksheet is an evidence-separation aid. It does not turn a private transaction into an appraisal-district comparable, direct an appraiser, or produce a value.

## The plan names another review input without making it controlling

Physical page 45 says performance is also measured by comparison with valid single-property appraisals submitted for staff review. Preserve each qualifier:

- the source says **valid** appraisals;
- it says **single-property** appraisals;
- it says they are **submitted for staff review**; and
- it does not say every submission is accepted or controlling.

Do not infer that a private appraisal exists for a named property, is public, was accepted, or establishes the correct current value. The statement identifies an additional review channel only.

## Local review and state oversight are different layers

The Pecos plan says its mineral appraisal methods and procedures are subject to review by the Property Tax Assistance Division of the Texas Comptroller's office. The [official Comptroller MAP overview](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/index.php) explains that MAP reviews cover appraisal-district governance, taxpayer assistance, operating procedures, and appraisal standards, procedures, and methodology.

The [Comptroller's Property Tax System Basics page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php) separately explains that appraisal districts determine local taxable values and that Comptroller values do not directly affect local values or property taxes. That distinction prevents a state-review statement from being misread as a current Pecos account decision.

This article does not identify a current Pecos CAD MAP report, score, recommendation, compliance determination, or follow-up result. “Subject to review” is not converted into “reviewed and approved.”

## A bounded owner evidence worksheet

If a current Pecos appraisal question matters, keep separate rows for:

1. **Plan identity:** official route, retrieval time, byte size, SHA-256, page count, encryption, and JavaScript status.
2. **Exact plan statement:** physical page, heading, complete sentence, and continuation across pages.
3. **Current account record:** account identifier, property description, appraisal year, notice, and value components.
4. **Income evidence:** production period, price input, expenses, decline or reserve assumptions, risk adjustments, and source dates.
5. **Sale evidence:** site count, included property, asset categories, consideration allocation, and disclosed conditions.
6. **Submitted appraisal:** property match, effective date, appraiser, scope, assumptions, and staff-review status.
7. **Oversight evidence:** exact program, review year, published report, recommendation, and follow-up status, if any.
8. **Private transaction evidence:** title, lease, production, engineering, risk, market, buyer, agreement, and allocation evidence kept outside the tax-appraisal shortcut.

Stop and seek qualified appraisal, legal, tax, or title help when the question requires a professional conclusion, a protest strategy, an ownership determination, or interpretation of a property-specific model.
