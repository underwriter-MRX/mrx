> **Aggregate ratio-study record, not a private mineral appraisal.** This page reports bounded fields from official Texas Comptroller sources. It does not identify an owner, parcel, mineral account, title, acreage, lease, well, production interest, appraisal notice, tax bill, protest result, market value, private mineral-interest value, offer, buyer action, or transaction result. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **the Reeves Appraisal District 2024 ratio-study summary displays 66 in the `G.OIL, GAS, MINERALS` row’s `Number of Ratios` field**. The same aggregate row displays `26,799,785,533` as `2024 CAD Reported Appraisal Value`, `1.00` as median level of appraisal, `0.94` as coefficient of dispersion, `100.00` in both displayed within-median percentage fields, and `1.00` as price-related differential.

Keep every number attached to its label. Sixty-six is the number of ratios, not a parcel count. The reported appraisal value is a district-study field, not a private mineral-interest value.

The official Reeves summary presents one aggregate Category G row across seven data columns:

This is a transcription of the displayed district-summary row. It is not a reconstruction of underlying observations, a property sample, a list of accounts, or an MRX score.

## Start with the official 2024 route

The [Texas Comptroller 2024 Appraisal District Ratio Study findings page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/data/property-tax/ratio-study/2024/) identifies the page as `2024 Tax Year Findings`, says the information was published on August 15, 2025 in compliance with Tax Code Section 5.10, and lists `195 Reeves` among the appraisal districts studied.

That page establishes three source-control facts: the study year, the publication date, and the link relationship to the Reeves summary. It does not supply a property result. Preserve the landing-page URL and the exact Reeves link target instead of citing the district table without its study-set route.

The current [Texas Comptroller Reeves County directory](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/reeves.php) identifies Reeves Appraisal District and displayed a May 7, 2026 update date during the controlled retrieval. The directory supports current district identity and contact routing only. It does not independently validate the historical ratio row or any private property conclusion.

## Preserve the district-summary identity

The linked [195-Reeves 2024 Appraisal District Summary Worksheet](https://comptroller.texas.gov/auto-data/PT2/ratio-study/2024/1950000001A.php) displays:

- study identity: `2024 Appraisal District Ratio Study`;
- worksheet identity: `Appraisal District Summary Worksheet`;
- district: `195-Reeves`; and
- update label: `Study Results Last Updated: Aug. 31, 2025`.

The tax year, publication date, and displayed update date are different fields. Do not silently replace 2024 with 2025, and do not describe the page-update date as the effective date of every underlying appraisal or property fact.

## Sixty-six is the number of ratios, not a parcel count

The table heading says `Number of Ratios **`; the Category G row displays `66` under that heading. This article does not relabel 66 as the number of parcels, owners, mineral accounts, leases, wells, samples, sales, notices, offers, or transactions.

The district page does not publish the underlying 66 observations in this summary. Without those records and a separately authorized analysis, the row cannot support property matching, recomputation, or a claim about what any single observation represents.

The page also carries a general warning that statistical measures may not be reliable when the sample is small. Preserve that warning with the source record. This article does not decide whether the warning applies to the Reeves Category G row or whether 66 is sufficient for another purpose.

## Keep the reported appraisal value inside the study

`26,799,785,533` appears under `2024 CAD Reported Appraisal Value`. That label matters. The number is not presented on this page as:

- a named owner’s mineral-rights value;
- a parcel, account, lease, well, or royalty value;
- a private market appraisal;
- an estimated sale price or offer;
- a tax bill or taxable amount for a named property; or
- a total that this article combines with the two school-district PVS figures.

A district-level field and a private mineral-interest question have different evidence requirements. Before anyone could analyze a particular interest, they would need separately verified ownership, legal description, interest type, decimal, production relationship, burdens, effective date, and purpose. This ratio-study row supplies none of those facts.

## Do not convert 1.00 into a property finding

The Category G row displays `1.00` in both `Median Level of Appraisal` and `Price-Related Differential`. It also displays `0.94` for `Coefficient of Dispersion` and `100.00` in each of the two within-median percentage columns.

The [Texas Comptroller ratio-study overview](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/ratio-study/index.php) says the program measures appraisal uniformity and median levels within major property categories using statistical analysis of data collected through the school-district PVS. It identifies median level, coefficient of dispersion, and other statistical measures as report components.

That program description does not turn a displayed aggregate number into a property conclusion. This article therefore does not call any field a pass, guarantee, accuracy certificate, market signal, sale indicator, tax result, or proof that a named account is correct. It does not recalculate, round, normalize, benchmark, rank, grade, trend, or compare the Reeves fields.
