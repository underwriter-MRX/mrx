> **District-process report, not a mineral valuation.** This article explains the structure and stated results of one official Reeves County Appraisal District MAP report. It does not establish title, ownership, acreage, a mineral account, property classification, appraisal correctness, a ratio result, a tax bill, protest rights, legal effect, market value, a private mineral-interest value, an offer, or a transaction result. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **the Reeves County Appraisal District 2025 final MAP report records PASS on all four mandatory requirements, review-area scores of 100, 92, 100, and 96, and two recommendations at questions 29 and 74**. Read those entries as findings about district processes, not as proof that a mineral account, appraisal, tax result, or private mineral value is correct.

## Direct answer: keep four result types separate

The report places several result types near each other, but they answer different questions. A useful reading record keeps each one in its own field.

| Result field | What the report supplies | What it does not supply |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Mandatory requirements | Four PASS entries | A property-specific certification |
| Review-area ratings | Two `Meets All` and two `Meets` labels | A mineral valuation grade |
| Review-area scores | 100, 92, 100, and 96 | A ratio for a mineral account |
| Recommendation rows | Questions 29 and 74 | A conclusion about ownership, tax, or value |
| Completion boundary | January 2027 on the results page | Proof that an item is already complete |

This separation is the central information gain. It prevents a district-level process review from being reused as evidence for a different owner-level question.

## Start with the MAP program scope

The [Texas Comptroller MAP overview](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/index.php) says Tax Code Section 5.102 requires a review of each appraisal district's governance, taxpayer assistance, operating procedures, and appraisal standards, procedures and methodology at least once every two years. PTAD reviews approximately half of the appraisal districts each year and uses a tier system to determine the number of questions.

The current [MAP review documents page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/review-documents.php) describes the 2024-25 guidelines as aids for reviewers answering instrument questions. It also says the guidelines are not intended as specific instructions to appraisal districts. That matters because a report question is part of a defined review instrument. It is not a universal checklist for deciding a private property dispute.

The [MAP program flyer](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/98-1061.pdf) describes the two-year cycle, four mandatory pass-or-fail questions, tier-based yes-or-no questions, and recommendation follow-up. Use it for program mechanics. Do not use it to claim that a specific Reeves recommendation is complete.

## Locate the final Reeves report and its date boundary

The official [2025 MAP Review Results](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/2025/) page lists Reeves among the appraisal districts whose reviews were completed in 2025. The page also states that districts reviewed in 2025 have until January 2027 to complete recommendations.

That date is a deadline boundary, not a completion receipt. A reader should record `January 2027 deadline stated` and leave `recommendation completion verified` blank unless a separate current source proves completion. The report itself does not provide that later proof.

## Read the four mandatory results as one block

The summary page of the [Reeves County Appraisal District 2025 final MAP report](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/2025/reeves-2025.pdf) records PASS for all four mandatory requirements. The four questions address:

1. administrative functions under Chapter 6 of the Texas Property Tax Code;
2. up-to-date appraisal maps;
3. current implementation of the most recent reappraisal plan; and
4. current appraisal records, established procedures and practices, and reproducible values.

These are district-process findings within the MAP instrument. A mandatory PASS does not identify an owner's account, confirm that a mineral interest appears in the correct name, establish a legal description, validate a category assignment, or approve a property-specific value.

## Keep the four area scores attached to their labels

The report supplies four named review-area scores:

| Review area | Score | Rating shown |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Governance | 100 | Meets All |
| Taxpayer Assistance | 92 | Meets |
| Operating Procedures | 100 | Meets All |
| Appraisal Standards, Procedures and Methodology | 96 | Meets |

Do not detach a number from its review-area label. The score is the report's percentage of Yes answers among applicable questions in that area. It is not a market ratio, a tax rate, an appraisal amount, a confidence score for a mineral record, or a percentage of private mineral value.

## Read question 29 without widening it

Question 29 appears in the Taxpayer Assistance section. The report answers No and recommends following the procedure described in Tax Code Section 11.43(q) when cancelling residence homestead exemptions for individuals who are 65 years of age or older.

Preserve all of those qualifiers. The row concerns one residence-homestead cancellation procedure. It does not say that a mineral account has a homestead exemption, that an owner's exemption was cancelled, or that the row changes a mineral appraisal or private value.

## Read question 74 without turning it into a property conclusion

Question 74 appears in Appraisal Standards, Procedures and Methodology. The report answers No and recommends delivering a notice of imposition of a penalty to a person who fails to timely file a rendition statement as required by Tax Code Section 22.28(a).

That is a notice-process recommendation. The report does not identify a person, rendition, mineral account, penalty amount, property category, appraisal result, or current compliance status. A separate account record and current district evidence would be required before applying the issue to a particular property.
