> **Three date roles, not one universal as-of date.** This page is a source-labeling guide for mixed Texas mineral records. It does not identify an owner, legal description, mineral account, lease, well, decimal interest, appraisal result, tax amount, private value, offer, buyer action, deadline, or transaction outcome. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **record the tax year or January 1 appraisal date, the RRC production month and processed-data status, and the download or retrieval date in separate fields**. They describe different events. Combining them into one date can make an old period look current, make a later download look like a source effective date, or hide the possibility of later corrections.

A retrieval date proves only when the researcher obtained the source. It does not replace the source's stated period, establish that the record was complete on that day, or convert a public record into an owner-specific conclusion.

## The three-date crosswalk

| Date role | What to copy | What it answers | What it does not prove |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Tax year or January 1 appraisal date | The year and effective date shown by the appraisal source | The ad valorem period the record addresses | Private sale value, offer quality, or current account correctness |
| RRC production month and processed-data status | The reported month plus the source's loaded-through or snapshot wording | The production period represented and the processing context visible at retrieval | Finality, ownership, royalty entitlement, or future production |
| Download or retrieval date | The date and time the source was obtained, plus URL or file path | When the researcher captured that source version | The source period, publication date, effective date, or absence of later corrections |

Keep an additional field for the issuer, dataset or report name, exact identifier, and any status label such as preliminary, amended, corrected, or loaded through. The date fields become useful only when the record identity is preserved beside them.

## Tax year and January 1 belong to the appraisal lane

The [Texas Comptroller Valuing Property page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/valuing-property.php) says that, with limited exceptions, appraisal districts appraise taxable property at market value as of January 1. That statement supplies the statewide property-tax context for an appraisal date.

The [Property Tax Data Reports and Surveys page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/reports/) separately explains that Comptroller reports use data and values submitted by appraisal districts. It describes annual or biennial report scopes and, for ADRS, the districts studied in the stated year.

Preserve four labels when using that lane:

1. the tax year shown by the source;
2. the January 1 appraisal date when the source applies it;
3. the report or worksheet publication identity; and
4. the date your family retrieved the page or file.

Those labels should not be collapsed. A report published or downloaded later can still describe an earlier tax year and a January 1 appraisal date. For the separate question of why a tax appraisal and private estimate may differ, use [Why Doesn't My Texas Mineral Tax Value Match a Sale Estimate?](/blog/why-doesnt-my-texas-mineral-tax-value-match-a-sale-estimate/). Article 297 does not repeat that comparison.

## Production month belongs to the RRC reporting lane

The [Texas RRC Oil and Gas Production Data page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/production-data/) describes production information as reported to the Commission by Texas operators and routes users to monthly products and production queries.

The [RRC Production Data Query System FAQ](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/about-us/faqs/oil-gas-faq/production-data-query-system-faqs/) adds the timing boundary. It says production reports are due after the production month, explains an online reporting lag, calls production information a snapshot in time, and says revised, corrected, or delinquent reports can change the data.

That means a record index should preserve both the **production month** and the **processed-data context visible when retrieved**. A family should not relabel a later download as the production month or describe a snapshot as permanently final.

Use [Texas RRC Production by Lease: Retrieval-Provenance Worksheet](/blog/texas-rrc-production-by-lease-retrieval-provenance-worksheet/) for the query-specific route. This article owns only the cross-system date labels, not the RRC query path or result fields.

## Download date is provenance, not the source period

The [RRC Data Sets Available for Download page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/data-sets-available-for-download/) lists different update schedules for different products, including monthly schedules and a production-query dump updated on the last Saturday of each month. Those schedules belong to the named datasets; they are not one universal freshness promise for every RRC page or file.

Recording a download date is still useful. It lets another reviewer know when the source was captured and which version may need a freshness check. But the date is researcher-created provenance. Unless the issuer itself labels that date as a publication, effective, reporting, or revision date, do not assign it one of those roles.

A compact retrieval label can use this structure:

- **Issuer:** agency or district
- **Source:** exact page, dataset, worksheet, or report
- **Identifier:** lease, district, account, file, or route identifier shown
- **Source period:** tax year, production month, or `not shown`
- **Processed-data status:** loaded through, preliminary, corrected, or `not shown`
- **Retrieved:** date, time, and timezone
- **Limitation:** question this record cannot answer

If a source period is absent, write `not shown`. Do not infer it from a browser timestamp, email date, local modified date, or the day the file was downloaded.

## A family packet should keep the lanes separate

When several relatives are gathering records, use one row per source object instead of one row per topic. That prevents an appraisal record, a production record, and a downloaded file from inheriting the same date by proximity.

| Packet field | Example form | Stop condition |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Source owner | Texas Comptroller, Texas RRC, local appraisal district, or another named issuer | Stop if the issuer is unclear |
| Exact source identity | Page title, dataset name, worksheet name, or file name | Stop if two documents share an informal nickname |
| Record identifier | District, lease, account, report, or route identifier shown | Stop if the identifier was copied from another source |
| Source-period date | Tax year, January 1 date, or production month as displayed | Write `not shown` rather than guessing |
| Processing or version label | Preliminary, corrected, amended, loaded through, or not shown | Do not invent finality |
| Retrieval date | When and where the source was captured | Do not substitute it for the source period |
| Question not answered | Ownership, decimal, appraisal correctness, private value, or another explicit limit | Stop before using the record outside its lane |

For the broader transaction control record, use [Understanding Texas Mineral Rights Transactions Today](/blog/understanding-texas-mineral-rights-transactions-today/). For general valuation-input and as-of error analysis, use [Understanding the Mineral Rights Assessment Errors](/blog/understanding-the-mineral-rights-assessment-errors/). Those pages own broader jobs that Article 297 deliberately avoids.

## What these dates do not establish

No single date in this crosswalk proves:

- legal ownership or authority to act;
- the property, lease, well, account, or decimal tied to a person;
- that a public record is complete or error-free;
- that an appraisal is correct or incorrect;
- a tax amount, protest deadline, or protest result;
- a private mineral-interest value or fair offer;
- whether a buyer is qualified or an agreement is suitable; or
- a future production or transaction outcome.

Those questions require their own records, current identifiers, applicable authority, and qualified review. A date label improves provenance; it does not expand what the source proves.

## Review boundary

Before using a dated record for an owner-specific decision, verify the owner, authority, legal description, source identifier, interest type, decimal, applicable period, version, and purpose from the responsible records. If the question concerns title, appraisal, tax, protest, accounting, valuation, or a transaction, use the responsible official office and qualified advisers.
