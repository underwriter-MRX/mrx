> **Official-source classification boundary.** This page compares reporting categories in one current Texas Comptroller guide. It does not classify a person, owner, account, property, parcel, lease, or asset; verify title or ownership; reproduce an appraisal; calculate tax; estimate mineral-rights sale value; compare offers; recommend a transaction; interpret law; or promise an outcome. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction; when that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The current [Texas Comptroller Property Classification Guide](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/docs/96-313.pdf) gives a compact answer: **Category G1 includes oil and gas interests, Category G2 includes minerals other than oil and gas, and Category G3 includes certain subsurface interests that are not defined as minerals plus mines and quarries.** The comparison is on **printed page 20**, which is **PDF page 23** in the 36-page file.

Those labels organize appraisal-roll reporting. They do not, by themselves, tell a mineral owner who owns an interest, what a particular account is appraised for, what tax is due, what the interest could sell for, or whether an offer is reasonable.

## Compare G1, G2, and G3 at a glance

| Official category | Guide description                                                                      | Examples named by the guide                                | Do not infer from the label alone                                                          |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| G1                | Oil and gas interests                                                                  | Oil and gas                                                | Ownership, reserve quantity, production, appraised value, tax, or sale value               |
| G2                | Minerals other than oil and gas                                                        | Uranium, lignite, and other substances defined as minerals | That every subsurface substance is a mineral or that a specific property contains it       |
| G3                | Interests in subsurface land that are not defined as minerals; also mines and quarries | Limestone, sand, caliche, gravel, mines, and quarries      | Mineral ownership, extraction rights, appraised value, tax treatment, or transaction value |

This table is a plain-language index to the official definitions. When a classification affects a real account or dispute, use the current source file, the relevant appraisal-district record, and appropriate professional guidance rather than relying on this summary.

## Keep surface land outside the G1 shortcut

On the same page, the guide tells appraisal districts not to include surface-land value in Category G1. Its ranch example separates the mineral interest associated with discovered oil from the larger surface acreage: the oil-and-gas mineral interest is treated as G1, while the surface land is assigned according to its principal use.

That example supports a classification boundary, not a title conclusion. It does not establish how a particular deed, reservation, lease, depth severance, tract, or ownership fraction should be read. It also does not establish an appraised value, taxable value, market value, or mineral-rights transaction value.

## Distinguish minerals from later handling activities

The guide also gives an example involving oil and storage tanks after production. It says post-production activities in that example do not qualify as G1 and places the oil and tanks in Category L2 personal property after the described field-processing stage.

The useful research lesson is narrow: a record label can depend on what the property is and where an activity sits in the described process. Do not convert that example into a universal operational, tax, or legal rule for a different fact pattern.
