> **Index-search log, not a title opinion.** This page preserves one Texas county real-property index attempt. It does not identify the legally correct person, determine what instrument transfers or reserves an interest, establish that a search is exhaustive, certify a copy, complete a chain of title, or decide current ownership or legal effect. Any later MRX assessment is educational and directional only, not a certified appraisal. This page is not individualized legal, tax, title, accounting, engineering, or investment advice. MRX may be the disclosed direct buyer in a later transaction; that role is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **record ten separate controls for one Texas county grantor-grantee index attempt: (1) county, (2) official route, (3) access time, (4) exact controlled party-name variant, (5) grantor or grantee role, (6) date range, (7) displayed instrument locator, (8) bounded result state, (9) controlled evidence reference, and (10) next administrative step. An index result is a locator lead. It is not proof of identity, ownership, title, completeness, or legal effect.**

That structure follows the index and cross-index functions described in [Texas Local Government Code §193.003](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/SDocs/LOCALGOVERNMENTCODE.pdf) and on PDF page 28, printed page II-6, of the [Texas Judicial Branch County Clerk Manual](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf). It also preserves the historical-index and county-variation cautions published by the [Texas State Library and Archives Commission](https://www.tsl.texas.gov/arc/local/aboutrecords.html).

## The ten fields in one attempt row

| Field | Record exactly | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| County | The county selected on the official source. | Do not substitute an appraisal district, operator address, mailing address, or nearby county. |
| Official route | The county-clerk or other official custodian URL, physical index, microfilm route, or clerk-assisted path actually used. | Do not describe an aggregator or search-engine result as the official route. |
| Access time | The actual timestamp and time zone of the attempt. | Do not use the instrument date or download date as the access time. |
| Exact controlled name variant | A controlled reference to the spelling, punctuation, suffix, estate, trust, or entity form actually tested. | Do not silently normalize, merge, or correct a name. |
| Search role | Grantor or grantee exactly as selected or represented by the index. | Do not infer legal capacity, ownership, or transaction direction from the role label alone. |
| Date range | The exact beginning and ending dates entered or the visible coverage range used. | Do not broaden a successful or failed attempt beyond its recorded dates. |
| Displayed locator | The instrument, file, document, volume/page, or other locator exactly as displayed. | Do not manufacture a locator from result order, a date, or a filename. |
| Bounded result | `locator lead displayed`, `no result displayed for this attempt`, `ambiguous result`, or `source unavailable`. | Do not write `owner confirmed`, `title clear`, or `no record exists`. |
| Evidence reference | A controlled pointer to the retained capture, export, clerk response, or observation note. | Do not place private names, signatures, addresses, or identifiers in a public example. |
| Next administrative step | A neutral next action, such as test a documented variant, change role, refine the date range, contact the clerk, or review the located instrument. | Do not turn the row into legal, title, tax, appraisal, or transaction advice. |

One row represents **one attempt**. If any search input changes, create another row instead of overwriting the first.

## Why grantor and grantee are separate roles

[Local Government Code §193.003](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/SDocs/LOCALGOVERNMENTCODE.pdf) requires the real-property-record index to state the specific location at which an instrument is recorded and to cross-index grantors and grantees alphabetically. The County Clerk Manual explains on PDF page 28 that a two-party instrument such as a deed is indexed by grantor name and by grantee name so it can be located when either party name is known.

The role is therefore part of the search input. It is not a conclusion about the person.

- A **grantor-role attempt** records what the selected source displayed when the controlled name variant was tested as grantor.
- A **grantee-role attempt** records what the selected source displayed when the same or another controlled variant was tested as grantee.
- If an interface uses different labels, preserve its wording and describe the mapping as unresolved unless the source explains it.

Do not merge role results. A name may appear in different roles across different instruments and dates, and a similar name can belong to another person or entity.

## Preserve the exact name variant without publishing private data

A reproducible search needs the exact name form that was tested. Differences can include initials, middle names, suffixes, former names, estate wording, trust wording, punctuation, spacing, or entity endings.

Keep the actual name variant in a controlled record available only to authorized reviewers. A public or shared template can use a neutral reference such as `NAME-VARIANT-01`. The controlled crosswalk can record the exact input without placing a real person's search history in a public article, screenshot, or broad team document.

Run a new row when the name form changes. Do not change `NAME-VARIANT-01` after the fact to make an old result appear to belong to a later spelling.

## Keep the date range and route attached to the result

The [TSLAC Land and Property History guide](https://www.tsl.texas.gov/ref/propertyresearch) explains that real-property records are maintained by the county where the property is located, that some counties provide online access, and that older-record practices differ by county. The TSLAC [About County Records](https://www.tsl.texas.gov/arc/local/aboutrecords.html) page adds that historical cumulative indexes may not be in perfect alphabetical sequence.

Those limits make the route and date range part of the evidence:

1. Record the official route actually opened.
2. Record the visible online coverage or the dates entered.
3. Record whether the source loaded and accepted the input.
4. Preserve the result only for that combination.
5. Use a separate row for a different portal, physical book, microfilm index, role, spelling, or date range.

An unavailable portal does not mean the underlying records are unavailable. A limited online range does not describe the full county record. A historical index that groups names by letter sequence may require a different research approach than a modern exact-name box.

## Use bounded result states

Use result language that says what was observed and stops there.

| Result state | What it means | What it does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Locator lead displayed | The source displayed a result with a locator for this attempt. | It does not confirm identity, ownership, title, completeness, or legal effect. |
| No result displayed for this attempt | The source showed no result for the recorded name variant, role, date range, route, and time. | It does not prove that no instrument exists. |
| Ambiguous result | One or more displayed entries could not be connected safely to the controlled research subject. | It does not authorize choosing the most similar name. |
| Source unavailable | The official route could not be used or verified at the recorded time. | It does not describe the underlying record set. |

Never shorten `no result displayed for this attempt` to `no deed`, `no mineral rights`, or `no ownership`. The longer phrase preserves the evidence boundary.

## The displayed locator starts the next task

When a result displays an instrument number, file number, volume/page, or another record location, transcribe it exactly and preserve the source label. Then stop the index row.

Article 318, [Texas Recorded Mineral Documents: Instrument Number, Volume/Page, and Certified-Copy Status](/blog/texas-recorded-mineral-documents-instrument-number-volume-page-certified-copy-status/), owns the separate control row for an already-found document and its working-copy or certification status. The [recorded mineral deed copy for CPA handoff guide](/blog/how-to-retrieve-recorded-mineral-deed-copy-for-cpa-handoff/) owns retrieval and handoff mechanics. Neither task should be compressed into the index attempt.

The index result can tell an authorized researcher what to inspect next. It cannot tell the researcher what the located instrument legally accomplishes.

## Claim, evidence, and limit register

- **C319-01 · INDEX:** Local Government Code §193.003 requires an alphabetical index to recorded real-property instruments and a specific recording location. This does not establish the completeness of one online portal.
- **C319-02 · CROSS-INDEX:** Section 193.003 and County Clerk Manual PDF page 28 support separate grantor and grantee indexing. The role label does not establish a person's identity, ownership, or legal capacity.
- **C319-03 · DIRECT-REVERSE:** TSLAC describes deed records as indexed by grantor (direct) and grantee (reverse). A county interface may use different visible labels or workflows.
- **C319-04 · HISTORICAL ORDER:** TSLAC cautions that older indexes may not be in perfect alphabetical sequence. This does not explain why a particular attempt failed.
- **C319-05 · COUNTY VARIATION:** TSLAC says property records are maintained by the county, some counties provide online records, and older practices differ. This does not decide the legally correct county for a disputed tract.
- **C319-06 · NEGATIVE RESULT:** `No result displayed for this attempt` is an MRX evidence-control state, not a legal or factual conclusion that no instrument exists.
- **C319-07 · LOCATOR LEAD:** A displayed index result is a lead for locating an instrument. It is not proof of identity, current ownership, title chain, completeness, or legal effect.

## Source-to-conclusion stop table

| The source can support | Stop before saying |
|---|---|
| A county index route was accessed at a recorded time. | This was the only relevant source or a complete statewide search. |
| One exact controlled name variant was tested in one role and date range. | Every spelling, role, date, person, estate, trust, or entity was searched. |
| The source displayed a locator lead. | The displayed party is the intended person or currently owns the interest. |
| The source displayed no result for this attempt. | No recorded instrument exists. |
| A locator was transcribed. | The located instrument is complete, controlling, certified, legally sufficient, or effective. |

## Answer capsule for research handoff

> A Texas grantor-grantee index search log should preserve one attempt at a time: county, official route, access timestamp, exact controlled name variant, grantor or grantee role, date range, displayed locator, bounded result, evidence reference, and next step. Texas law and the County Clerk Manual describe alphabetical grantor/grantee cross-indexing and record-location references. County interfaces and historical indexes vary. A displayed result is a locator lead; no displayed result is limited to that attempt. Neither state proves identity, current ownership, title, completeness, or legal effect.

## Before handing off the search log

1. Is the county and official route recorded?
2. Is the access timestamp complete with time zone?
3. Does one row contain only one exact controlled name variant?
4. Is the role recorded as grantor, grantee, or the source's exact label?
5. Is the date range attached to the result?
6. Is the locator transcribed without interpretation?
7. Is the result one of the four bounded states?
8. Does the evidence reference avoid exposing private data in a public file?
9. Does the next step remain administrative rather than legal or transactional?
10. Does the row avoid any identity, ownership, title, completeness, or legal-effect conclusion?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a grantor-grantee index?

It is an index used to locate recorded real-property instruments by party name and recording location. Texas Local Government Code §193.003 requires a grantor/grantee cross-index for the covered records.

### Should I search the same name in both roles?

If the official source offers both roles and both are relevant, record each as a separate attempt. Do not merge the results or infer legal capacity from the role label.

### Does no result displayed mean no deed exists?

No. It means only that the source displayed no result for the exact recorded attempt. Spelling, role, date range, coverage, historical indexing, and source availability remain possible limits.

### Does an index hit prove mineral ownership?

No. It is a locator lead. The relevant instrument and surrounding record chain must be reviewed, and qualified title or legal help may be needed for an ownership conclusion.

### Is this the same as ordering a certified copy?

No. This article stops at the index attempt and locator. Copy retrieval, copy status, certification, legal sufficiency, and title effect are separate tasks.
