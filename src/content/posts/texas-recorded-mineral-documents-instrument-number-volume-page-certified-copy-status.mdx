> **Record-control guide, not a title opinion.** This page organizes source identity for an already-found Texas recorded mineral document. It does not decide what instrument transfers an interest, whether a chain is complete, whether a copy is legally sufficient, admissible, or certified, or who currently owns the interest. Any later MRX assessment is educational and directional only, not a certified appraisal. This page is not individualized legal, tax, title, accounting, engineering, or investment advice. MRX may be the disclosed direct buyer in a later transaction; that role is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **keep five fields separate for an already-found recorded mineral document: (1) county, (2) instrument number or county-clerk file number, (3) volume/page or film code when present, (4) online-image or plain-copy status, and (5) county-clerk certification status. A locator tells you how the record is referenced. Certification describes the copy. Neither field alone proves current ownership.**

That separation follows the locator forms in [Texas Property Code Chapter 11](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.11.htm) and the recording and certified-copy explanations on PDF pages 27 and 45 of the [Texas Judicial Branch County Clerk Manual, 2023 edition](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf). The worksheet is a transcription control, not a legal conclusion.

## The five fields in one control row

| Field | Record exactly | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| County | The Texas county named by the official source, clerk response, or record context. | Do not substitute a mailing address, appraisal district, operator address, or nearby county. |
| Instrument or county-clerk file number | The identifier exactly as displayed, including punctuation or leading zeros when those characters are verified. | Do not manufacture an identifier from a date, page count, or search-result order. |
| Volume/page or film code | The exact older-system locator when the record supplies one. Keep volume and page in separate subfields if the worksheet supports that structure. | Do not convert an instrument number into volume/page or assume both systems exist. |
| Working-copy status | Online image, downloaded image, plain copy, or another accurately described working state. Preserve the source URL or receipt separately. | Do not label a file certified merely because it came from a county-facing website. |
| Clerk-certification status | Verified only when the copy itself or a clerk-issued record shows the applicable certification. | Do not treat a border, watermark-like background, scan quality, or filename as proof of certification. |

If a field is not verified, record it as blank or unknown. **Unknown** is better evidence than an invented locator.

## Why the county is the first field

[Texas Property Code §11.001](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.11.htm) says an instrument relating to real property must be recorded in the county in which part of the property is located to be effectively recorded. The [Texas State Library and Archives Commission property-research guide](https://www.tsl.texas.gov/ref/propertyresearch) likewise says real-property records, including deeds, surveys, liens, and tax records, are maintained by the county where the property is located and directs researchers to the County Clerk for help locating records.

For a control row, the practical lesson is narrow: **record the county as source identity before transcribing a locator.** The same number can be meaningless without the county or record system that issued it.

This does not determine which county is legally correct for a disputed tract, whether a document was properly recorded, or whether a county record affects a particular interest. Those questions require the applicable record and, when needed, qualified legal or title review.

## Instrument number, volume/page, and film code are locator forms

[Texas Property Code §11.007](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.11.htm) identifies three forms of reference to a county's real-property records: volume and page number, film code number, or county-clerk file number. The statute addresses their effect when referenced in an instrument. This article uses the list only to identify recognized locator forms; it does not apply the statute to an owner or decide notice.

The [County Clerk Manual](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457469/county-clerk-manual-2023-edition.pdf) explains the recording mechanics on PDF page 27, printed page II-5. When a storage medium is a book, the clerk generally specifies the book's volume and the page or pages where the instrument is recorded. The same page notes that an electronically recorded and stored instrument may instead have one unique instrument number with recording details and the database name.

That source explains why a worksheet should not force every record into the same locator format:

- an older book-based record may present volume and page;
- another record may present a film code;
- an electronic record may present a unique instrument number; and
- a particular source may display more than one reference, in which case each should be transcribed in its own field without deciding which is legally controlling.

The labels a county uses can vary. Preserve the source label when possible instead of silently renaming **instrument number**, **document number**, or **county-clerk file number** as though every county used identical terminology.

## Online image and certified copy are different states

An online image can be useful for research, but **online availability is not the same as county-clerk certification**. Section 11.008(k) requires a specified warning for certain county online databases: the online database does not constitute the official repository of real-property records and may not reflect the complete or unaltered contents maintained in the official records.

That provision is not a claim that every online image is wrong. It is a reason to record the working file's source and status accurately rather than upgrading it by assumption.

PDF page 45, printed page II-23, of the County Clerk Manual describes a certified copy, also called an attested copy. It says the clerk affixes a seal and signature and certifies that the copy is a true and exact replica of the recorded instrument in its entirety. The page also notes that clerks who scan instruments generally reference an instrument number rather than volume/page when certifying the copy.

The control rule is therefore:

1. Record **online image** or **plain copy** when that is what the evidence supports.
2. Record **certified copy** only when the copy or clerk-issued evidence supports that status.
3. Preserve the certification date, receipt, request number, or delivery record in separate fields when available.
4. Do not convert certification into a conclusion about current ownership.

Certification answers a copy-fidelity question. Whether an instrument proves, transfers, reserves, burdens, or affects a current interest is a different question.

## A blank worksheet that preserves uncertainty

Use one row per recorded instrument or clerk-issued copy. A source-preserving row can look like this:

| Control field | Entry rule |
|---|---|
| County | Exact county name from the source; otherwise blank. |
| Record collection | Exact visible collection label, if verified; otherwise blank. |
| Instrument/file number | Exact source value; otherwise blank. |
| Volume | Exact source value; otherwise blank. |
| Page | Exact source value; otherwise blank. |
| Film code | Exact source value; otherwise blank. |
| Working-copy type | Online image, downloaded image, plain copy, or certified copy only when supported. |
| Certification evidence | Clerk certificate, receipt, or verified response reference; otherwise blank. |
| Source URL or custody note | Direct source path or a note identifying the custodian and retrieval route. |
| Retrieved on | The actual retrieval date, not the record's filing date. |
| Open question | What remains unresolved without turning the gap into a conclusion. |

Do not place Social Security numbers, driver's-license numbers, signatures, bank information, or other unnecessary personal data in a shared tracking sheet. The [mineral-rights document redaction checklist](/blog/mineral-rights-document-redaction-checklist-before-sharing-records/) owns the separate process for preserving originals and preparing a limited share copy.

## Locator accuracy is not ownership proof

A complete locator row can show **which record was reviewed** and **which copy state was available**. It cannot, by itself, show:

- whether the instrument is the latest relevant document in the chain;
- whether a later deed, probate order, affidavit, release, assignment, reservation, or court order changes the analysis;
- whether names, legal descriptions, reservations, exceptions, exhibits, or recording references connect correctly;
- whether the record is legally effective, admissible, or sufficient for a particular purpose;
- the current owner, fractional interest, royalty rate, tax treatment, private value, or transaction outcome; or
- whether a clerk, attorney, title examiner, CPA, engineer, or appraiser would require a different copy or additional evidence.

For the separate mechanics of finding and handing off a deed copy, use the [recorded mineral deed copy for CPA handoff guide](/blog/how-to-retrieve-recorded-mineral-deed-copy-for-cpa-handoff/). Article 318 begins after the instrument has been found and owns only the locator-and-copy-status control row.

## Claim, evidence, and limit register

- **C318-01 · COUNTY:** Property Code §11.001 ties recording to the county containing part of the property. This does not decide the proper county for a disputed owner or tract.
- **C318-02 · LOCATOR-FORMS:** Section 11.007 names volume/page, film code, and county-clerk file number as reference forms. This article does not apply the statute's notice effect to a particular person.
- **C318-03 · MINERAL-RECORD EXAMPLE:** Section 11.008(a) includes a mineral lease within that section's recorded real-property instrument definition. It does not classify every mineral-related document for every purpose.
- **C318-04 · BOOK LOCATOR:** County Clerk Manual PDF page 27, printed II-5, explains volume/page location for book storage. It does not say every older record has a complete locator online.
- **C318-05 · ELECTRONIC LOCATOR:** The same page says electronically recorded and stored instruments may have a unique instrument number. It does not establish one statewide number format.
- **C318-06 · ONLINE LIMIT:** Section 11.008(k) says the covered online database is not the official repository and may not reflect complete or unaltered official-record contents. It does not mean every displayed image is inaccurate.
- **C318-07 · CERTIFIED COPY:** County Clerk Manual PDF page 45, printed II-23, describes the clerk's certification of a true and exact replica. Certification status must be verified from the copy or clerk evidence.
- **C318-08 · OWNERSHIP LIMIT:** A locator and certified-copy status identify a record and copy state; they do not by themselves establish current ownership or legal sufficiency.
- **C318-09 · CUSTODIAN ROUTE:** TSLAC directs researchers to the county and County Clerk for record-locating help. It does not replace the county's official record or certify a copy.

## A citation-ready answer

> For an already-found Texas recorded mineral document, record the county, instrument or county-clerk file number, any volume/page or film code, the working-copy type, and clerk-certification status in separate fields. Texas law recognizes multiple record-reference forms, and the County Clerk Manual explains that electronic records may use a unique instrument number while older book records use volume/page. A locator identifies the record; certification describes the copy. Neither alone proves current ownership.

This answer names the source types, preserves the distinction, and states the limit. It does not promise ranking, indexing, retrieval, or citation by a search or answer engine.

## Questions before relying on the control row

1. Which Texas county and record collection does the source itself identify?
2. Is the visible locator an instrument number, county-clerk file number, volume/page, film code, or more than one form?
3. Was each character transcribed from the source rather than reconstructed?
4. Is the working file an online image, plain copy, or clerk-certified copy?
5. What evidence supports the certification status?
6. Are every page, exhibit, attachment, stamp, and marginal notation present?
7. Does the requested conclusion require qualified title or legal review beyond source control?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an instrument number the same as volume and page?

Not necessarily. Texas Property Code §11.007 recognizes volume/page, film code, and county-clerk file number references. The County Clerk Manual explains that electronically stored instruments may use a unique instrument number instead of a book volume and page.

### Is a county website image automatically a certified copy?

No. Record online-image or plain-copy status separately from clerk-certification status. Section 11.008(k) also warns that the covered online database is not the official repository and may not reflect complete or unaltered official-record contents.

### Does a certified copy prove current ownership?

Not by itself. The clerk's certification addresses whether the copy replicates the recorded instrument. Current ownership can depend on the relevant chain and other evidence reviewed by a qualified title professional or attorney.

### What if one locator field is missing?

Leave it blank or mark it unknown. Preserve the verified county, source path, working-copy state, and any other locator exactly as shown. Do not manufacture a second locator.

### Does this worksheet tell me which document is legally sufficient?

No. It records source identity and copy status only. Legal sufficiency, title effect, admissibility, and current ownership are separate professional questions.
