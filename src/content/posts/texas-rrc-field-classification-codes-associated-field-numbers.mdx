> **Field-table interpretation, not property proof.** This page explains selected Texas Railroad Commission field-data labels and row relationships. It does not identify a field boundary, county, district, well, lease, tract, unit, API number, owner, title interest, production result, reserve, private value, offer, buyer action, or transaction outcome. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **read <code>field_class_code</code> in <code>og_field</code> as the field-level G, O, or B classification, and read <code>oil_or_gas_code</code> in <code>og_field_info</code> as the role of each type-specific row**. Expect one <code>og_field_info</code> row when the field is oil-only or gas-only. Expect two rows, one oil and one gas, when the field is classified as both.

Resolve an associated field number only through the exact source field that defines the pointer. Preserve the raw code, table name, field label, <code>field_id</code>, field number, row role, source object, version or access date, pointer status, and exception. A class code or pointer is not evidence that a well, lease, tract, owner, production record, or value belongs to the field.

## The field classification and row model

The [RRC Oil and Gas Field Information data dictionary](https://rrc.texas.gov/media/01wfdxlx/oilgasfieldrules.pdf) describes two different levels of data. The <code>og_field</code> table holds general field information, including <code>field_number</code>, <code>field_name</code>, <code>field_id</code>, and <code>field_class_code</code>. The <code>og_field_info</code> table holds information specific to a field type and includes <code>oil_or_gas_code</code>, <code>field_info_id</code>, and <code>field_id</code>.

That difference is the organizing rule:

| Source location                                       | Code or key                      | Source-scoped meaning                                                            | Safe handling                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <code>og_field.field_class_code</code>                | <code>g</code>                   | Gas field                                                                        | Retain as field-level class; expect one gas-type information row             |
| <code>og_field.field_class_code</code>                | <code>o</code>                   | Oil field                                                                        | Retain as field-level class; expect one oil-type information row             |
| <code>og_field.field_class_code</code>                | <code>b</code>                   | Associated field, both oil and gas                                               | Expect separate oil and gas type-specific rows; do not collapse them         |
| <code>og_field_info.oil_or_gas_code</code>            | <code>g</code> or <code>o</code> | Role of one type-specific information row                                        | Keep the role beside the row and its <code>field_id</code>                   |
| <code>og_field.associated_field_id</code> table label | Numeric column                   | Related-field pointer context documented for differing oil and gas field numbers | Preserve the exact label and resolve only under the reviewed source contract |

The manual explains that <code>og_field_info</code> has one row when a field is either oil or gas and two rows when the field is both, one oil row and one gas row. That is a cardinality rule, not a suggestion to duplicate or merge records. If a field marked both has only one type-specific row in an extract, treat the record set as incomplete or out of scope until the exact export and filter are checked.

## G, O, and B are field-data codes

The dictionary describes <code>g</code> as gas, <code>o</code> as oil, and <code>b</code> as associated or both oil and gas. It also says initial classification depends on the first well's gas-to-oil ratio, while administrative hearings and later discoveries can affect the field relationship. That history is why a code should be carried with its source and date instead of treated as a permanent natural-law label.

For display, this article capitalizes G, O, and B. In a data pipeline, preserve the raw character exactly as delivered. If a source supplies lowercase <code>g</code>, retain lowercase in the raw field. A separate normalized field may use uppercase only if the transformation rule, source, and validation result are recorded.

Do not translate B into a private conclusion such as "both products are produced from this owner's tract." The code describes the RRC field-data relationship in the reviewed source. It does not establish a geographic or ownership connection to a private asset.

## One field can require two type-specific rows

The safest mental model is one parent field record with one or two child information rows:

1. Read the parent <code>og_field</code> row and preserve <code>field_id</code>, field number, field name, and raw class code.
2. Locate <code>og_field_info</code> rows that use that exact <code>field_id</code> within the same controlled source.
3. If class is G, require a gas-role information row.
4. If class is O, require an oil-role information row.
5. If class is B, require both an oil-role row and a gas-role row.
6. Keep each row's <code>oil_or_gas_code</code> and type-specific facts separate.

Do not select the first row returned by a query and discard the second. Do not average, concatenate, or overwrite type-specific values merely because the parent <code>field_id</code> matches. The parent key relates the records; the type code tells you why more than one child row can exist.

The current [RRC Field Data page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/research-and-statistics/field-data/) links the Oil and Gas Field Information Query separately from the Proration Schedule by Field. The [RRC research-query catalog](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/) separately lists the Field Rules Query. A field-data table relationship does not prove that a particular query, schedule, or downstream extract returned the same rows.

## Resolve an associated field number as a pointer

The reviewed manual's narrative says that, for gas fields, the associated-field data item stores an eight-digit oil field number when a gas field is associated with an oil field and the two use different field numbers. The same manual explains that related oil and gas fields usually share a field number; the pointer matters when they do not.

That makes pointer resolution a controlled lookup, not a fuzzy match:

1. Identify the exact source table and field label that supplied the value.
2. Preserve the originating gas field's <code>field_id</code> and field number.
3. Preserve the raw associated-field value as text so all eight positions remain visible.
4. Label the target as a related oil field number only because the reviewed dictionary says so.
5. Search the same controlled field-number domain for an exact target.
6. Record <code>resolved_exact</code>, <code>unresolved</code>, <code>ambiguous</code>, <code>blank</code>, or <code>out_of_scope</code>.
7. Keep the source and target records separate even when the pointer resolves.

A resolved pointer does not authorize a join to a wellbore API number, oil lease number, gas well ID, deed, tax account, royalty statement, or owner record. The [RRC explanation of oil and gas queries](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/research-queries/about-oil-gas-data-queries/) says each query is one slice of the larger system and distinguishes wellbore, completion, API, lease or well ID, field, district, and operator contexts. Each identifier lane needs its own evidence.

## Preserve the manual's label inconsistency

The table definition on the reviewed manual endpoint uses <code>associated_field_id</code>. The narrative data dictionary uses <code>associate_field_id</code>. The difference is one letter, but silent normalization would erase useful provenance.

Use two fields in the audit record:

| Audit field                          | Example role                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <code>source_field_label</code>      | Exact spelling copied from the table definition or narrative                                       |
| <code>canonical_mapping_label</code> | Internal label approved by a documented schema contract                                            |
| <code>source_location</code>         | Document, page, table, or dictionary section                                                       |
| <code>mapping_status</code>          | <code>exact</code>, <code>reviewed_alias</code>, <code>unresolved</code>, or <code>conflict</code> |

Do not infer from spelling alone that the two labels are interchangeable in every RRC delivery. The reviewed document supports recording the inconsistency. A production mapping still needs the exact export schema, data dictionary, and version that govern the file being processed.

## Current endpoint does not erase document age

The Oil and Gas Field Information manual is available from the current RRC download catalog, while its PDF metadata and export description are historical. The article therefore records two separate facts: the endpoint was accessible on August 31, 2026, and the manual describes an older Oracle export.

The current [Data Sets Available for Download page](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/data-sets-available-for-download/) lists Oil and Gas Field Name and Numbers and Oil and Gas Field Rules as separate monthly products. That catalog supports current product identity and cadence. It does not prove that every older column, data type, or relationship is unchanged.

The [legacy Field Information tape-layout manual](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/media/sn4f1nyy/field_information_oga090.pdf) separately documents G, O, and B class values and associated-gas-field pointer records. It is useful corroboration that these concepts have historical continuity. It is not a substitute for a current schema contract, and its tape record layout must not be presented as current Oracle-table parity.

## Build a field-code evidence row

For each reviewed field record, keep enough context to reproduce the interpretation:

| Evidence field                  | Entry rule                                                        | Failure state                 |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| <code>source_object</code>      | Exact file, query export, or controlled document                  | Missing source identity       |
| <code>source_sha256</code>      | Hash the retrieved object when available                          | Bytes changed or unavailable  |
| <code>source_version</code>     | Stated version, export date, or access date                       | Undated source                |
| <code>table_name</code>         | Exact source table                                                | Unknown table                 |
| <code>source_field_label</code> | Exact source spelling                                             | Label drift                   |
| <code>raw_code</code>           | Preserve G, O, B, blank, or other value                           | Invalid or unmapped code      |
| <code>field_id</code>           | Preserve source key without treating it as public property proof  | Missing or duplicate key      |
| <code>field_number</code>       | Preserve exact eight-position value as text                       | Truncated or malformed        |
| <code>row_role</code>           | Parent field, oil information, gas information, or pointer target | Role unresolved               |
| <code>pointer_status</code>     | Exact, unresolved, ambiguous, blank, or out of scope              | Review required               |
| <code>exception_note</code>     | Explain mismatch, missing row, alias, or version drift            | Never leave a silent fallback |

This row design creates information gain because it exposes where a parser can fail. It also keeps a future reviewer from mistaking a normalized result for the original source.

## Reconcile the parent, child rows, and pointer

A deterministic review can use these checks:

1. Fingerprint the source object and attach its exact data dictionary.
2. Count parent <code>og_field</code> rows by raw class code.
3. Count child <code>og_field_info</code> rows by <code>field_id</code> and <code>oil_or_gas_code</code>.
4. Require one expected child role for G and O and two distinct roles for B.
5. Record duplicate, missing, unexpected, and filtered-out rows separately.
6. Resolve nonblank associated-field values only in the documented field-number domain.
7. Count exact, unresolved, ambiguous, blank, and out-of-scope pointers.
8. Compare all classified parent rows to the total reviewed population.
9. Stop when totals, roles, source versions, or target domains do not reconcile.

Useful controls are:

<code>reviewed parents = G parents + O parents + B parents + unmapped parents</code>

and:

<code>reviewed pointers = exact + unresolved + ambiguous + blank + out of scope</code>

These controls validate the parsing workflow. They do not validate a field boundary, property match, ownership chain, production record, reserves, or value.

## What the codes and pointer do not prove

Texas RRC field classification codes do not independently establish:

- a geographic field boundary, county assignment, district mapping, or surface location;
- that a wellbore, API number, completion, lease, gas well ID, tract, unit, or deed belongs to the same private asset;
- mineral ownership, title quality, signing authority, royalty entitlement, or payment responsibility;
- production completeness, product mix for a particular owner, forecast, reserves, decline, or future development;
- tax treatment, appraisal result, mineral value, offer quality, buyer qualification, or sale outcome; or
- that two records describe the same interest because their field names, numbers, codes, or pointers appear related.

For the broader distinction among RRC identifiers, use [Texas RRC District, County, and Field Codes: A Source-Label Crosswalk](/blog/texas-rrc-district-county-field-codes-source-label-crosswalk/). Article 299 owns that non-equivalence boundary. For query inputs, retrieval time, result filters, and downloaded evidence, use the [Texas RRC Field Search Retrieval Provenance Worksheet](/blog/texas-rrc-field-search-retrieval-provenance-worksheet/). Article 301 does not replace either job.
