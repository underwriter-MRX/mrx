> **Format inspection, not an owner or property result.** This article records privacy-safe structural observations from one Upton County Appraisal District archive. It does not identify an owner, account, lease, property, tract, operator, well, acreage position, title interest, appraisal result, tax outcome, production result, mineral value, offer, or transaction result. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **treat the Upton CAD TXT and CSV members as two different source objects, preserve their exact bytes, and parse them independently**. In the bounded inspection used for this guide, the first two physical TXT records each measured **2,260 bytes after their trailing CRLF bytes were removed**. The CSV behaved differently: a standard first pass read its first physical line as one outer field, and a second bounded parse exposed **244 nested labels**.

The first inspected CSV data record also parsed to 244 fields. That is a useful reconciliation point, not proof that every row matches, every byte is valid text, every label is correctly understood, or the data establishes anything about a person or property.

## Bind the publisher, page, archive, and members first

Upton CAD’s [Data and Records page](https://uptoncad.org/home/DataRecords) labels a download as the **2026 Certified Mineral Appraisal Roll (Zip)**. The linked [official ZIP](https://uptoncad.org/Forms/ZipFileDownload?fileName=1784839292_UPTON+CAD+2026+CERTIFIED+MINERAL+OPEN+RECORDS.zip&subPath=Data+Records) returned 79,434,297 bytes and SHA-256 `8d6086b338cb7ffd7c882183842aaf25f697dda513b74d842ed7d95afbcd7423` when retained for this review.

Its central directory listed seven members totaling 946,089,069 uncompressed bytes: an appraisal-roll PDF, layout PDF, flat-text data file, CSV data file, recap PDF, year-by-year appraisal PDF, and master-inventory PDF. This article inspects only the format boundary of the TXT and CSV members. The [Loving CAD archive member map](/blog/loving-cad-2026-certified-mineral-roll-zip-pdf-txt-csv-member-map/) owns the separate question of stored order and complete member identity for a different county archive.

## Measure the TXT records without printing them

The reviewed TXT member is named `3. MINERAL DATA FLAT TEXT FILE.TXT`. A privacy-safe structural check read only enough bytes to identify the first two physical line boundaries. No row contents were printed, copied into this article, or sent to another service.

Each of those first two lines ended in carriage-return plus line-feed bytes. After removing those two terminator bytes, each record measured 2,260 bytes. That observation should be recorded with its exact scope:

- source member hash;
- physical record indexes inspected;
- raw byte count including the line terminator;
- terminator type;
- record-byte count after terminator removal; and
- tool and parser version.

Do not silently measure Unicode characters after decoding and call that a byte length. A character count can differ from a byte count when non-ASCII bytes appear. Do not trim spaces either: trailing spaces may be part of a fixed-position record. Preserve the raw bytes, remove only the explicitly observed CRLF terminator for the reported measurement, and label the result **two-record sample**.

The byte-identical layout PDF found inside this Upton archive was already used by an admitted Loving CAD layout article. Article 303 therefore does not republish or claim ownership of that layout. It owns only the new Upton TXT/CSV structural observations bound to Upton’s archive and member hashes.

## The CSV header requires two bounded parsing steps

The reviewed CSV member is named `4. MINERAL DATA CSV FILE.CSV`. Its first physical line measured 5,233 bytes including CRLF. A byte-level check found a heavily quoted prefix, 245 comma bytes, 978 quote bytes, and a non-UTF-8 byte immediately before the closing quote and CRLF.

A standard CSV parse of that physical line produced **one outer field**. That is the first-pass result and should not be overwritten. A second parse of the inner field, after isolating the terminal replacement marker created during bounded decoding, produced **244 labels**.

The safe workflow is:

1. Hash and retain the raw CSV member.
2. Read only the first physical line as bytes.
3. Preserve its byte count, CRLF terminator, quote count, comma count, and terminal-byte observation.
4. Run a standards-based first CSV parse and record the one-field result.
5. Treat that field as a candidate nested header, not as a proven schema.
6. Isolate the terminal decoding anomaly without rewriting the retained source.
7. Parse the nested value and record the 244-label result.
8. Stop before assigning semantics or ingesting the remaining owner-level rows.

This two-pass result explains why a naive import may display one enormous header cell. It does not prove why the publisher produced the wrapper, which encoding applies to the whole file, or whether every later record follows the same structure.

## Reconcile one data record, then stop

For one bounded cross-check, the first physical data record measured 2,993 bytes including CRLF and parsed to 244 fields. That matches the nested-header label count in this small inspection.

Record the reconciliation as:

`first inspected data record field count = nested header label count = 244`

Do not promote that statement into “the CSV has 244 valid columns.” Full-file parity would require a separately authorized, privacy-safe process that checks every physical record, parser exception, quoting case, null representation, byte anomaly, and field count without leaking row data. Encoding validity would require its own evidence. Schema validity and field semantics would require publisher documentation or confirmation.

## Use a discrepancy register instead of making corrections

When observations do not line up, preserve the difference. A defensible discrepancy row contains:

| Field | Record |
| ----------------- | ------ |
| `source_object` | Complete ZIP and member name |
| `source_sha256` | Exact member hash |
| `physical_scope` | Header, record index, or bounded byte range |
| `raw_observation` | Byte count, line ending, quote count, comma count, or parser result |
| `parser_settings` | Delimiter, quote behavior, encoding mode, error handling, and version |
| `expected_basis` | The separate evidence that created an expectation |
| `difference` | Exact mismatch without a guessed repair |
| `status` | Open, publisher-confirmed, parser-resolved, or excluded from use |

Do not fix a header, discard a byte, rename a field, or pad a record silently. Preserve the original object and make any working copy, transformation, or exclusion independently reproducible.

## Route property questions outside the bulk files

Upton CAD’s [Property Search](https://www.uptoncad.org/Home/Search) offers visible search fields that include property type, lease number, and lease name. That route can help a reader frame a current district question. A search field does not prove that a matching result is correct, current, complete, owned by a particular person, or legally sufficient.

Upton CAD also publishes an [Open Records Request form](https://uptoncad.org/Forms/GetDocument?fileName=1749827956_open+records+request.pdf&subPath=Data+Records). Use the district’s current route when a source definition, corrected file, accessible copy, or record clarification is needed. Preserve the request, response, date, and exact file returned.

The Texas Comptroller’s [Upton County property-tax directory](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/upton.php) separates the local appraisal-district and tax-office contacts. Appraisal questions and tax-collection questions belong in different lanes. Neither office substitutes for a title examiner, attorney, engineer, accountant, or private-market valuation professional.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long are the reviewed Upton CAD mineral TXT records?

The first two physical records in the retained 2026 TXT member each measured 2,260 bytes after removing their trailing CRLF bytes. That is a two-record observation, not proof about every record.

### Why did the reviewed Upton CAD CSV header parse as one field?

The physical header line was itself wrapped as one quoted outer field. A second bounded parse of that field exposed 244 labels after a terminal decoding marker was isolated.

### Does the first data record prove that the CSV has 244 columns throughout?

No. Its 244 parsed fields reconcile with the bounded nested-header count, but one record cannot prove full-file row parity, encoding validity, schema validity, or field semantics.

### Can I publish rows from the Upton CAD mineral data files?

This guide does not. Define a legitimate purpose, minimize the data, protect personal and account information, and obtain official or qualified guidance before handling or republishing row-level records.

### Do these files prove mineral ownership or market value?

No. Appraisal records and parsing observations do not replace title evidence or establish private mineral value, an offer, production, tax treatment, or a transaction outcome.
