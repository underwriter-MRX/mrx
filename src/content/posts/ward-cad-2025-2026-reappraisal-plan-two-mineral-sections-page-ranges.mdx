The direct answer is: **the Ward CAD 2025-2026 reappraisal plan contains two separate mineral-treatment sections, not one continuous section.** The district's mineral valuation-process section appears at whole-PDF pages **26-28**, which show printed district-plan pages **24-26**. A later contractor mineral-interests section appears at whole-PDF pages **64-69**, which show Appendix B printed pages **17-22**.

Appendix B restarts its printed numbering. That is why “page 17” can correctly identify a contractor appendix page even though the same sheet is the sixty-fourth page in the complete PDF. A precise citation should name both the whole-PDF position and the printed page system.

## Page-range crosswalk

Use these locations when checking the source:

- **District mineral section:** whole-PDF pages 26-28; printed district-plan pages 24-26.
- **District boundary check:** whole-PDF page 29 begins the next property topic, confirming that the mineral section ended on PDF page 28.
- **Appendix B identity:** whole-PDF page 45 introduces the contractor's 2025-2026 reappraisal plan as Appendix B.
- **Appendix B table of contents:** whole-PDF page 48 places the mineral-interests section at Appendix B printed page 17.
- **Contractor mineral section:** whole-PDF pages 64-69; Appendix B printed pages 17-22.

These are three distinct identifiers: a viewer's whole-PDF page position, the district plan's printed page number, and Appendix B's restarted printed page number. A search result, citation, or internal note that says only “page 17” is ambiguous unless it also names Appendix B.

## What the district section does

The first section begins on PDF page 26 under the district's mineral valuation-process heading. It is part of the main Ward CAD plan, and its printed footer shows page 24.

Across PDF pages 26-28, the district section summarizes its treatment of mineral appraisal work. The supported themes include:

- which function or contractor is responsible for the work;
- the public and industry data or resources used in the process;
- the kinds of model inputs and property information considered;
- model calibration and review activity; and
- a direction to consult Appendix B for the contractor's separate plan.

This is a process summary. It does not show that a particular owner has a mineral interest, that a tract is producing, that an account is taxable, or that a public appraisal figure equals private transaction value.

PDF page 29 begins the next property category. That page is useful negative evidence: it establishes where the district mineral discussion stops instead of leaving the section boundary to guesswork.

## What the Appendix B section adds

The same 75-page file later includes a separately numbered contractor reappraisal plan. Whole-PDF page 45 identifies that material as Appendix B. Whole-PDF page 48 is the appendix table of contents and points to the mineral-interests section at Appendix B printed page 17.

The contractor section begins at whole-PDF page 64 and runs through page 69. Those sheets show Appendix B printed pages 17-22. Its supported themes include:

- the section's scope and stated assumptions;
- data collection and validation practices;
- calibration of mineral appraisal models;
- individual account or property review activity; and
- performance tests used within the contractor's process.

The contractor text is related to the district summary, but it is not the same page range and should not be collapsed into it. The district section points to the appendix; the appendix then supplies its own longer mineral-interest process description.

## Why the handoff matters

Someone who stops after the district's printed page 26 will miss the later contractor section. Someone who searches only for Appendix B printed page 17 may land on the wrong whole-PDF position if a viewer displays continuous PDF numbering. The crosswalk preserves both paths.

A source-preserving research note should record:

1. the complete-file identity and retrieval date;
2. the whole-PDF page position;
3. the printed page number and the section that owns it;
4. the nearby heading or table-of-contents pointer; and
5. the claim limit for that page.

That method is useful for auditability and future refreshes. It does not elevate a page locator into a professional opinion or an endorsement of the process described.

## What this page crosswalk cannot establish

The reappraisal plan describes processes used for public appraisal work. It is not a mineral deed, lease, division order, title opinion, production statement, engineering report, private market study, purchase offer, tax ruling, or property-specific appraisal report.

The two sections cannot by themselves establish:

- the owner or decimal interest for a tract;
- whether a particular asset is taxable or exempt;
- a tax bill, protest result, or deadline for a reader;
- current production, reserves, decline, or future drilling;
- the private-sale value of mineral rights;
- whether an offer is fair; or

Those questions require their own current, authoritative records and the appropriate qualified professionals. Keep the public-process source in its lane.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the first mineral section in the Ward CAD plan?

It begins on whole-PDF page 26, printed district-plan page 24, and continues through PDF page 28, printed page 26. Whole-PDF page 29 begins the next property topic.

### Where is the second mineral section?

The contractor mineral-interests section begins on whole-PDF page 64, Appendix B printed page 17, and continues through whole-PDF page 69, Appendix B printed page 22.

### Why are there three page-number references?

The viewer has a continuous whole-PDF position, the main district plan prints its own page numbers, and Appendix B restarts printed numbering. Naming the system prevents a reader from opening the wrong page.

### Do the two sections say exactly the same thing?

No. The district section summarizes the district process and points to Appendix B. The contractor section separately describes its mineral-interest scope, data, calibration, review, and performance-testing work.

### Does this plan tell me what my mineral rights are worth?

No. It describes public reappraisal processes and their source locations. It does not establish private value, title, tax liability, production, reserves, or a property-specific appraisal conclusion.
