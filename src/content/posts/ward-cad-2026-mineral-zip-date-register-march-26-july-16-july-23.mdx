## Why the clock times differ

The [PKWARE ZIP APPNOTE page](https://support.pkware.com/pkzip/appnote) identifies APPNOTE as the maintained ZIP format specification. In the exact APPNOTE 6.3.10 text retrieved for this review, section 4.4.6 says the conventional date and time fields use standard MS-DOS format, differ from UTC, and use two-second precision. Section 4.6.1 maps header ID `0x5455` to the extended timestamp field.

The Ward archive's verbose central-directory report shows both fields on every entry. ZipInfo renders the extended timestamp in the current local environment and in UTC. That is why one entry can show three clock readings without representing three separate events.

Member 1 is the clearest example:

- DOS representation: July 16 at `18:52:44`;
- UT displayed locally in `America/Los_Angeles`: July 16 at `16:52:44`;
- UT displayed as UTC: July 16 at `23:52:44`.

The correct citation must name the tool, environment, and representation. Saying only “the file date is 6:52 p.m.” would hide which stored field and rendering produced that time.

The one-second differences on members 3 through 6 are also expected evidence to preserve, not round away. The conventional DOS format has two-second precision. The extended field can retain an odd-numbered second. This explains a format-level precision difference; it does not explain who set a timestamp or why.

## Number order is not date order

Reading the filename prefixes from 1 through 7 produces a convenient inventory. It does not produce the metadata-date order.

If the rows are grouped by the supported calendar date, the sequence is:

1. March 26: member 2;
2. July 16: members 6, 7, 3, 5, and 1 when sorted by the displayed UT value;
3. July 23: member 4.

That reordered list is a sort of archive metadata, not a reconstruction of the publisher's workflow. The sources do not state that the earliest timestamp marks the first created file, that the latest timestamp marks a later correction, or that the numbered prefixes describe processing order.

Several explanations could produce a mixed-date archive: preserving prior file times, exporting components at different moments, copying files between systems, or assembling a container from existing objects. None is selected here because the reviewed sources do not identify the archive creator, the commands used, the source systems, or the intended chronology.

The three date groups support a small set of source-control statements:

Archive metadata should not be promoted into a business fact without another source that defines the relationship.

## Questions to ask before relying on a date

If a timestamp matters to a property, appraisal, tax, title, or transaction question, ask for evidence that defines the date's role:

## Frequently asked questions

### What calendar dates appear in the Ward CAD 2026 mineral ZIP?

ZipInfo 3.00 groups member 2 on March 26, members 1, 3, 5, 6, and 7 on July 16, and member 4 on July 23, 2026. Those dates are archive-entry metadata.

### Are members 1 through 7 arranged by date?

No. Member 2 has the earliest calendar date, five members have July 16 dates, and member 4 has the latest calendar date. The numeric prefixes are inventory labels, not proven chronology.

### Why does one member show more than one clock time?

Each entry has conventional DOS date/time fields and a `0x5455` extended timestamp field. ZipInfo 3.00 can display the extended value in the local `America/Los_Angeles` environment and as UTC. Quote the representation with the time.

### Do the timestamps prove when Ward CAD published or certified each file?

No. The reviewed sources do not define the fields as publication, certification, creation, update, effective, filing, delivery, upload, approval, or data-as-of dates.
