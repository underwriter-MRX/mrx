**Wyoming W.S. 30-5-301(b) distinguishes two small-payment conditions.** For someone legally entitled to Wyoming production proceeds, subsection (b)(i) provides for **annual remittance of up to twelve months' accumulated proceeds when the total owed is $100 or less**. Subsection (b)(ii) separately says that, on a payee's written request, payment is to be remitted within 60 days after receipt if accumulated proceeds are **at least $25**. Neither figure, alone, proves that your payor has recorded a balance or that a particular check is late. [Wyoming Legislature, current Title 30](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124).

## The $100 and $25 conditions do different jobs

The **$100-or-less** language concerns the _total amount owed_ under the statute's annual-aggregation provision. It does not authorize an indefinite hold of a small balance. The **$25-or-more** language is a separate condition tied to a **written request by the payee** and 60 days following its receipt. It does not say an accumulated $25 automatically generates an immediate check or that an informal inquiry is a statutory request. The section also specifies separate payment boundaries after production ceases or the party responsible for payment changes. [W.S. 30-5-301(b)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124).

Keep the statute's subject in view: [W.S. 30-5-301(a)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124) addresses proceeds from Wyoming oil-and-gas production owed to people legally entitled to them. It sets default first and subsequent payment periods but recognizes other periods or arrangements in a valid contract with the entitled person. The responsible payor can depend on legal arrangements; subsection (c) addresses a purchaser paying owners under an arrangement between the operator and purchaser. A general article cannot identify your governing contract, entitlement, recorded balance or responsible payor.

## Keep the relevant payor and period records together

Retain the prior check or statement, property or well identifier, sales months, name of the payor shown, any notice naming a changed payor and communications about a held amount. Ask the **verified payor** whether it has recorded an amount for the identified interest and sales period, how it tracks accumulated proceeds, which payment arrangement it believes applies and where it has sent recent statements or checks. Its answer is evidence of its records, not independent proof of title, decimal or amount owed. Do not post full owner, tax or banking identifiers in public search or chat.

If you are considering a written request under subsection (b)(ii), its wording and application to your facts matter. This article is **not** a request template or an instruction to send one. A qualified Wyoming attorney can evaluate a material dispute, especially when entitlement, contract terms, the amount accumulated or payor identity is contested.

[W.S. 30-5-302](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=125) separately addresses circumstances in which payment cannot be made and an escrow process. That separate rule does not establish that any reader's proceeds were escrowed. Neither determines whether this interest has accumulated a payment under subsection (b).

### Does Wyoming let a payor hold a $100-or-less amount indefinitely?

No. [W.S. 30-5-301(b)(i)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124) addresses annual remittance for up to twelve months of accumulated proceeds when the total owed is $100 or less. Its application requires the owner-specific entitlement, payor, period and governing records.

### Does an accumulated $25 automatically require an immediate check?

No. [W.S. 30-5-301(b)(ii)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124) refers to a payee's written request and remittance within 60 days after receipt if accumulated proceeds are at least $25. This article is not a request template and does not determine whether one was received in any individual case.

### What if production stopped or the payor changed?

Subsections [(b)(iii) and (iv)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title30.pdf#page=124) address separate twelve-month payment boundaries after cessation of production and a party no longer responsible for payment. Preserve the relevant dates and determine the responsible party with qualified review rather than assuming either provision applies.

### What should I preserve before asking about a missing check?

Keep the prior statement or check, property or well ID, sales period, named payor and related correspondence. Ask the verified payor about its recorded amount and period without putting private owner or bank details into public search or chat.

### Do the $100 and $25 figures prove an amount is owed to me?

No. They are conditions in a statutory timing provision, not proof of title, legal entitlement, an accumulated balance, a contract term or a late-payment violation. A material owner-specific dispute calls for qualified Wyoming legal review.

**Bottom line:** Wyoming's current text distinguishes annual remittance for $100-or-less accumulated proceeds from a conditional written-request path at $25 or more. Preserve the specific property, period, payor and communications before interpreting a missing check.
