If you are collecting Ohio mineral records for a title review, start with the recorder for the county where the property is located. Ohio law directs specified real-property instruments to that county and expressly lists deeds, leases and lease memoranda, plus specified notices and affidavits, among the recorder's official-record categories. Those are **different document classes to request and compare**, not a statewide mineral-ownership database. Use that county's current search and copy route, and send the actual instruments, not just name-index hits, to a qualified reviewer. A recorder result does not establish your mineral fraction or whether a lease remains in force. [Ohio Revised Code §§5301.25(A)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5301.25), [317.08(A)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-317.08).

## Find the right county office first

Use the property's county, not the owner's mailing address or a well operator's location. Ohio Revised Code [§5301.25(A)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5301.25) supplies the county-of-premises recording rule for the instruments it covers. The [Ohio Recorders' Association](https://www.ohiorecorders.com/aws/ORA/pt/sp/home_page) connects the public to the state's 88 county recorder offices. Select the county office, then use **its** current official search or copy instructions. The association is a directory, not a single statewide index of deeds and leases.

For a later reviewer, capture each retrieved instrument's county, type, parties, recording date, instrument or book-and-page number, property description, and source or copy-request reference. Preserve the full document image where available. Keep private royalty statements, signatures, account numbers, and personal addresses out of public search queries or questions. The [document-redaction checklist](/blog/mineral-rights-document-redaction-checklist-before-sharing-records/) helps prepare a controlled handoff; it does not change the public-record search.

## Which instrument categories should you look for?

Ohio Revised Code [§317.08(A)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-317.08) lists record classes, not a decision that any particular document controls your minerals. Depending on your file, ask about:

| Potential record | Why it belongs in the collection file | What its presence alone cannot decide |
|---|---|---|
| Deed or other conveyance | Shows the language and parties in a recorded transfer that may bear on the identified property. | Whether later reservations, transfers, inheritance, or another instrument changed the interest. |
| Oil-and-gas lease, lease memorandum, supplement, modification, or amendment | Preserves the recorded text or reference a reviewer can compare with the owner's own lease documents. | Whether a lease currently covers the owner's tract or remains effective. |
| Relevant recorded notice, affidavit, or order | Flags a document that may need separate legal examination in the chain. | Whether a statutory process was satisfied or a mineral interest vested in anyone. |

The first two classes are identified in §317.08(A)(1) and (25); the notice/affidavit/order categories appear in §317.08(A)(2), (5), and (11). The latter provisions refer to specific statutory instruments; they are **not** a general instruction to file a notice or affidavit. Their application and any deadlines require the actual documents and qualified Ohio counsel.

## If the online search looks empty

Do not turn “no online result” into “no mineral deed” or “no lease.” Counties run their own record systems and have different digital coverage. As one limited example, the [Erie County Recorder FAQ](https://www.eriecounty.oh.gov/RecorderFAQs.aspx) says its online index and images begin in 1990 and older material is being added. It explains how to request a copy that is not available online. That date and procedure belong to **Erie County**, not every Ohio recorder.

For an **Erie County** example, its [current recorder FAQ](https://www.eriecounty.oh.gov/RecorderFAQs.aspx) links to the county-designated document search. It instructs users to enter a party name in the “Both Names” field as last name then first name, or to use another listed category, then view the document image from a result. Record the exact input, result identifier, image availability, and search date; a name match alone does not identify the controlling document for a tract. The FAQ gives a copy-request route for unavailable images and says recorder staff do not perform a full history or title search. This is **Erie's** workflow, not a statewide search recipe.

Another county can use a different index: the [Ashtabula County Recorder](https://www.ashtabulacounty.us/Recorder) links its own land-record search, describes deeds and leases among its holdings, and distinguishes maintaining records from title examination. If you expected a document, use **your county's** recorder contact or copy-request path with the names, approximate date, property description, and any known locator. Ask how that office handles older or non-online material. Preserve the unanswered search as an open item, not a conclusion that no document exists.

## Hand off a reviewable file, not a title conclusion

For a qualified Ohio mineral-title reviewer, send a dated list of the county and property identifiers, the full recorded documents and locators, any lease papers the family or payor supplied, and the questions you could not reconcile. Keep competing versions side by side. Ask the reviewer to determine what the instruments mean for the particular tract, minerals, depths, parties, and time period. A recorder index, an isolated deed, a lease memorandum, and a royalty payment each have different evidence roles. None alone computes an ownership fraction, decides a dormant-mineral issue, guarantees lease status, or establishes a fair sale price.

If you also have a written purchase offer, use the [offer-review page](/offer-review/) to keep the buyer's property-and-rights assumptions next to the recorded-source questions. If you are still researching, the [title, lease, and ownership pillar](/learning-center/title-lease-ownership/) is the better next step; no contact or booking is required merely to collect the public records.

### What if I only have an old owner's name?

Use the name as a starting search term in the **relevant county** and retain spelling, middle-name, entity-name, and date variants as hypotheses. A matching name is not proof that a recorded instrument concerns your tract or that the named person held the interest you are researching. Compare the full property description and document trail with a qualified reviewer.

### Does a recorded Ohio lease prove I am still being paid correctly?

No. A recorded lease or memorandum gives a document to examine, but it is not a private payor ledger. Current payment questions require the owner's statements and payor explanation, the actual lease and any amendments, and qualified review of the interest and applicable terms. The [royalties pillar](/learning-center/oil-and-gas-royalties/) covers the statement-reading path without treating the recorder index as a payment audit.

### Can a county recorder tell me who owns the minerals?

The office maintains and makes recorded documents accessible. It does not issue a tract-specific mineral-title opinion. [Ashtabula County](https://www.ashtabulacounty.us/Recorder) explicitly says its recorder is not authorized to perform title examinations, and [Erie County](https://www.eriecounty.oh.gov/RecorderFAQs.aspx) says its office does not conduct complete history/title searches. Ask the recorder for search and copy instructions; ask a qualified professional to analyze disputed ownership or legal effect.

### Is there one statewide Ohio recorder index for all mineral deeds and leases?

The [Ohio Recorders' Association](https://www.ohiorecorders.com/aws/ORA/pt/sp/home_page) connects the public with the 88 county recorder offices. It is an office directory, not a single statewide deed-and-lease search. Use the current official search or copy route for the county where the property is located.

### What if the online index does not show an older document?

Keep the exact county search and result as an unresolved lead, then ask that county recorder about older indexes or copy requests. [Erie](https://www.eriecounty.oh.gov/RecorderFAQs.aspx) reports online indexing and images since 1990 with older material being added, but that date is Erie-specific. An empty online result is not proof that no instrument exists.
