> **Study-set routing, not a mineral-property finding.** This page explains where Reeves appears in two official Texas Comptroller program lists. It does not identify an owner, parcel, mineral account, title, acreage, lease, well, production interest, appraisal notice, tax bill, protest result, market value, private mineral-interest value, offer, buyer action, or transaction result. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **the official 2025 appraisal-district ratio-study findings list does not include `195 Reeves`, so that publication set has no Reeves district worksheet**. Reeves appears instead in the official 2025 Methods and Assistance Program list. Texas Comptroller guidance explains that appraisal districts move through a two-year review-and-study cycle rather than every district receiving both programs every year.

For the Reeves appraisal-district ratio-study worksheet in this controlled source set, use the official 2024 findings route. The 2025 omission is a study-set fact. It is not evidence that a mineral record is missing or that any property value changed.

## Direct answer: the 2025 findings list has no 195 Reeves row

The [Texas Comptroller 2025 Appraisal District Ratio Study findings page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/data/property-tax/ratio-study/2025/) identifies itself as `2025 Tax Year Findings` and says the information was published on January 30, 2026 in compliance with Tax Code Section 5.10. Under `Appraisal Districts Studied`, it presents the districts included in that publication set.

`195 Reeves` does not appear in that list. The controlled page capture contains no Reeves link, no 195 district entry, and therefore no route to a 2025 Reeves appraisal-district summary worksheet.

That is the full negative claim. It does not say the website lost a file, the district failed to submit something, a mineral account is absent, or a property record changed. It says only that Reeves is not one of the appraisal districts studied in the published 2025 ADRS list.

## Reeves appears in the separate 2025 MAP list

The [Texas Comptroller 2025 MAP Review Results page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/map/2025/) says the listed appraisal-district MAP reviews were completed in 2025. Reeves appears in that list.

MAP and ADRS are different programs. The MAP page routes to a review report. The ADRS findings page routes to district ratio-study worksheets. A Reeves entry on the MAP page should not be copied into the ADRS list, and the MAP report should not be described as a ratio-study worksheet.

This page uses the Reeves MAP listing only as cycle context. For the internal findings and recommendations in that review, use the dedicated Reeves MAP article. Article 296 does not reproduce or reinterpret those results.

## The statewide cycle explains the route

The [Texas Comptroller Property Tax System Basics page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/basics.php) says PTAD performs MAP reviews of all appraisal districts every two years, reviews approximately half of the districts each year, and subjects school districts in counties not receiving a MAP review that year to an SDPVS and ADRS.

The [Property Tax Data Reports and Surveys page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/reports/) states the ADRS cadence another way: Tax Code Section 5.10 requires the Comptroller to conduct a ratio study measuring each appraisal district at least once every two years and publish the results. It also says the published results cover each appraisal district studied that year.

Read together, those official descriptions support a narrow routing conclusion:

1. the 2025 MAP list includes Reeves;
2. the 2025 ADRS findings list does not include Reeves; and
3. the ADRS requirement is at least once every two years, not a Reeves worksheet in every annual publication set.

This is not a claim about how Reeves CAD performed. It is not a trend analysis. It does not establish why any individual record was selected, excluded, appraised, or taxed.

## Use the 2024 findings route for the Reeves worksheet

The [Texas Comptroller 2024 Appraisal District Ratio Study findings page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/data/property-tax/ratio-study/2024/) identifies the prior tax-year publication set and includes `195 Reeves` in its studied-district list. That entry links to the Reeves appraisal-district summary worksheet.

That article owns the row labels and figures. Article 296 owns only the year-to-program routing question and does not repeat those fields.

## What the missing 2025 route does not prove

No Reeves entry in the 2025 findings list does not prove any of the following:

- that a Reeves mineral account or appraisal record is missing;
- that Reeves CAD omitted a property from its local roll;
- that a named property increased or decreased in value;
- that an appraisal is correct or incorrect;
- that a tax amount or protest outcome changed;
- that a private mineral interest has a particular value;
- that an offer, buyer, sale, or transaction outcome is supported; or
- that 2024 and 2025 study figures should be combined or trended.

Those conclusions require different records and different authority. A statewide publication list cannot establish property ownership, title, acreage, decimal interest, production, burdens, appraisal correctness, tax liability, private market value, or a transaction result.

## A repeatable missing-record check

When a year-specific district worksheet cannot be found, preserve the search in this order:

1. Record the exact program and tax year being searched.
2. Open the official annual findings landing page.
3. Confirm the page labels its district links as the districts studied.
4. Search for the district number and district name separately.
5. Record whether a link is present without inventing a reason for absence.
6. Check the official MAP year list for cycle context.
7. Use the statewide basics and reports pages for the program cadence.
8. Locate the closest official findings year that actually contains the district route.
9. Keep any worksheet fields in the separate article that owns that record.
10. Stop before making an owner, property, appraisal, tax, value, offer, or transaction inference.

This sequence creates an auditable answer to a retrieval question. It does not transform absence into a substantive property finding.
