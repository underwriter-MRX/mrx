> **Published source map, not an account-specific date determination.** This page reports what an official Winkler CAD contractor plan says about its January 1 appraisal snapshot and later information. It does not calculate a deadline, interpret appraisal standards or the Texas Property Tax Code, decide what evidence applies to an account, review a protest, establish title or taxability, determine private mineral-rights value, or provide a transaction recommendation. Any later MRX assessment is educational and directional only, not a certified appraisal. This page is not individualized legal, tax, title, accounting, engineering, or investment advice. MRX may be the disclosed direct buyer in a later transaction; that role is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **the Winkler CAD 2025-2026 contractor plan describes January 1 as the Texas ad-valorem lien date and appraisal effective date, even though an appraisal or report may be dated several months later. The plan says later information applies only when it confirms a trend or appraisal condition that existed and was generally known as of the effective date.** A later document date does not automatically move the snapshot forward.

That statement appears on [whole-PDF page 20, printed page 18 of the 2025-2026 Biennial Reappraisal Plan](https://www.winklercad.org/Forms/GetDocument?fileName=1764618216_Biennial-Reappraisal-Plan.PA_.MIUP_.2025-2026.pdf&subPath=Data+Records). The document was printed in July 2024. The 2025-2026 label is the plan period, not the print date, the effective date, or a current private-market update.

## The date-role map

| Date or time reference | What the cited passage says | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| January 1 | The plan describes it as the Texas lien date and stated effective date for ad-valorem purposes. | An owner-specific legal conclusion, protest deadline, or private transaction date. |
| Appraisal date | The plan says appraisals are typically dated several months after the effective date. | That the later appraisal date automatically replaces January 1. |
| Report date | The plan says reports are also typically dated several months after the effective date. | That the report measures every fact known on its preparation date. |
| Later information | The plan limits applicability to information confirming a trend or appraisal condition that existed and was generally known as of the effective date. | That any later event, price, filing, or corrected record can be back-applied. |

The source uses a narrow test. It does not say that later information is always excluded, always included, or independently controls the snapshot.

## Why the snapshot and preparation date are different

The effective date answers **when the appraisal is anchored**. The appraisal or report date answers **when the work or written communication is dated**. The source says those dates can be separated by several months.

That distinction prevents a common reading error. A report created later may describe a conclusion as of an earlier effective date. The cited passage does not say the report is automatically current through its preparation date. It also does not say that every piece of information available on that later date belongs in the effective-date analysis.

For a broader evidence-control framework, the [Texas mineral-record date roles guide](/blog/tax-year-production-month-and-download-date-texas-mineral-records/) separates tax year, production month, and retrieval date. That page owns cross-source date fields. This page owns only the Winkler contractor plan's January 1 and later-information statement.

## The later-information boundary

The passage conditions later information on two linked ideas:

1. the information confirms a trend or other appraisal condition that existed as of the effective date; and
2. that trend or condition was generally known as of the effective date.

The source does not provide a checklist of qualifying records in this paragraph. It does not say how a particular account, price, production correction, operator statement, appraisal adjustment, or later event should be classified. Those are stop conditions for source-specific review, not blanks to fill with assumptions.

Use the source wording as a boundary, not as permission to select only later facts that support a preferred result.

## What annual reappraisal adds, and what it does not

The same printed page says all mineral properties, meaning mineral interests in the plan's terminology, are reappraised annually. That supplies context for a recurring appraisal snapshot.

It does not mean every account changes, every input is new, every later fact applies to the prior snapshot, or a public tax value equals a private sale estimate. The page also contains a separate discussion about the interest being appraised rather than oil or gas in place. That separate subject is outside Article 315 and is not expanded here.

## A four-field evidence worksheet

Keep four dates or time references separate when reading a mineral appraisal record:

| Field | Record | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Effective date | Exact date and source passage anchoring the conclusion | Stop if the record does not identify the date the conclusion addresses. |
| Appraisal or report date | Date shown for the work product or report | Do not substitute it for the effective date without source support. |
| Later-information date | Date and identity of a later record offered as confirmation | Stop if the claimed effective-date trend or condition is not identified. |
| Retrieval date | Date, time, URL, and file identity captured by the researcher | Do not call access time the source effective date. |
