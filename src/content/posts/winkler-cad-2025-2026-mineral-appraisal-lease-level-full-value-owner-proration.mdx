The [Winkler CAD 2025-2026 contractor plan](https://www.winklercad.org/Forms/GetDocument?fileName=1764618216_Biennial-Reappraisal-Plan.PA_.MIUP_.2025-2026.pdf&subPath=Data+Records) describes a qualified three-stage sequence. Mineral interests applying to one producing lease are **typically** consolidated by type, each type is appraised for full value, and that value is distributed pro rata to fractional decimal-interest owners according to their type and percentage. The paragraph explains a mass-appraisal process. It does not calculate an owner's decimal or prove an owner-specific value.

## The qualifier comes first

The source says **typically**, not always. It describes Pritchard & Abbott's stated scope and method in a July 2024 contractor plan covering tax years 2025 and 2026. It does not establish how every Winkler CAD account was handled, whether a later plan changed the procedure, or which records control a particular owner's interest.

That boundary matters because three different levels appear in one paragraph:

| Level in the stated sequence | What the source says | What the source does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Producing lease | Mineral interests applying to a single producing lease are typically considered together for the next step. | That every public lease identifier matches a reader's tract, account, or payment property. |
| Interest type | The interests are typically consolidated by type; working and royalty are the examples in the paragraph. Each type is appraised for full value. | Which legal category applies to an owner, or that every possible interest type is listed. |
| Fractional owner | Full value is then distributed pro rata according to individual type and percentage amount. | The correct decimal, title chain, account value, tax result, or private transaction value for a specific owner. |

## Stage one: the lease and interest-type grouping

The first paragraph on printed page 17 describes P&A's mineral valuation department as responsible for developing values for full or fractional percentage ownership of oil-and-gas leasehold interests associated with producing or capable-of-producing leases. It adds that the amount and type of those interests are legally or contractually created and specified through deeds, leases, and similar instruments.

The paragraph then narrows to interests that apply to one producing lease. It says those interests are typically consolidated by type. Working and royalty are the two examples in parentheses.

This sentence does not allow a reader to reverse-engineer title from an appraisal label. It does not say which deed, lease, assignment, probate record, division order, or other instrument controls a particular interest. It also does not say that a public lease-level production record is the same thing as an owner's payment property or tract.

For the surrounding source categories, the [Winkler CAD mineral-data chain](/blog/winkler-cad-2025-2026-mineral-data-chain-rrc-files-operator-inputs-taxpayer-records/) maps the plan's RRC, operator, and taxpayer inputs. That page owns the data-source question. This page owns only the sequence stated in the first paragraph of printed page 17.

## Stage two: full value at the type level

After the type grouping, the source says each type is appraised for full value. Within the grammar of the paragraph, **full value** belongs to the consolidated interest type before the next owner-level distribution step.

It should not be relabeled as any of the following without separate evidence:

- one owner's appraised value;
- the value of all oil or gas in place;
- a tax bill or taxable amount;
- a current private-market value;
- a sale price or offer; or
- an MRX purchase proposal.

The paragraph provides no dollars, production volumes, reserve quantities, price assumptions, expenses, discount rate, tax rate, or account record. It cannot support a numerical example that appears to calculate a real owner's result.

## Stage three: owner proration

The passage says the full value is then distributed to fractional decimal-interest owners **pro rata** according to each owner's interest type and percentage amount. In plain language, the stated process moves from a type-level whole to owner-level shares.

That is a directional process description, not a decimal calculator. The source does not provide the numerator, denominator, net acres, tract size, royalty fraction, unit participation, allocation factor, burden, decimal, or ownership instrument needed to reproduce a particular account. It does not resolve whether an owner record is correct.

The safest reading is therefore sequential:

1. identify the producing-lease context described by the contractor;
2. preserve the source's interest-type grouping and its **typically** qualifier;
3. distinguish the type-level full-value step from the owner-level distribution step; and
4. stop before inferring a reader's type, percentage, decimal, or value.
