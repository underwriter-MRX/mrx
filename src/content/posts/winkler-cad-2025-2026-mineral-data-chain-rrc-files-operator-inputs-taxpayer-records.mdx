> **Published process description, not a property-specific conclusion.** This page reports source categories and workflow statements from an official Winkler CAD contractor plan. It does not establish an owner's identity, title, taxability, tax liability, private mineral-rights value, current production, reserves, model validity, an appraisal result, a protest result, or a transaction recommendation. Any later MRX assessment is directional only and is not an appraisal performed by a state-licensed or state-certified real estate appraiser. This page is not individualized legal, tax, title, accounting, engineering, or investment advice. MRX may be the disclosed buyer in a later transaction; that role is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: **the Winkler CAD contractor plan says its main mineral-property data source is Railroad Commission of Texas data as reported by operators, with updated and new well and production files received monthly.** It also describes appraiser fieldwork, operator financial data, information from chief appraisers and tax assessors, trade publications, local newspapers, and facts supplied by members of the public.

For ownership information, the plan separately names pipeline purchasers or other entities that disburse income, including operators, and says those requests are made under a promise of confidentiality concerning owners' personal information. It also names taxpayer-filed deeds of ownership transfer and direct correspondence from taxpayers.

These are source categories stated in a July 2024 plan for the 2025-2026 plan period. They do not prove that a particular person owns an interest, that a particular record is complete, or that a public appraisal input produces a private mineral-rights value.

## The main source and monthly cadence

Whole-PDF page 23, printed page 21, states that the main source of property data is Railroad Commission of Texas data as reported by operators. The plan then says its data-processing department receives updated and new well and production data in tapes or electronic files as a monthly activity.

That wording supports a narrow four-part statement:

- the named public agency is the Railroad Commission of Texas;
- the data are described as operator-reported;
- the files contain updated and new well and production data; and
- the stated receipt cadence is monthly.

The plan does not identify a specific file schema, query interface, retrieval timestamp, lease, well, operator, or account on that page. It also does not say that every operator report is complete, error-free, final, or sufficient for another purpose. Article 313 therefore reports the category and cadence without turning the statement into a record-level quality guarantee.

## Supplemental discovery inputs

The plan lists other discovery tools after the main RRC/operator source. They are separate categories with different evidentiary roles:

- **Appraiser fieldwork:** the plan names direct field activity as a discovery tool. It does not disclose a visit log or prove inspection of a specific property.
- **Financial data from operators:** the plan names operator financial information. It does not publish a property-level operating statement on the cited page.
- **Chief appraisers and tax assessors:** the plan names information from local appraisal and tax officials. It does not assign every fact to a particular office.
- **Trade publications:** the plan names industry publications as discovery inputs. It does not identify an article, issue, or fact used for a particular account.
- **City and local newspapers:** the plan names local news sources. A news item would still require its own date, publisher, claim, and verification.
- **Members of the public:** the plan says members of the public often provide information about new wells and other useful facts. A supplied fact is an input, not automatic verification.

Putting these categories on one list does not make them interchangeable. A public-agency regulatory record, an operator submission, a field observation, a local-government communication, a publication, and a public tip can differ in authority, granularity, timing, confidentiality, and error risk.

## Ownership-information sources and the privacy boundary

The same page describes another source lane for ownership information. It says mineral leases are commonly fractionated among several or many owners, then identifies two pathways.

First, the plan describes requesting information from pipeline purchasers or other entities, such as operators, that disburse income to mineral-interest owners. It expressly pairs that request with a promise of confidentiality concerning owners' personal information.

Second, the plan identifies taxpayers who file deeds of ownership transfer or correspond with the contractor or appraisal district directly.

The safe conclusion is limited: **the plan describes those entities and taxpayers as ownership-information inputs within its process.** This article does not claim that the inputs are public, that MRX has access to them, that MRX reviewed them, or that a reader can retrieve them through the public Data and Records page.

It also does not elevate an appraisal-system input into a title conclusion. A taxpayer submission, deed copy, operator record, purchaser record, pay-deck record, division order, and recorded instrument can serve different purposes. Ownership and title questions require the controlling recorded documents, relevant chain of title, and qualified professional review.

## Statewide downloads and individual record enrichment

The plan distinguishes two collection scales. It says production and price data for the entire state are downloaded into the computer system together. It then says appraisers individually gather and record specific information in appraisal-file records.

That distinction matters:

1. **Statewide collection** supplies broad production and price inputs across the company's work.
2. **Property-specific collection** adds particular information to an appraisal file.

The plan does not publish the complete field list, transformation logic, error-resolution rules, or contents of any specific appraisal file on the cited page. It therefore cannot support a claim that an individual file contains every record needed for title, tax, production, reserve, or private-value analysis.

The related [CAD account, RRC lease ID, and operator number crosswalk](/blog/cad-account-number-rrc-lease-id-operator-number-texas-mineral-records/) explains why identifiers from different record systems should not be treated as interchangeable. An identifier can help join evidence without proving that two systems define the same property, owner, or legal interest.

## What the source chain cannot prove

The presence of a source category in a public plan cannot by itself establish:

- the identity or decimal interest of an owner;
- whether a deed, lease, or transfer is valid or controlling;
- whether a particular account is taxable or correctly classified;
- the completeness or accuracy of an operator or public record;
- current production, reserves, decline, expenses, or future development;
- a public appraisal result or likely protest result;
- the private-sale value of mineral rights;
- whether an offer is fair; or
- a legal, tax, title, accounting, engineering, or investment recommendation.

Each conclusion needs its own evidence, effective date, purpose, and qualified reviewer. The public plan is useful for understanding a described workflow, not for filling gaps in a property-specific evidence package.

## Questions to ask before relying on a source input

1. Which exact source supplied the fact: public agency file, operator submission, fieldwork, local official, publication, public tip, disbursing entity, or taxpayer record?
2. What retrieval, effective, reporting, or filing date belongs to that source?
3. Is the information public, commercially licensed, confidential, or property-owner supplied?
4. Which identifier connects the record to a lease, well, account, tract, or interest?
5. Does the intended conclusion require title, tax, engineering, appraisal, or other qualified professional review?
