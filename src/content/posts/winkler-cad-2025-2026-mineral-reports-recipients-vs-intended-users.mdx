> **Published source map, not an assignment-specific determination.** This page reports what an official Winkler CAD contractor plan says about report recipients and intended users. It does not decide who is an intended user for a particular assignment, interpret professional appraisal standards, validate a report, establish title or taxability, determine private mineral-rights value, or provide a transaction recommendation. Any later MRX assessment is directional only and is not an appraisal performed by a state-licensed or state-certified real estate appraiser. The direct answer is: **the Winkler CAD contractor plan says intended users are typically the clients for which Pritchard & Abbott is under direct contract. It also says that receiving a report does not automatically make a taxpayer, agent, disclosure recipient, or open-records recipient an intended user.** The appraiser's assignment-specific identification still matters.

That distinction appears on [whole-PDF page 19, printed page 17 of the 2025-2026 Biennial Reappraisal Plan](https://www.winklercad.org/Forms/GetDocument?fileName=1764618216_Biennial-Reappraisal-Plan.PA_.MIUP_.2025-2026.pdf&subPath=Data+Records). The document was printed in July 2024. The 2025-2026 label is the plan period, not the print date.

|---|---|---|
| Direct-contract client | Intended users are typically the clients for which Pritchard & Abbott is under direct contract. | That every client is an intended user for every report. |

The table preserves the verbs used by the source. **Typically**, **often receive**, **does not automatically**, **unless specifically identified**, and **potential** do different work. Collapsing them into one label would remove the main information on the page.

## Why receiving a copy is not the same classification

The page separates delivery from assignment identity. A taxpayer might receive a report because a rule requires delivery or because a client or appraiser provides it as a courtesy. A private party might obtain a copy through an open-records request. Those are delivery paths.

The intended-user statement is narrower. The page ties the typical category to direct-contract clients and says a disclosure recipient becomes an intended user only when the appraiser specifically identifies that party as one. This article reports that distinction. It does not interpret the standard, decide whether a report satisfies it, or infer rights or obligations from the label.

For general questions about formal valuation work, the [mineral-rights valuation near you guide](/blog/understanding-mineral-rights-valuation-near-you/) explains why intended use, required credentials, effective date, workfile, and named users should be checked before work begins. That broader guide does not replace the exact Winkler page record here.

## Potential other users are a separate source category

The page identifies three groups as potential other users:

1. people involved in adjudicating valuation disputes, with examples such as review board members, lawyers, and judges;
2. governmental agencies that periodically review appraisals for statutory purposes, with the Texas Comptroller's Office as an example; and
3. private parties who may obtain copies through open-records requests to governmental agencies.

The word **potential** prevents an automatic conclusion. The page does not say that every review board member, lawyer, judge, agency employee, or open-records requester is an intended user of every report. An assignment-specific report and its identification language would be needed before making a narrower statement.

## The service-scope exception on the same page

The plan adds a separate scope boundary. It says this professional-standards report section from Pritchard & Abbott does not apply to mineral or mineral-interest property that an appraisal district appraises outside Pritchard & Abbott services. In that situation, the page directs readers to the appraisal district overall appraisal-standard report.

That statement changes the first source to inspect. It does not tell a reader which organization appraised a specific account, validate a report, or resolve a disagreement. Preserve the property reference and locate the applicable district or contractor report before relying on this section.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who are the typical intended users named in the Winkler CAD contractor plan?

The plan says intended users are typically the clients for which Pritchard & Abbott is under direct contract. That is a source-attributed plan statement, not a determination for a particular report or assignment.

### Does receiving a mineral report make a taxpayer an intended user?

Not automatically. The plan says taxpayers or their agents may receive reports by law or courtesy, but receipt alone does not make them intended users as defined in the plan.

### Does a disclosure recipient become an intended user?

The plan says a party receiving a copy to satisfy disclosure requirements does not become an intended user unless the appraiser specifically identifies the party as one. This article does not decide whether that occurred in any particular assignment.

### What does the plan say about people who obtain reports through open-records requests?

It lists private parties who obtain copies through open-records requests among potential other users. The page does not establish intended-user status for every requester.

### Does this article interpret professional appraisal standards or a specific mineral appraisal report?

No. It maps the wording and page location in one official plan. A qualified professional should address assignment-specific standards, compliance, or legal consequences.
