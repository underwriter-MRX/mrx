Pecos CAD Oil and Gas Property Discovery: RRCID, Permit, and January 1

The Pecos plan describes an RRCID-to-permit discovery sequence and three January 1 tests, but those statements do not establish a current property result.

By MRX Editorial Team · August 31, 2026 · 10 min read · Reviewed Aug 31, 2026

Direct answer for mineral owners

Pecos CAD’s plan says mineral properties are identified using an RRCID, while production before that identifier may be reported by drilling permit number. It then lists three January 1 tests and a records sequence for location, situs, and interests. These are plan statements, not proof about a current property.

Key takeaways for mineral owners

  • The controlled plan PDF is 971,223 bytes with SHA-256 c95d84641a0d024c04b9d8ba491e5972085b29a05a9aba4038128d78e8e12d01.
  • Physical page 42 preserves the plan’s RRCID and drilling permit number wording without defining those labels as equivalent.
  • The plan prints three separate January 1 conditions: reported production, completion shown by a report, or perforation into a producing formation showing oil or gas.
  • Completion reports and plats support the plan’s location-to-situs sequence; DOI statements have a separate interest role.
  • Later reviews and up-to-five-year supplementation are process statements, not proof that a named property was added or changed.

Official-plan process locator, not a current property finding. This article records what physical pages 42-43 of Pecos County Appraisal District’s 2025-2026 Reappraisal Plan say about oil-and-gas property discovery. It does not establish a named well’s status, whether production occurred before a date, whether a completion report was filed, situs, ownership, title, an interest decimal, an appraisal, a tax amount, private value, an offer, or a transaction result. MRX may have an economic interest in a later mineral transaction. When that applies, the buyer relationship is disclosed in writing before an agreement is signed.

The direct answer is: the Pecos plan describes a sequence, not a current-property result. Physical page 42 says mineral properties are identified and appraised based on an RRCID. It says production may be reported by Drilling Permit Number (DP) when a well starts producing before an RRCID has been issued. The same page then prints three conditions tied to January 1.

Keep those labels, steps, and source statements separate. Do not silently replace RRCID with API number, lease ID, gas ID, or drilling permit number. Do not apply a plan condition to a particular property without the current official records for that exact question.

Bind the exact source before using the procedure

The controlled PDF retrieved for this review is 971,223 bytes with SHA-256 c95d84641a0d024c04b9d8ba491e5972085b29a05a9aba4038128d78e8e12d01. It has 75 physical pages, is unencrypted, and contains no embedded JavaScript. Its metadata records creation on August 8, 2024 and modification on September 2, 2024.

Physical page 42 begins the relevant section under the printed heading Property Discover and Data Collection Process. The source uses “Discover,” not “Discovery.” This article preserves that heading as an observed publisher string while using ordinary grammar in the article title.

The file controls make the citation reproducible. They do not prove the plan was implemented, that its terminology matches every current RRC field, or that a condition applies to one property.

Preserve the identifier terminology instead of normalizing it

Physical page 42 uses Railroad Commission Identification Number (RRCID) and Drilling Permit Number (DP). It does not state on that page that RRCID is an API number, oil lease number, gas ID, or drilling permit number.

The Texas RRC Well Records - Online page currently describes searches and record packets using labels such as API number, oil lease number, gas ID number, W-1 drilling permit, G-1 gas completion packet, and W-2 oil completion packet. The RRC downloadable-data page separately says production on a pending lease may use API or drilling permit identifying information before an official lease ID is assigned.

Those RRC pages corroborate that multiple identifiers and record types exist. They do not supply a one-line definition proving which current field the Pecos plan means by RRCID. The controlled rule is therefore:

  • quote RRCID when reporting the Pecos plan;
  • quote the RRC page’s own API, lease, gas ID, permit, and form labels when reporting that page; and
  • leave the mapping unresolved unless an official source defines the exact relationship for the exact context.

That unresolved terminology boundary prevents a plausible but unsupported identifier substitution.

The three January 1 conditions are separate

Physical page 42 says a well is considered to have value as of January 1 if one of three conditions is present:

  1. it reported production before January 1;
  2. it filed a completion report showing completion before January 1; or
  3. it was perforated into a producing formation that showed the presence of oil or gas before January 1.

These are three source statements joined by “or.” Do not collapse them into one “production date” test. Do not convert them into a legal conclusion, a current appraisal rule, or proof that a named well had value. This article does not inspect a live production record, completion packet, perforation record, appraisal account, or tax roll.

The distinction matters because the plan itself describes different evidence paths. A completion-report date is not silently substituted for a production date, and a perforation statement is not silently treated as either one.

Location, situs, and interests occupy different steps

The next two procedure items on physical page 42 assign distinct roles:

  • Completion reports and plats: the plan says they are retrieved from the RRC to identify producing-well locations.
  • Jurisdictional maps: the plan says locations are cross-referenced with these maps to establish situs.
  • Division of Interest statements: the plan says these are requested from the operator to establish working and royalty interests.

A process sequence is not a result. A completion report or plat does not by itself prove current appraisal situs. A DOI statement is not a title opinion, recorded conveyance, current division order, pay-deck confirmation, or final ownership determination. The plan’s assigned roles should not be merged.

For statewide instructions on retrieving a permit query, use the separate Texas RRC Drilling-Permit Query Retrieval Provenance Worksheet. That page owns the query-retrieval job. This Pecos page owns only the appraisal plan’s discovery sequence.

Physical page 43 adds later review and supplementation

Procedure item 4 continues onto physical page 43. The plan says additional RRC database reviews occur during the year to identify wells that may have entered the database after the first of the year but were completed before January 1 of the appraisal year. It then says new producing wells identified after the appraisal period are supplemented, going back up to five years.

Preserve all qualifiers:

  • the statement is a plan procedure;
  • “may have been added” describes a search rationale;
  • the completion timing remains before January 1 of the appraisal year;
  • “going back up to five years” modifies the printed supplementation statement; and
  • no named well, account, supplement date, appraisal change, notice, or result appears in this article.

Physical page 43 also says other subject-property data may come from regulatory reports, the Texas Comptroller, and the property owner; some submitted data may require confidentiality. It says data are verified through existing records and published reports and notes that no standard data-collection form or manual exists because of the unique nature of many oil-and-gas properties. Those are source statements, not permission to disclose confidential data or infer that a particular confidential record exists.

A bounded owner verification worksheet

If the plan sequence matters to a current property question, keep a source log with separate rows:

  1. Plan route and bytes: record the official URL, retrieval time, byte size, SHA-256, page count, encryption status, and JavaScript status.
  2. Exact plan term: record RRCID without expanding it unless the controlling source does so.
  3. Current RRC identifiers: record the exact API, lease, gas ID, drilling permit, or form label displayed by the current RRC source.
  4. January 1 condition: record which of the three plan statements is being investigated; do not substitute another condition.
  5. Location evidence: keep the completion report and plat distinct from the jurisdictional map and situs conclusion.
  6. Interest evidence: keep a DOI statement distinct from recorded title, a division order, and current payment records.
  7. Later review evidence: record whether a later search or supplement is actually documented; do not infer one from the plan.
  8. Current account evidence: retrieve the applicable Pecos CAD account, notice, or roll record for any present appraisal or tax question.
  9. Transaction evidence: use separate property, title, lease, production, risk, market, buyer, and agreement evidence for a private value or offer question.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the Pecos CAD plan describe oil and gas property discovery?

Physical pages 42 and 43 contain the section titled Property Discover and Data Collection Process and its four-step procedure.

Does the plan say an RRCID and a drilling permit number are the same?

No. It uses the labels for different points in its description. This article preserves that wording and does not infer one-to-one equivalence.

What are the three January 1 conditions printed on physical page 42?

The plan lists reported production before January 1, a completion report showing completion before January 1, or perforation into a producing formation that showed oil or gas before January 1.

Do completion reports, plats, and DOI statements prove current ownership?

No. The plan assigns them process roles for location, situs work, and working or royalty interests. Current ownership and title require separate evidence.

Does the up-to-five-year statement prove a property was supplemented?

No. Physical page 43 describes a supplementation process. It does not identify a particular property, action, date, or result.
